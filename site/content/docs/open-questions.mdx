## 1. How many applications per day go through the full eight-agent path?

<Callout type="error" title="Single most important open question in the engagement">
Without this one number, the cost comparison against the customer's reported **$700K–$1M**
Snowflake AI spend is **unresolvable**. With it, the comparison is decided in a sentence.
</Callout>

Measured cost is **$0.1440 per adjudication** (p50, n=41 priced runs,
`evals/results/pipeline_14x3_us-east-1.json`). Annualising it requires a volume:

| Applications/day through the full 8-agent forensic path | Annualised at measured p50 |
|---|---|
| ~3,000 | **~$158K** |
| ~300,000 | **~$15.8M** |

Arithmetic: volume x 365 x $0.14395847. The per-adjudication p50 is measured; the two volumes are
illustrative brackets chosen to show the range, **not** the customer's figures. At the low end
the port sits comfortably below the incumbent spend; at the high end it is an order of magnitude
above it.

**Exact ask.** Not "how many applications do you process" — consortium volume is much larger than
forensic-underwriting volume, and mixing them is how this question gets answered wrongly. Ask:
*of the applications you ingest, how many per day receive the full eight-agent forensic
underwriting treatment, as opposed to a score-only or single-product path?*

Two secondary figures worth asking for at the same time, because they change the arithmetic
rather than just the total:

- What fraction of that volume is re-run (repeat submissions, retro backfills)?
- Is the eight-agent path applied to every application, or only above a score threshold?

## 2. Which artifact defines the authoritative enums?

Her own two artifacts disagree, and a third vocabulary sits between them. Recorded in
`agents/contracts.py:ENUM_CONFLICT`.

| | `docs/master_agent_prompt.txt` — **implemented** | Process-guide PDF §5.1–5.2 — **not implemented** | The eight specialist prompts |
|---|---|---|---|
| Mid band | **`MEDIUM RISK`** | **`POSSIBLE RISK`** | **`POSSIBLE RISK`** |
| Recommendation values | 3: `APPROVE`, `REVIEW AND APPLY STIPULATIONS`, `DECLINE` | 4: `APPROVE`, `APPROVE WITH STIPULATIONS`, **`MANUAL REVIEW`**, `DECLINE` | n/a — specialists classify, they do not recommend |
| `top_risk_factors`, `*_flag` columns | Required | **Absent** | n/a |
| Status | The string the production agent runs today, so it wins here | Narrative documentation | Their `CALIBRATION` blocks |
| **Verdict** | **Implemented.** Executable artifact beats narrative | Exposed via `PDF_TO_PROMPT_ENUM_MAP`, never silently adopted | Recorded: the specialists agree with the **PDF**, not the master prompt |

So a band rename happens between the analysis layer and the adjudication layer — by design or by
drift, and only the customer knows which.

**What is mechanically mappable and what is not.** `PDF_TO_PROMPT_ENUM_MAP` handles
`POSSIBLE RISK` → `MEDIUM RISK` and `APPROVE WITH STIPULATIONS` → `REVIEW AND APPLY
STIPULATIONS`. **`MANUAL REVIEW` is deliberately absent** and `map_pdf_enum` raises
`EnumConflictError` for it, because it is not a rename of any prompt value — it is a fourth
outcome that does not exist in the executable artifact. Inventing a mapping there would be a
business decision disguised as a default.

Blast radius, quoted from `ENUM_CONFLICT`: *"the enum strings are persisted values in
`UNDERWRITING_REPORT_OUTPUT` and are read by downstream workflow routing."* Anything keying off
the band string — a queue router, a dashboard, an audit trail — is wrong for one of the two
artifacts. A real DDL question, not a documentation tidy-up.

**Exact ask.** *Which of the two is canonical for `UNDERWRITING_REPORT_OUTPUT`, and if the PDF
is, what does `MANUAL REVIEW` map to in the routing that consumes it? Separately: is the
specialist-to-master band rename intentional?*

One related item recorded but not enforced: PDF section 5.3 lists a controlled stipulation
vocabulary (proof of income, employment verification, proof of residence, driver's licence
validation, and so on). It is captured in `contracts.STIPULATION_VOCABULARY` and **not**
enforced, because the executable prompt places no constraint on that string. If downstream
routing keys off those exact phrases, that is a third thing to settle.

## 3. Calibration failures no model choice fixes [#3-the-calibration-failures-no-model-choice-fixes]

Two fixtures are mis-banded by **all five models across both families**, and every one is wrong
the *same way*. That is the signature of a shared prompt defect rather than five independent
mistakes, and a bigger model does not buy the fix.

Source: `evals/results/set_comparison.json` (`known_failure_reproduction`) and
`evals/results/tier_quality.json`.

| Fixture / agent | Pinned | Every model said | Requirement |
|---|---|---|---|
| **APP-1014 / rings** | `LOW RISK` | `POSSIBLE RISK` — Claude Opus 5, Claude Sonnet 5, GPT-5.6 Luna, Terra, Sol. **0 of 5 correct.** | `RNG-020` |
| **APP-1004 / bustout** | `POSSIBLE RISK` | `HIGH RISK` — same five models. **0 of 5 correct.** | `BST-020` |

Reproduction is recorded explicitly in the artifact: *"GPT reproduced it 3/3; Claude 2/2. Correct
band: GPT 0/3, Claude 0/2."*

**The fault the judges named in every case is the same:** a second alert firing off the *same
underlying behaviour*, treated as independent corroboration.

Sharpest form of the finding, and worth checking against her text rather than taking on trust:
both `bustout_agent_prompt.txt` and `rings_agent_prompt.txt` already carry a sentence making
exactly this point — that alert volume alone does not necessarily indicate material fraud risk,
because several alerts may share one underlying behaviour or one weak heuristic. Her wording is
not reproduced here; see the file.

So the principle **is** stated, in the exact terms of the failure, in both of the prompts that
fail — and five models across two families still treat co-originating alerts as independent
corroboration. Which means the fix is not "state the principle"; it is to state it as an
operational counting rule rather than as guidance. Which sentence achieves that is the thing
worth drafting with her.

Six of the eight prompts additionally state that LOW should be the most common outcome. Neither
bustout nor rings contains that sentence — worth noting, since over-escalation is exactly what
both failures are. The Claude-only tier study reached the same conclusion at three tiers; the
cross-family study upgrades it from *"no tier fixes it"* to **"no model choice fixes it."**

**Exact ask.** *May we draft a sentence for the bustout and rings prompts stating that alerts
arising from one underlying behaviour count as one signal, for your review?* We can draft it; she
owns the artifact, and modifying a verbatim customer prompt is not our call.

**NOT MEASURED:** whether adding that sentence fixes either band. It requires a live run against
modified prompt text, which is her artifact. It is the recommendation, not a result.

## 4. Artifacts never received [#4-the-artifacts-we-never-received]

Three documents would settle several of these questions at once, and none of them arrived.

| Artifact | Why it matters | Current state |
|---|---|---|
| **`full_underwriting_signal_dictionary.xlsx`** | The authoritative signal contract | Appears only as an **embedded thumbnail on page 3** of the process-guide PDF, with **zero extractable attachments**. The 60-signal registry in `signal_layer/registry.py` is **reconstructed from the nine prompts.** |
| **`CLEAN_MAIN_STREAM_FILE.sql`** | The output DDL and the real column set | Never received |
| **`PP_Forensic_{Hi,M,L}...pdf`** (page 10 attachments) | Almost certainly gold-standard example outputs per risk band — i.e. the actual acceptance criteria | Never received |

**What "reconstructed" means for the signal registry.** It derives from the SIGNAL INTERPRETATION
sections of the nine prompts. That is a defensible reconstruction — every threshold in it is the
customer's verbatim sentence, read out of `prompts/verbatim/` at import rather than retyped — but
it is **not** the same as having her contract. Two specific consequences:

- Signals she uses that no prompt names would be **missing**, and we cannot know how many.
- Near-duplicate spellings across prompts are both materialised rather than aliased
  (`employer_other_ssns_count` vs `employer_distinct_other_ssns_count`, plus two other pairs),
  because collapsing them would silently degrade one agent. Her dictionary would tell us whether
  they are genuinely two signals or one signal spelled two ways.

A discrepancy in our own documents, noted so nobody trips on it: `CLAUDE.md` says *"~75-signal
registry"* while the module contains **60** entries in `SIGNAL_OWNER` (55 single-owner plus 5
shared). The module is authoritative; the `CLAUDE.md` line is stale.

**Exact ask.** *Can you send `full_underwriting_signal_dictionary.xlsx`,
`CLEAN_MAIN_STREAM_FILE.sql`, and the three `PP_Forensic_*` example outputs?* Between them they
settle the signal set, the output DDL, every enum conflict, and the acceptance criteria — four
open items for one email.

One more platform-side question in the same family: **is her Cortex Analyst backed by a semantic
VIEW or by a stage-based semantic model YAML?** Snowflake's managed MCP server supports views
only, so this single answer gates the whole "keep the semantic layer in place" option. See
[Semantic layer](/docs/platform/semantic-layer).

## 5. Two prompts carry no character cap [#5-two-of-her-prompts-carry-no-character-cap]

Six of the eight specialist prompts cap output at 4,000 characters. `bustout_agent_prompt.txt`
and `rings_agent_prompt.txt` close with a one-line instruction to return only the analysis — no
length rule, no title, no emoji ban, no alert-number ban. Their two closing sentences differ from
each other by one word, which is why `agents/output_contract.py` derives each agent's rules from
its own prompt text instead of matching one string across all eight.

Measured consequence, from `evals/results/tail_analysis.json` over n=334 calls:

| | Domains | n calls | Output tokens p50 | Analyses over 4,000 chars |
|---|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Prompt caps at 4,000 chars | identity, dealer, straw, employment, income, synthetic | 251 | **381** | 9 (3.6%) |
| **No cap in prompt** | **bustout, rings** | 83 | **1,331** | **25 (30.1%)** |

3.5x the output tokens, and since wall clock is linear in output tokens at r²=0.9531, that is the
structural owner of the fan-out floor. Those same two agents are also the **only** two ever
measured to truncate, and the source of all
[24 invented titles](/docs/fidelity/output-contract-defects).

**Exact ask.** *Should the "max 4000 characters" sentence be added to the bustout and rings
prompts?* A one-line change to two verbatim customer artifacts — her decision, not ours.

**NOT MEASURED:** whether adding the sentence actually shortens the output, because this work did
not modify her verbatim prompts. It is the recommended fix and it is unproven.

<Callout type="warn" title="Do not reach for max_tokens instead">
That obvious engineering substitute was measured and it **failed all three criteria**: no latency
gain, 14 of 30 cells truncated, and only 13 of 30 kept the same band. A truncated call is the
worst outcome available — full wall clock, fully billed, text discarded, fraud dimension lost.
[The measurement](/docs/cost/what-does-not-work).
</Callout>
