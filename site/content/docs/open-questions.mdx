## 1. How many applications per day go through the full eight-agent path?

<Callout type="error" title="This is the single most important open question in the engagement">
Without this one number, the cost comparison against the customer's reported **$700K–$1M**
Snowflake AI spend is **unresolvable**. With it, the comparison is decided in a sentence.
</Callout>

Our measured cost is **$0.1440 per adjudication** (p50, n=41 priced runs,
`evals/results/pipeline_14x3_us-east-1.json`). Annualising it requires a volume:

| Applications/day through the full 8-agent forensic path | Annualised at measured p50 |
|---|---|
| ~3,000 | **~$158K** |
| ~300,000 | **~$15.8M** |

Arithmetic: volume x 365 x $0.14395847. The per-adjudication p50 is measured; the two volumes
are illustrative brackets chosen to show the range, **not** the customer's figures.

Same architecture, opposite conclusion. At the low end the port is comfortably below the
incumbent spend; at the high end it is an order of magnitude above it.

**The exact ask.** Not "how many applications do you process" — the consortium volume is much
larger than the forensic-underwriting volume, and mixing them is how this question gets
answered wrongly. Ask: *of the applications you ingest, how many per day receive the full
eight-agent forensic underwriting treatment, as opposed to a score-only or single-product
path?*

Two secondary figures worth asking for at the same time, because they change the arithmetic
rather than just the total:

- What fraction of that volume is re-run (repeat submissions, retro backfills)?
- Is the eight-agent path applied to every application, or only to those over a score
  threshold?

Full detail, including what the $700K–$1M does and does not cover:
[the Snowflake comparison](/docs/cost/snowflake-comparison).

in `agents/contracts.py:ENUM_CONFLICT`.

**`docs/master_agent_prompt.txt`** — the executable artifact. This is the string the
production agent runs today, so it wins in this port.

- `overall_risk_decision` ∈ `LOW RISK` | **`MEDIUM RISK`** | `HIGH RISK`
- `recommendation_decision` ∈ `APPROVE` | `REVIEW AND APPLY STIPULATIONS` | `DECLINE`
  — **three** values

</Tab>

<Tab value="Not implemented (the PDF)">

**`docs/Agent_Underwriting_Process_Guide (1).pdf`** sections 5.1 and 5.2 — narrative

- `overall_risk_decision` ∈ `LOW RISK` | **`POSSIBLE RISK`** | `HIGH RISK`
- `recommendation_decision` ∈ `APPROVE` | `APPROVE WITH STIPULATIONS` | **`MANUAL REVIEW`** |
  `DECLINE` — **four** values

The PDF also shows **no** `top_risk_factors` column and no `*_flag` columns, which the prompt
file requires.

</Tab>

<Tab value="The third vocabulary">

**The eight `<domain>_agent_prompt.txt` CALIBRATION blocks** — the specialists classify in
`LOW RISK` | **`POSSIBLE RISK`** | `HIGH RISK`.

So the specialists agree with the **PDF**, not with the master prompt. A band rename happens
between the analysis layer and the adjudication layer — by design or by drift, and only the
customer knows which.

</Tab>

</Tabs>

**What is mechanically mappable and what is not.** `PDF_TO_PROMPT_ENUM_MAP` handles
`POSSIBLE RISK` → `MEDIUM RISK` and `APPROVE WITH STIPULATIONS` → `REVIEW AND APPLY
STIPULATIONS`. **`MANUAL REVIEW` is deliberately absent** and `map_pdf_enum` raises
`EnumConflictError` for it, because it is not a rename of any prompt value — it is a fourth
outcome that does not exist in the executable artifact. Inventing a mapping there would be a
business decision disguised as a default.

<Callout type="warn" title="Blast radius">
Quoted from `ENUM_CONFLICT`: *"the enum strings are persisted values in
`UNDERWRITING_REPORT_OUTPUT` and are read by downstream workflow routing."* Anything keying
off the band string — a queue router, a dashboard, an audit trail — is wrong for one of the
two artifacts. This is a real DDL question, not a documentation tidy-up.
</Callout>

**The exact ask.** *Which of the two is canonical for `UNDERWRITING_REPORT_OUTPUT`, and if the
PDF is, what does `MANUAL REVIEW` map to in the routing that consumes it? Separately: is the
specialist-to-master band rename intentional?*

One related item recorded but not enforced: PDF section 5.3 lists a controlled stipulation
vocabulary (proof of income, employment verification, proof of residence, driver's licence
validation, …). It is captured in `contracts.STIPULATION_VOCABULARY` and **not** enforced,
because the executable prompt places no constraint on that string. If downstream routing keys
off those exact phrases, that is a third thing to settle.

## 3. The calibration failures no model choice fixes

<Callout type="error" title="Raise this before Friday, not after">
Two fixtures are mis-banded by **all five models across both families** — and every one of
them is wrong the *same way*. That is the signature of a shared prompt defect, not five
independent mistakes, and buying a bigger model does not buy the fix.
</Callout>

Source: `evals/results/set_comparison.json` (`known_failure_reproduction`) and
`evals/results/tier_quality.json`.

| Fixture / agent | Pinned | Every model said | Requirement |
|---|---|---|---|
| **APP-1014 / rings** | `LOW RISK` | `POSSIBLE RISK` — Claude Opus 5, Claude Sonnet 5, GPT-5.6 Luna, Terra, Sol. **0 of 5 correct.** | `RNG-020` |
| **APP-1004 / bustout** | `POSSIBLE RISK` | `HIGH RISK` — same five models. **0 of 5 correct.** | `BST-020` |

The artifact records the reproduction explicitly: *"GPT reproduced it 3/3; Claude 2/2. Correct
band: GPT 0/3, Claude 0/2."*

**The fault the judges named in every case is the same:** a second alert firing off the *same
underlying behaviour* treated as independent corroboration.

<Callout type="warn" title="And the prompts already say not to do that">
This is the sharpest form of the finding, and it is worth checking against her text rather
than taking on trust. Both `bustout_agent_prompt.txt` and `rings_agent_prompt.txt` already
carry a sentence making exactly this point — that alert volume alone does not necessarily
indicate material fraud risk, because several alerts may share one underlying behaviour or
one weak heuristic. (Her wording, not reproduced here; see the file.)

So the principle **is** stated, in the exact terms of the failure, in both of the prompts that
fail — and five models across two families still treat co-originating alerts as independent
corroboration. That means the fix is not "state the principle"; it is to state it as an
operational counting rule rather than as guidance. Which sentence achieves that is the thing
worth drafting with her.

Six of the eight prompts additionally state *"LOW should be the most common outcome."* Neither
bustout nor rings contains that sentence — worth noting, since over-escalation is exactly what
both failures are.
</Callout>

The Claude-only tier study reached the same conclusion at three tiers; the cross-family study
upgrades it from *"no tier fixes it"* to **"no model choice fixes it."**

**The exact ask.** *May we draft a sentence for the bustout and rings prompts stating that
alerts arising from one underlying behaviour count as one signal, for your review?* We can
draft it; she owns the artifact, and modifying a verbatim customer prompt is not our call.

<Callout type="warn" title="NOT MEASURED">
Whether adding that sentence fixes either band is **unmeasured**. It requires a live run
against modified prompt text, which is the customer's artifact. It is the recommendation, not
a result.
</Callout>

<Callout type="warn" title="What 'reconstructed' means for the signal registry">
The registry is derived from the SIGNAL INTERPRETATION sections of the nine prompts. That is a

- Signals she uses that no prompt names would be **missing**, and we cannot know how many.
- Near-duplicate spellings across prompts are both materialised rather than aliased
  (`employer_other_ssns_count` vs `employer_distinct_other_ssns_count`, and two other pairs),
  because collapsing them would silently degrade one agent. Her dictionary would tell us
  whether they are genuinely two signals or one signal spelled two ways.

Note also a discrepancy in our own documents: `CLAUDE.md` says *"~75-signal registry"* while
the module contains **60** entries in `SIGNAL_OWNER` (55 single-owner plus 5 shared). The
module is authoritative; the `CLAUDE.md` line is stale.
</Callout>

**The exact ask.** *Can you send `full_underwriting_signal_dictionary.xlsx`,
`CLEAN_MAIN_STREAM_FILE.sql`, and the three `PP_Forensic_*` example outputs?* Between them they
would settle the signal set, the output DDL, every enum conflict, and the acceptance criteria —
four open items for one email.

One more platform-side question in the same family: **is the customer's Cortex Analyst backed

## 5. Two of her prompts carry no character cap

Six of the eight specialist prompts state *"max 4000 characters"*. `bustout_agent_prompt.txt`
says only *"Return only the final analysis."* and `rings_agent_prompt.txt` only *"Return only
final analysis."* — no length rule, no title, no emoji ban, no alert-number ban. (The two
closing sentences differ from each other by one word, which is why
`agents/output_contract.py` derives each agent's rules from its own prompt text instead of
matching one string across all eight.)

The measured consequence, from `evals/results/tail_analysis.json` over n=334 calls:

| | Domains | n calls | Output tokens p50 | Analyses over 4,000 chars |
|---|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Prompt caps at 4,000 chars | identity, dealer, straw, employment, income, synthetic | 251 | **381** | 9 (3.6%) |
| **No cap in prompt** | **bustout, rings** | 83 | **1,331** | **25 (30.1%)** |

3.5x the output tokens, and since wall clock is linear in output tokens at r²=0.9531, that is
the structural owner of the fan-out floor. Those same two agents are also the **only** two
ever measured to truncate, and the source of all
[24 invented titles](/docs/fidelity/output-contract-defects).

**The exact ask.** *Should the "max 4000 characters" sentence be added to the bustout and rings
prompts?* It is a one-line change to two verbatim customer artifacts — her decision, not ours.

<Callout type="warn" title="NOT MEASURED">
Whether adding the sentence actually shortens the output is **unmeasured**, because this work
did not modify the customer's verbatim prompts. It is the recommended fix and it is unproven.
</Callout>

<Callout type="error" title="And do not reach for max_tokens instead">
The obvious engineering substitute was measured and it **failed all three criteria**: no
latency gain, 14 of 30 cells truncated, and only 13 of 30 kept the same band. A truncated call
is the worst outcome available — full wall clock, fully billed, text discarded, fraud dimension
lost. [The measurement](/docs/cost/what-does-not-work).
</Callout>
