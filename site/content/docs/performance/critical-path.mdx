## The structural fact

A fan-out cannot return before its slowest member. So end-to-end latency is:

```
end_to_end  =  slowest specialist
            +  orchestration above the floor
            +  synthesis + validation
```

Measured, n=42:

| Layer | p50 | p95 | max | Median share of e2e |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| **Fan-out floor** (slowest agent's own wall clock) | **24.03 s** | 41.04 s | 69.78 s | — |
| Orchestration above the floor | **0.83 s** | 1.87 s | 2.71 s | — |
| Fan-out total | 24.93 s | 41.05 s | 70.62 s | **85.6%** |
| Synthesis + validation | 4.20 s | 5.59 s | 15.21 s | 14.4% |

| Share | Median |
|---|---:|
| Floor as a share of fan-out | **96.3%** |

Quoting the artifact's reading: *"Fan-out cannot return before its slowest member, so the floor
distribution IS the fan-out latency distribution up to ~1s of gathering. Any fix that does not
lower the slowest agent lowers nothing."*

<Callout type="warn" title="There is nothing to win in the orchestration layer">
0.83 s at the median, and **never more than 2.71 s** across 42 runs. A perfect orchestration
layer — zero scheduling cost, zero gathering cost — buys under a second at the median and under
three at the worst observed case.

That is worth stating explicitly because "optimise the orchestration" is the first instinct on a
multi-agent latency problem, and here it is measurably the wrong place to look. The 0.83 s covers
`asyncio.gather` scheduling, the admission semaphore, and result collection for eight concurrent
Bedrock streams.
</Callout>

## Who owns the critical path

The slowest specialist in each of the 42 runs:

| Specialist | Model in this run | Runs where it was slowest | Its own p50 | Its own max |
|---|---|---:|---:|---:|
| **`rings`** | Opus 5 | **25** (59.5%) | **20.96 s** | 36.17 s |
| `bustout` | Sonnet 5 | 7 | 14.46 s | **69.78 s** |
| `income` | Sonnet 5 | 5 | 8.09 s | **66.01 s** |
| `identity` | Sonnet 5 | 4 | 12.56 s | 41.04 s |
| `synthetic` | Opus 5 | 1 | 8.52 s | 26.70 s |
| `dealer` | Haiku 4.5 | 0 | 6.56 s | 18.85 s |
| `straw` | Haiku 4.5 | 0 | 4.40 s | 12.52 s |
| `employment` | Sonnet 5 | 0 | 7.48 s | 20.00 s |

Three agents **never** led. `dealer`, `straw` and `employment` are structurally irrelevant to
latency: `employment`'s p95 of 17.64 s is below `rings`'s p50 of 20.96 s.

### rings leads because its floor is high, not because it spikes

The distinguishing statistic is `rings`'s **minimum**: 14.47 s. That is above `dealer`'s p95
(14.56 s is marginally higher, but `dealer`'s p50 is 6.56 s) and above `straw`'s **max** of
12.52 s. There is no cheap run of rings.

`rings`'s max, 36.17 s, is meanwhile the **lowest max** of the five agents that ever led. It is
the most consistently slow agent and the least explosive one.

<Callout type="error" title="The agent that sets the median and the agent that produces the tail are different agents">
Split the 42 runs by speed:

| Subset | Critical-path owners |
|---|---|
| The **10 slowest** runs | bustout 3, identity 3, income 2, rings 2 |
| The **10 fastest** runs | **rings 7**, bustout 3 |

`rings` appears in the fastest ten more than three times as often as in the slowest ten. The
artifact records `same_owner_for_median_and_tail: false`.

Practical consequence: **fixing rings moves the p50 and does nothing for the p95 or the max.**
Both over-target runs were owned by `bustout` (69.78 s) and `income` (66.01 s), and those were
[server-side stalls, not slow agents](/docs/performance/the-tail). Two separate problems that a
single "make the slow agent faster" framing merges into one.
</Callout>

## The ceiling on any single-agent fix — measured

Slowest minus second-slowest, per run. This is the most any single-agent improvement can remove
from fan-out before another agent becomes the floor:

| n | min | p50 | p95 | max | mean |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 42 | **0.014 s** | **6.34 s** | 20.56 s | 50.13 s | 8.53 s |

Definition, quoted from the artifact: *"slowest specialist wall clock minus second slowest, per
run: the most any single-agent fix can remove from fan-out before another agent becomes the
floor."*

<Callout type="warn" title="Making the critical-path agent instantaneous buys 6.34 s at the median">
Not 20 s. Not "half the latency." **6.34 s**, at p50 — from a 31.45 s end-to-end p50 to roughly
25 s if nothing else changed, which is about a 20% reduction and does **not** touch either
over-target run.

The minimum of 0.014 s is the sharpest form of the point: in at least one run the two slowest
agents finished **14 milliseconds apart**, so removing the leader bought nothing at all. The
p95 of 20.56 s is the opposite case — the runs where one agent genuinely dominated — and those are
precisely the stalled ones, which no tier change prevents.
</Callout>

## What was actually done about it

`rings` was moved from Opus 5 to Sonnet 5 on 2026-07-28, on measurement rather than on this
latency observation alone.

From `evals/results/tier_quality.json`, n=5 applications per cell, paired so only same-payload
analyses are compared:

| | Opus 5 | Sonnet 5 |
|---|---:|---:|
| Band agreement with the pinned expectation | 4 / 5 | **4 / 5** |
| Judge-confirmed false positives | 1 | **1** |
| Judge calibration mean | 4.20 | 4.00 |
| Wall clock p50 | 32.81 s | **22.05 s** |
| $/call p50 | $0.0696 | **$0.0241** |
| Output tokens p50 | 2,448 | 2,026 |

Same band, same false positive, 1.49x faster, 2.9x cheaper. The only remaining Opus edge is
**+0.20 judge calibration**, which is **smaller than the judge's own measured +0.31 self-preference
for its family** and is therefore not distinguishable from bias. Full reasoning:
[tier re-derivation](/docs/model-selection/tier-rederivation) and
[judge methodology](/docs/model-selection/judge-methodology).

<Callout type="error" title="NOT MEASURED: the end-to-end effect of that change">
The 42-run benchmark **predates** the move — `run.model_ids` still records rings and synthetic on
Opus 5. So no measured end-to-end p50 exists for the current assignment.

Applying the 6.34 s p50 ceiling to it would be arithmetic on a projection, and this site does not
publish that. One command produces the measurement:

```bash
AGENTCORE_BENCH_ALLOW_LIVE=1 python -m evals.bench --pipeline --repetitions 3
```

Roughly $5 of spend, about 25 minutes of wall clock.
</Callout>

## Where the remaining floor sits, and where it does not

If `rings` drops to roughly its Sonnet-5 p50 of 22.05 s, the next candidates on measured p50 are
`bustout` at 14.46 s and `identity` at 12.56 s. Both are already on Sonnet 5, so there is no tier
step left to take on either.

That leaves exactly two levers on the floor, and only one of them is ours:

| Lever | Status |
|---|---|
| The **missing character cap** in the bustout and rings prompts | The customer's artifact. Those two write 1,331 output tokens p50 against 381 for the six capped agents, and latency is linear in output tokens at r²=0.9531 (n=332). **Unmeasured** whether adding the sentence helps. [The ask](/docs/open-questions#5-two-of-her-prompts-carry-no-character-cap) |
| Lowering `max_tokens` | **Measured and rejected.** 14 of 30 cells truncated, no latency gain, 13 of 30 band agreement. [The measurement](/docs/performance/the-tail#the-max-tokens-fix-was-measured-and-it-failed) |

<Callout type="info" title="One lever exists that this engagement did not test">
Moving the four cross-family-swept agents to GPT-5.6 Luna would drop the fan-out **floor lower
bound** from **22.05 s** (rings, the swept agent that currently binds) to **8.09 s** (`income`,
the fastest unswept agent that would then bind) — and a projected **47.8%** cost reduction,
$0.1247 → $0.0651 per adjudication.

Both are **projections**, labelled as such in `docs/model-selection.md` §"the price of holding".
The terms are measured — 22.05 s from `evals/results/tier_quality.json` (n=5) and 8.09 s from
`evals/results/pipeline_14x3_us-east-1.json` (n=42) — but the arrangement is not, and
`python -m evals.bench --pipeline` is what would confirm either.

It is rejected on `_context` grounding, not on latency, and one specific reason is a critical-path
reason: a **partial** migration would leave `income` — which has **no quality evidence at all**,
in either study — as the binding fan-out agent.
[Cross-family study](/docs/model-selection/cross-family-study).
</Callout>

## For Friday: which fixtures to demo on

The only defensible basis for predicting a single live run is an application that ran three
repetitions of byte-identical input within a narrow band. Three qualify.

<Callout type="info" title="Recommended demo fixtures">
| Application | Repetitions (s) | Spread | Critical path each rep | Pinned band | Observed |
|---|---|---:|---|---|---|
| **APP-1010** | 27.96, 28.38, 28.59 | **0.64 s** | rings, rings, rings | LOW RISK | LOW RISK, 3/3 |
| **APP-1002** | 22.32, 22.92, 23.18 | **0.86 s** | rings, rings, rings | LOW RISK | LOW RISK, 3/3 |
| **APP-1011** | 21.22, 22.40, 22.79 | **1.57 s** | bustout, bustout, bustout | LOW RISK | LOW RISK, 3/3 |

All three are **verdict-stable** as well as latency-stable, and all three had the **same** agent on
the critical path in all three repetitions — which is a second, independent sign of a
well-behaved cell.

Sources: `evals/results/tail_analysis.json` (spread), `evals/results/pipeline_14x3_us-east-1.json`
(`verdict_stability`, `raw_runs`).
</Callout>

Two things those three fixtures also demonstrate on substance, not just on latency:

- **APP-1010** is an **anti-false-positive** fixture: eleven fired alerts, every one from a single
  shared household phone, pinned LOW. It came back LOW on all three runs. That is the customer's
  own quality bar — *"LOW should be the most common outcome"* — being met on the hardest case for
  it, and it is a much better demo than a HIGH-risk application, which any system can flag.
- **APP-1011** has **no co-borrower and no alerts at all**, which is the `straw` prompt's
  "inherently minimal, 2-3 sentences" branch. It is also the run that lost two specialists to
  `server_5xx` on repetition 3 and **still validated all 21 keys in 21.2 s** — so if a 5xx happens
  live, that behaviour has been seen.

### What to avoid, and why

| Application | Repetitions (s) | Why not |
|---|---|---|
| **APP-1007** | 23.52, 34.58, **74.97** | 51.45 s spread on identical input. The 75 s run |
| **APP-1001** | 18.93, 24.25, **70.24** | 51.32 s spread. The 66 s `income` stall |
| APP-1005 | 42.01, 43.91, 46.55 | Tight, but **verdict-unstable** (HIGH / MEDIUM across reps) and the slowest stable application |
| APP-1012 | 36.58, 38.22, 42.72 | Verdict-unstable, matched its pin 1 of 3 |
| APP-1003 | 31.68, 34.30, 34.41 | Latency-stable but returned LOW on all three runs against a MEDIUM pin |

<Callout type="warn" title="n=3 is an observation, not a guarantee">
Stated plainly because it is the part a demo plan gets wrong: the stall rate measured in this
engagement is **0.6% per call**, and one adjudication makes **eight** calls. Three clean
repetitions of APP-1010 is evidence that its workload is well-behaved; it is not protection
against a draw.

And the 0.6% itself is *"a count divided by a denominator, not a predicted frequency"* — two events
over 336 calls cannot support a rate. If a live demo draws a stall, the correct response is the
tail page: it is inside Bedrock's own reported latency, there were zero throttles, and 40 of 42
measured runs finished under target.
</Callout>
