## The question

The 42-run benchmark has a p50 of 31.45 s and a max of **74.97 s**. Two runs of 42 exceeded the
customer's 60-second target. Those two runs are the whole risk in the performance story, so they
were investigated as their own study rather than described as variance.

The study asks four questions in order, and each one eliminates an explanation:

| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| 1. Where in the pipeline is the tail? | In one specialist call, at 98.8% and 99.3% of its run's fan-out |
| 2. Is it output length (verbosity)? | **No** for the tail; **yes** for the floor. Two mechanisms, not one |
| 3. Is it retries or throttling? | **Ruled out.** Zero throttles on 336 calls; ≤1.8% of the excess attributable |
| 4. What is the theoretical floor? | The slowest agent. It is 96.3% of fan-out at the median |

## 1. The tail is not the agent that sets the median

| `bustout` | 7 |
| `income` | 5 |

### 61% of the variance is *within* an application

The decisive framing. Every application ran three repetitions of **byte-identical** input —
fixtures are deterministic and `build_payload` runs outside the timed window — so any spread
within a row is service-side, not workload.

| | Share of total variance |
|---|---:|
| Between applications | 39.0% |
| **Within an application, across repetitions of identical input** | **61.0%** |

Method, quoted: *"one-way sum-of-squares split on application id; reported as a variance SHARE,
not an F test — n=3 per cell cannot support a significance claim."* Interpretation rule, also
quoted: *"within-application share above 50% means most of the spread is service-side variance on
identical input, so a verbosity or model change cannot remove it."*

<Callout type="info" title="Repetition index is a wall-clock window, not a warm-up state">
Per-repetition means were 30.29 s / **37.62 s** / 30.50 s, and both over-target runs are in

Agent on a shared botocore client, so there is no per-agent cache to warm."* Repetition 2 is
simply the wall-clock window the two stalls happened to land in. Two events cannot establish a
time-of-day effect either, and the study does not claim one.
</Callout>

## 2. Latency is linear in output tokens — except for exactly two calls

Pooled regression of wall clock against output tokens:

| Fit | n | r | r² | ms per output token | Fixed overhead | Marginal rate |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| All calls | 334 | 0.8514 | 0.7249 | 11.2419 | 3.40 s | 89.0 tok/s |
| **Excluding the 2 stalls** | **332** | **0.9763** | **0.9531** | 11.3379 | 2.98 s | 88.2 tok/s |

Removing two calls out of 334 lifts r² from 0.72 to **0.95**. That is the finding: the model is
`wall_ms = 2980 + 11.34 × output_tokens` for 332 calls, and two calls obey a different mechanism.

### The two stalls

| Application | Rep | Agent | Wall | Model-reported latency | Unaccounted | Output tokens | **tok/s** | stop_reason | Error |
|---|---:|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---|---|
| APP-1007 | 2 | bustout | 69.78 s | 69.45 s | 0.33 s | 1,120 | **16.1** | `end_turn` | none |
| APP-1001 | 2 | income | 66.01 s | 65.01 s | 1.00 s | 306 | **4.7** | `end_turn` | none |

Against the healthy population, n=332 calls under the 60 s threshold:

| Output tok/s | min | p50 | p95 | **p99** | max | mean |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| n=332 | 20.93 | **65.50** | 86.63 | **89.78** | 95.88 | 65.93 |

<Callout type="warn" title="This is the one p99 in the engagement that clears its minimum">
n=332 exceeds the 100-sample requirement, so `p99 = 89.78 tok/s` is a real p99 and the
`suppressed` map for that sample is empty. Every *other* percentile quoted on this site sits at
n=42 or below on the p99 axis and carries a refusal instead. The distinction is worth making
because it shows the policy is a threshold, not a blanket refusal.
</Callout>

The `income` stall is the sharper one: **306 output tokens in 66 seconds**. At the fitted
marginal rate of 88.2 tok/s, 306 tokens should have taken about 6.5 seconds including overhead —
and `income`'s p50 in the same benchmark is 8.09 s. It was **14x** slower than the median call
in its own population while writing a *below-median* amount of text.

The diagnosis rule the artifact states, before applying it: *"A stall whose tok/s is an order of
magnitude below the healthy median, whose unaccounted time is near zero and whose stop_reason is
end_turn was slow INSIDE the model. It is not a retry, not a truncation, and not verbosity — it
is server-side generation speed, which the client cannot configure away."*

### The verbosity half is real, and it is a different problem

Verbosity does not explain the tail. It **does** explain the floor.

| | Domains | n calls | Output tok p50 | Output tok max | Analysis chars p50 | Over 4,000 chars |
|---|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Prompt caps at 4,000 chars | identity, dealer, straw, employment, income, synthetic | 251 | **381** | 3,582 | 1,224 | 9 (3.6%) |
| **No cap in prompt** | **bustout, rings** | 83 | **1,331** | 3,257 | 3,519 | **25 (30.1%)** |

3.5x the output tokens. Latency is linear in output tokens at r²=0.9531, so those two agents buy
the floor one token at a time. They are the two whose prompts carry
[no character cap at all](/docs/fidelity/output-contract-defects#the-two-prompts-with-no-character-cap).

Per-domain fits, which also show *where* the two stalls live:

| Agent | Prompt cap | n | r² all | r² excl. stalls | ms/output token | Fixed overhead |
|---|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| identity | 4000 | 42 | 0.9834 | 0.9834 | 10.34 | 4.20 s |
| dealer | 4000 | 42 | 0.9107 | 0.9107 | 10.37 | 3.70 s |
| straw | 4000 | 42 | 0.8439 | 0.8439 | 10.08 | 2.48 s |
| employment | 4000 | 42 | 0.9669 | 0.9669 | 10.71 | 3.17 s |
| **income** | 4000 | 42 | **0.3855** | **0.9619** | 10.63 | 3.18 s |
| synthetic | 4000 | 41 | 0.8931 | 0.8931 | 12.90 | 2.48 s |
| **bustout** | **NONE** | 42 | **0.3077** | **0.9810** | 10.83 | 2.16 s |
| rings | **NONE** | 41 | 0.8437 | 0.8437 | 13.80 | 1.10 s |

`income` and `bustout` are the only two rows where r² moves at all when the stalls are removed —
0.39 → 0.96 and 0.31 → 0.98 — because they are the two agents that stalled. One outlier in 42
destroys the fit for that agent and leaves the other six untouched. That is a much stronger form
of "two events, one mechanism" than a pooled number.

<Callout type="info" title="A character cap and a max_tokens cap are not the same constraint">
Measured chars-per-output-token medians: dealer 4.51, straw 4.22, synthetic 3.10, employment
3.03, rings 2.81, income 2.72, bustout 2.67, **identity 1.55** — with an observed minimum of
**0.33** on an identity call that spent 3,582 output tokens to produce 1,191 characters of
analysis.

Quoting the artifact: *"Where this ratio collapses, the model is billed for output the analysis
text does not contain, so a cap chosen from the character contract truncates earlier than the
contract implies."* A `max_tokens` derived from "4,000 characters" is therefore not a translation
of the customer's rule — it is a different and unpredictably tighter rule.
</Callout>

## 3. It is not retries and it is not throttling

The strongest single piece of evidence, because it removes the explanation everyone reaches for
first.

| | Value |
|---|---|
| Retry strategy in force | `max_attempts=4`, `initial_delay=1`, `max_delay=8` → ladder **[1, 2, 4] s**, worst case +7 s |
| What it retries on | **`ModelThrottledException` only** (`strands.event_loop._retry` 1.26.0) |
| `ThrottlingException` / `ModelThrottledException` recorded on 336 calls | **0** |
| Failure kinds actually recorded | `synthetic: server_5xx` ×1, `rings: server_5xx` ×1 |
| Synthesis repair attempts | **0** |
| `stop_reason` distribution | `end_turn` 334, `None` 2 (the two 5xx) |

Wall clock minus the model's own `latencyMs`, over the 334 calls that reported both clocks:

| n | min | p50 | p95 | p99 | max | mean |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 334 | 0.16 s | **0.73 s** | 1.69 s | 2.59 s | 7.66 s | 0.81 s |

Bucketed against the backoff ladder:

| Bucket | n calls |
|---|---:|
| Below the first backoff step — **cannot** be a retry | 246 |
| One step, consistent with at most one retry | 85 |
| Two steps or more | 3 |
| At or above the full ladder | 1 |

And on the two stalls specifically: unaccounted wall clock of **1.00 s** and **0.33 s**.

<Callout type="error" title="The verdict, quoted in full">
> *"RULED OUT as the cause of the tail. No ThrottlingException was recorded on any of 336 calls,
> and ModelThrottledException is the only exception this strategy retries. The two stalled calls
> carry 1.00s, 0.33s of unaccounted wall clock; even attributing ALL of it to backoff explains at
> most **1.8%** of their excess over the median call. The tail is inside the model's own reported
> latency, not in the client."*

The 1.8% is the load-bearing number. It is not "we found no evidence of retries" — it is
"even if every unaccounted millisecond were backoff, it explains 1.8% of the problem." That also
rules out connection-pool queueing, since queueing would appear as unaccounted client-side wall
clock too.
</Callout>

<Callout type="info" title="A 5xx has the opposite shape, and the sample contains one">
The degraded run, APP-1011 rep 3, lost both `synthetic` and `rings` to `InternalServerException`
and finished in **21.2 s** — among the quickest in the sample. The two failed calls spent 8.57 s
and 8.02 s before giving up.

A 5xx **fails fast**, so it costs a **fraud dimension, not time**. A stall costs **time and loses
nothing**. They are different failures with opposite signatures, and conflating them is how a
tail investigation goes wrong.
</Callout>

## 4. The floor: fan-out cannot beat its slowest agent

| Layer | n | p50 | p95 | max |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Fan-out floor (slowest agent's own wall clock) | 42 | **24.03 s** | 41.04 s | 69.78 s |
| Orchestration above the floor | 42 | **0.83 s** | 1.87 s | 2.71 s |
| Synthesis + validation | 42 | 4.20 s | 5.59 s | 15.21 s |

| Share | Median |
|---|---:|
| Floor as a share of fan-out | **96.3%** |
| Fan-out as a share of end-to-end | 85.6% |
| Post-fan-out as a share of end-to-end | 14.4% |

Quoting the reading: *"Fan-out cannot return before its slowest member, so the floor distribution
IS the fan-out latency distribution up to ~1s of gathering. Any fix that does not lower the
slowest agent lowers nothing."*

Orchestration costs 0.83 s at the median and **never more than 2.71 s**. There is nothing to win
there — a perfect orchestration layer buys under a second.

### The ceiling on any single-agent fix

Slowest minus second-slowest, per run — the most any single-agent improvement can remove before
another agent becomes the floor:

| n | min | p50 | p95 | max | mean |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 42 | 0.014 s | **6.34 s** | 20.56 s | 50.13 s | 8.53 s |

Making the critical-path agent **instantaneous** recovers 6.34 s at the median. Detail:
[critical path](/docs/performance/critical-path).

## The max_tokens fix was measured, and it failed

The obvious engineering response to "the floor is bought one output token at a time" is to cap
output tokens. It was tested rather than assumed: **30 live Bedrock calls** in us-east-1,
2026-07-28, caps 1024 / 1536 / 2048 / 3072 / 4096 across bustout and rings on APP-1004, APP-1012
and APP-1013. **$0.6278 measured** on the 16 that returned an analysis, plus a bounded **~$0.4378**
on the 14 that truncated.

The control is cap 4096 — the shipped value for these tiers — **re-run in the same session**, so a
cap delta is measured against a same-session control rather than against a run from another day.

The gate stated before the sweep ran: *"A cap is only a fix if it (a) lowers wall clock,
(b) truncates nothing, and (c) leaves every specialist's risk band unchanged. Failing (b) or (c)
makes a latency win a lost fraud dimension."*

### Criterion (a): it did not shorten the calls that survived it — FAILED

Of the 10 cells that returned an analysis at **both** their cap and the control:

| | Value |
|---|---:|
| Faster than control | **5** |
| Slower than control | **5** |
| Delta vs control: min / p50 / mean / max | −10.39 s / −1.52 s / **−0.23 s** / **+17.84 s** |

A mean of −0.23 s over ten cells, with a worst case of **17.84 seconds slower**. The mechanism is
visible in the cell table: at a given cap the model writes what it wants to write, and the cap
either does not bind or kills the call. **It never produces a shorter analysis.**

<Callout type="warn" title="14 cells were excluded from the latency comparison, and the exclusion is the honest part">
Quoted: *"Only cells that RETURNED AN ANALYSIS at both caps can support a latency claim. A cell
that truncated finished early because it stopped, not because it was faster, and counting it
would turn a lost fraud dimension into a latency win."*

Including them would have produced a headline like "lower caps are 25% faster." That headline
would have been arithmetic on nothing.
</Callout>

### Criterion (b): 14 of 30 truncated — FAILED

| | Value |
|---|---:|
| Cells truncated | **14 of 30** |
| Cells completed | 16 |
| Wall clock burned for **no analysis** | **263.2 s** (mean 18.8 s each, max 29.0 s) |
| Bounded cost of those calls | **~$0.4378** |

A truncation here is `MaxTokensReachedException` from Strands, which **fails the whole specialist**
rather than shortening it. Quoting the artifact: *"The output is not a briefer analysis; the
dimension is gone and fan-out degrades to seven of eight."*

<Callout type="error" title="A truncated call is the worst outcome available">
Full wall clock. Fully billed. Text discarded. Fraud dimension lost. **A cap low enough to shorten
these agents is a cap low enough to delete them.**

The bounded-cost basis is stated per cell rather than estimated: output tokens assumed equal to
the cap — which is what raised the exception — and input tokens taken from that same cell's
completed sibling at another cap. Labelled *bounded*, not measured, because a truncated call
reports no usage.
</Callout>

### And the failure boundary is not monotonic

| Agent | Caps tested | Truncated at | Completed at | Lowest cap where **every** cell completed |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| bustout | 1024, 1536, 2048, 3072, 4096 | **1024, 1536, 2048** | 1536, 2048, 3072, 4096 | **3072** |
| rings | 1024, 1536, 2048, 3072, 4096 | **1024, 1536, 2048** | 1536, 2048, 3072, 4096 | **3072** |

Note the overlap: both agents **both truncated and completed** at 1536 and at 2048, depending on
the application and the draw. One cell — APP-1012 rings — completed at 1536 and then **truncated at
2048**, a higher cap. There is no safe cap below the shipped 4096 for these two agents on these
three applications, and 3072 is only "lowest that completed every cell" at one draw each, which is
not enough to call it safe.

### Criterion (c): band agreement 13 of 30 — FAILED

| | Value |
|---|---:|
| Cells agreeing with the same-session control band | **13 of 30** |
| Disagreeing | 17 |
| Of which the band was **lost entirely** (truncation) | 14 |

The three disagreements that were **not** truncations still moved a fraud band:

| Cell | Control band | At the cap |
|---|---|---|
| APP-1012 bustout @ 3072 | `HIGH RISK` | `POSSIBLE RISK` |
| APP-1012 rings @ 1536 | `POSSIBLE RISK` | `HIGH RISK` |
| APP-1013 rings @ 3072 | `HIGH RISK` | `POSSIBLE RISK` |

<Callout type="warn" title="NOT ESTABLISHED — and the artifact refuses to claim it">
Quoted: *"whether those band moves are caused by the cap or are the same run-to-run instability
the 42-run benchmark already records on APP-1005 and APP-1012. One draw per cell cannot separate
the two, and this sweep does not claim to."*

The source benchmark independently records band instability on APP-1005 and APP-1012 with **no cap
change at all**, so the honest statement is "evidence of instability at that cap," not "the cap
caused it." One draw per cell, and the cell count is stated so nobody reads it as more.
</Callout>

### Which caps would bind on the observed traffic

Separately from the live sweep, a census of the 334 successful pipeline calls — which ones already
wrote more than a given cap, i.e. which would have raised `MaxTokensReachedException`:

| max_tokens | Calls already over it | % | Domains affected |
|---:|---:|---:|---|
| 1024 | 105 / 334 | **31.4%** | rings 41, bustout 24, identity 14, income 13, employment 5, synthetic 5, dealer 3 |
| 1536 | 45 / 334 | 13.5% | rings 18, income 9, bustout 8, identity 7, employment 2, synthetic 1 |
| 2048 | 21 / 334 | 6.3% | identity 7, income 5, bustout 5, rings 4 |
| 3072 | 3 / 334 | 0.9% | identity 2, bustout 1 |
| **4096** | **0 / 334** | **0.0%** | — |

Shipped caps by tier: light 3072, mid 4096, heavy 4096. The census is labelled precisely:
*"MEASURED: which observed calls already wrote more than the cap... NOT MEASURED here: the latency
or cost of running with the cap, because a cap changes what the model writes."*

At 1024 the binding is not confined to the two verbose agents — **seven of eight** domains are
affected, including `dealer` on Haiku.

## The conclusion

The tail has **two mechanisms** and only one of them belongs to us.

<Callout type="info" title="1. Verbosity sets the FLOOR — fixable, but not by a cap">
Wall clock is linear in output tokens at r²=0.9531 over n=332, so the floor is bought one token
at a time. The two agents with no character cap write 1,331 output tokens at p50 against 381 for
the six that have one, and `rings` alone owned the critical path in 25 of 42 runs.

The live sweep shows `max_tokens` is the **wrong instrument**: it does not shorten the analysis,
it deletes it. The instrument that matches the mechanism is the **customer's own prompt text** —
adding the "max 4000 characters" sentence bustout and rings are missing. That is a one-line change
to two verbatim customer artifacts, which is
[her decision, not ours](/docs/open-questions#5-two-of-her-prompts-carry-no-character-cap).

**NOT MEASURED:** whether adding that sentence shortens the output. This work did not modify the
customer's verbatim prompts.
</Callout>

<Callout type="error" title="2. Server-side generation speed sets the TAIL — not fixable at all">
Both over-target runs were caused by **2 of 336 calls (0.6%)** generating at 16.1 and 4.7 output
tok/s against a 66 tok/s median, **inside the model's own reported latency**, with no throttle, no
retry and `stop_reason=end_turn`. **61.0%** of all end-to-end variance is within-application, i.e.
across repetitions of byte-identical input.

Quoted verbatim: *"This is irreducible run-to-run Bedrock variance and no client-side change
removes it."*
</Callout>

## What this analysis explicitly did **not** establish

The refusals, quoted from the artifact's own closing section. This list is the reason the rest of
the page can be trusted.

- **Why** a Bedrock call generates at 4.7 tok/s. *"Only that it does, that it did so on 0.6% of
  336 calls, and that it is not throttling, not retries and not output length."*
- **Whether the stall rate is stable.** *"Two events over 336 calls cannot support a rate; the
  0.6% figure is a count divided by a denominator, not a predicted frequency."*
- Whether adding the missing character cap to the prompts shortens their output.
- Whether the band moves in the sweep were **caused** by the cap.
- **Any p99.** *"n=42 runs and n=42 fan-outs; the p99 stays suppressed, which matters here
  precisely because the tail is the question — a 0.6%-per-call event is exactly what a sample this
  size cannot pin down."*
- AgentCore runtime overhead, signal generation, and concurrent throughput, all of which the
  source benchmark also excludes.

<Callout type="warn" title="The most important refusal is the fifth one">
The tail is a rare-event question, and a rare-event question needs a rare-event sample. n=42 is
not one. So the honest position is: **the tail is characterised, not bounded.** We can say what
caused these two events and what did not. We cannot say how often it happens, and this site does
not.
</Callout>

## The mitigation that is actually available

Not a code change. Run selection plus a floor reduction.

<Step>

### Demo on an application with a measured-stable profile

Three repetitions of identical input each, which is the only defensible basis for predicting one
live run:

| Application | Repetitions (s) | Spread |
|---|---|---:|
| **APP-1010** | 27.96, 28.38, 28.59 | **0.64 s** |
| **APP-1002** | 22.32, 22.92, 23.18 | **0.86 s** |
| **APP-1011** | 21.22, 22.40, 22.79 | **1.57 s** |

Against APP-1007 (23.5 / 34.6 / 75.0 s) and APP-1001 (18.9 / 24.3 / 70.2 s), whose spreads exceed
51 s.

**Caveat stated plainly:** n=3 per application makes this a low-variance **observation**, not a
guarantee. The stall rate is 0.6% **per call** and a run makes eight of them.

</Step>

<Step>

### Move rings off Opus 5 — done, and the reason is measured

`rings` owned the critical path in 25 of 42 runs and the floor is 96.3% of fan-out, so this is
the one change that moves the median.

**From the committed artifact** `evals/results/tier_quality.json`, n=5 applications per cell:
`rings` p50 wall clock **22.05 s on Sonnet 5 against 32.81 s on Opus 5** at 2,026 against 2,448
output tokens p50, both reaching the pinned band **4 of 5** with the same one judge-confirmed
false positive.

`evals/results/tail_analysis.md` additionally quotes a smaller separate draw — *"n=3
applications, one draw each"* — at 16.22 s vs 32.81 s. That draw's source file is
`/tmp/bench/tiering.json`, **a scratch file that is not a committed artifact**, so the figure to
quote is the 22.05 s one.

The change has been applied to `agents/models.py`. **The end-to-end effect is not measured** — the
42-run benchmark predates it. [Tier re-derivation](/docs/model-selection/tier-rederivation).

</Step>

<Step>

### Do not lower max_tokens

Measured above: 14 of 30 cells truncated, non-monotonically, and a truncated call pays full time
and full money for no analysis.
