description: Two of 336 calls generated at 16.1 and 4.7 output tok/s against a 65.50 tok/s median, inside Bedrock's own reported latency, with zero throttles — and the max_tokens fix that was measured and failed all three of its criteria.
---

*For the customer's engineer. Every figure comes from `evals/results/tail_analysis.json`, derived from `evals/results/pipeline_14x3_us-east-1.json`, with a human-readable sidecar at `evals/results/tail_analysis.md`.*

**Two calls out of 336 caused both over-target runs, and neither is fixable from the client.**
They generated at **16.1** and **4.7** output tokens per second against a healthy median of
**65.50 tok/s** (n=332), entirely inside the model's own reported latency, with zero throttles and
`stop_reason=end_turn`. This is the page where a measurement was allowed to say no: four candidate
causes were tested, three were ruled out, and the obvious engineering fix was then measured and
made things worse.

## Four questions, asked in order

Two mechanisms come out of that sequence, and only one of them belongs to us. Verbosity sets the
**floor** and is addressable through the prompt. Server-side generation speed sets the **tail** and
is not addressable at all.

## Where the tail sits, and who owns it

Critical path — the slowest specialist — across all 42 runs, then split by run speed:

| Specialist | Slowest in all 42 runs | In the 10 slowest runs | In the 10 fastest runs |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| `rings` | **25** | 2 | **7** |
| `bustout` | 7 | 3 | 3 |
| `income` | 5 | 2 | 0 |
| `identity` | 4 | 3 | 0 |
| `synthetic` | 1 | 0 | 0 |

`same_owner_for_median_and_tail: false` is recorded in the artifact, and this table is why: `rings`
owns the median and appears in the fastest ten more than three times as often as in the slowest
ten. Tail ownership belongs to `bustout` and `income`.

The six slowest runs, with the critical path's share of its own fan-out:

|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---|---:|
| APP-1007 | 2 | **75.0 s** | 70.6 s | 4.3 s | bustout 69.8 s | **98.8%** |
| APP-1001 | 2 | **70.2 s** | 66.5 s | 3.7 s | income 66.0 s | **99.3%** |
| APP-1005 | 1 | 46.5 s | 41.0 s | 5.5 s | identity 41.0 s | 100.0% |
| APP-1005 | 3 | 43.9 s | 39.7 s | 4.2 s | identity 39.7 s | 100.0% |
| APP-1012 | 1 | 42.7 s | 37.5 s | 5.3 s | bustout 36.7 s | 98.0% |

One call held APP-1007 rep 2 open for 40 extra seconds on its own. Its other seven specialists
finished in 29.03 s or less, and six of them in under 10 s.

### 61% of the variance is within an application

Here is the decisive framing. Every application ran three repetitions of **byte-identical** input
— fixtures are deterministic and `build_payload` runs outside the timed window — so any spread
within a row is service-side, not workload.

| | Share of total variance |
|---|---:|
| Between applications | 39.0% |
| **Within an application, across repetitions of identical input** | **61.0%** |

Method, quoted: *"one-way sum-of-squares split on application id; reported as a variance SHARE, not
an F test — n=3 per cell cannot support a significance claim."* Interpretation rule, also quoted:
*"within-application share above 50% means most of the spread is service-side variance on
identical input, so a verbosity or model change cannot remove it."*

Per-application spread, widest first, over 14 applications:

|---|---|---:|---:|
| APP-1007 | 23.52, 34.58, **74.97** | **51.45 s** | 3.19x |
| APP-1001 | 18.93, 24.25, **70.24** | **51.32 s** | 3.71x |
| APP-1014 | 24.75, 31.63, 41.90 | 17.15 s | 1.69x |
| APP-1006 | 22.31, 23.76, 33.67 | 11.35 s | 1.51x |
| APP-1012 | 36.58, 38.22, 42.72 | 6.14 s | 1.17x |
| APP-1008 | 25.50, 28.46, 31.45 | 5.94 s | 1.23x |
| APP-1013 | 34.01, 35.19, 39.77 | 5.75 s | 1.17x |
| APP-1005 | 42.01, 43.91, 46.55 | 4.54 s | 1.11x |
| APP-1004 | 36.25, 37.79, 39.23 | 2.98 s | 1.08x |
| APP-1003 | 31.68, 34.30, 34.41 | 2.73 s | 1.09x |
| APP-1009 | 23.84, 25.67, 25.80 | 1.95 s | 1.08x |

Twelve of the fourteen sit within 17 s of themselves; two sit within 51 s of themselves. So the
distribution is not "some applications are slow" — it is "any application can draw a slow call."

Repetition index is a wall-clock window, not a warm-up state. Per-repetition means ran 30.29 s /
**37.62 s** / 30.50 s, and both over-target runs landed in repetition 2, but quoting the artifact,
*"each agent is a fresh Strands Agent on a shared botocore client, so there is no per-agent cache
to warm."* Repetition 2 is simply the window the two stalls happened to fall in, and two events
cannot establish a time-of-day effect either. The study does not claim one.

## Latency is linear in output tokens — except for two calls

Pooled regression of wall clock against output tokens:

| Fit | n | r | r² | ms per output token | Fixed overhead | Marginal rate |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| All calls | 334 | 0.8514 | 0.7249 | 11.2419 | 3.40 s | 89.0 tok/s |
| **Excluding the 2 stalls** | **332** | **0.9763** | **0.9531** | 11.3379 | 2.98 s | 88.2 tok/s |

Removing two calls out of 334 lifts r² from 0.72 to **0.95**. So `wall_ms = 2980 + 11.34 ×
output_tokens` describes 332 calls, and two calls obey a different mechanism entirely.

### The two stalls

| Application | Rep | Agent | Wall | Model-reported latency | Unaccounted | Output tokens | **tok/s** | stop_reason | Error |
|---|---:|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---|---|
| APP-1007 | 2 | bustout | 69.78 s | 69.45 s | 0.33 s | 1,120 | **16.1** | `end_turn` | none |
| APP-1001 | 2 | income | 66.01 s | 65.01 s | 1.00 s | 306 | **4.7** | `end_turn` | none |

Against the healthy population, n=332 calls under the 60 s threshold:

| Output tok/s | n | min | p50 | p95 | p99 | max | mean |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Healthy calls | 332 | 20.93 | **65.50** | 86.63 | **89.78** | 95.88 | 65.93 |

That `p99 = 89.78 tok/s` is the **one p99 in the whole engagement that clears its minimum** — n=332
exceeds the 100-sample requirement, and its `suppressed` map is empty. Every other percentile on
this site sits at n=42 or below on the p99 axis and carries a refusal instead, which is worth
naming because it shows the policy is a threshold rather than a blanket refusal.

Sharper of the two is `income`: **306 output tokens in 66 seconds**. At the fitted marginal rate of
88.2 tok/s those tokens should have taken about 6.5 s including overhead, and `income`'s p50 in the
same benchmark is 8.09 s. It ran **14x** slower than the median call in its own population while
writing a *below-median* amount of text.

Diagnosis rule, stated in the artifact before being applied: *"A stall whose tok/s is an order of
magnitude below the healthy median, whose unaccounted time is near zero and whose stop_reason is
end_turn was slow INSIDE the model. It is not a retry, not a truncation, and not verbosity — it is
server-side generation speed, which the client cannot configure away."*

### Verbosity is real, and it is a different problem

Verbosity does not explain the tail. It does explain the floor.

| | Domains | n calls | Output tok p50 | Output tok max | Analysis chars p50 | Over 4,000 chars |
|---|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Prompt caps at 4,000 chars | identity, dealer, straw, employment, income, synthetic | 251 | **381** | 3,582 | 1,224 | 9 (3.6%) |
| **No cap in prompt** | **bustout, rings** | 83 | **1,331** | 3,257 | 3,519 | **25 (30.1%)** |

3.5x the output tokens. Latency is linear in output tokens at r²=0.9531, so those two agents buy
the floor one token at a time. They are also the two whose prompts carry [no character cap at
all](/docs/fidelity/output-contract-defects#the-two-prompts-with-no-character-cap).

Per-domain fits show *where* the two stalls live:

| Agent | Prompt cap | n | r² all | r² excl. stalls | ms/output token | Fixed overhead |
|---|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| identity | 4000 | 42 | 0.9834 | 0.9834 | 10.34 | 4.20 s |
| dealer | 4000 | 42 | 0.9107 | 0.9107 | 10.37 | 3.70 s |
| straw | 4000 | 42 | 0.8439 | 0.8439 | 10.08 | 2.48 s |
| employment | 4000 | 42 | 0.9669 | 0.9669 | 10.71 | 3.17 s |
| **income** | 4000 | 42 | **0.3855** | **0.9619** | 10.63 | 3.18 s |
| synthetic | 4000 | 41 | 0.8931 | 0.8931 | 12.90 | 2.48 s |
| **bustout** | **NONE** | 42 | **0.3077** | **0.9810** | 10.83 | 2.16 s |
| rings | **NONE** | 41 | 0.8437 | 0.8437 | 13.80 | 1.10 s |

Only `income` and `bustout` move at all when the stalls are removed — 0.39 → 0.96 and 0.31 → 0.98 —
because they are the two agents that stalled. One outlier in 42 destroys the fit for that agent and
leaves the other six untouched, which is a much stronger form of "two events, one mechanism" than a
pooled number.

Note also that a character cap and a `max_tokens` cap are not the same constraint. Measured
chars-per-output-token medians: dealer 4.51, straw 4.22, synthetic 3.10, employment 3.03, rings
2.81, income 2.72, bustout 2.67, **identity 1.55** — with an observed minimum of **0.33** on an
identity call that spent 3,582 output tokens to produce 1,191 characters of analysis. Quoting the
artifact: *"Where this ratio collapses, the model is billed for output the analysis text does not
contain, so a cap chosen from the character contract truncates earlier than the contract implies."*
A `max_tokens` derived from "4,000 characters" is a different and unpredictably tighter rule.

## Not retries, and not throttling

This is the strongest single piece of evidence, because it removes the explanation everyone reaches
for first.

| | Value |
|---|---|
| Retry strategy in force | `max_attempts=4`, `initial_delay=1`, `max_delay=8` → ladder **[1, 2, 4] s**, worst case +7 s |
| What it retries on | **`ModelThrottledException` only** (`strands.event_loop._retry` 1.26.0) |
| `ThrottlingException` / `ModelThrottledException` recorded on 336 calls | **0** |
| Failure kinds actually recorded | `synthetic: server_5xx` ×1, `rings: server_5xx` ×1 |
| Synthesis repair attempts | **0** |
| `stop_reason` distribution | `end_turn` 334, `None` 2 (the two 5xx) |

Wall clock minus the model's own `latencyMs`, over the 334 calls reporting both clocks, then
bucketed against the backoff ladder:

| n | min | p50 | p95 | p99 | max | mean |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 334 | 0.16 s | **0.73 s** | 1.69 s | 2.59 s | 7.66 s | 0.81 s |

| Bucket | n calls |
|---|---:|
| Below the first backoff step — **cannot** be a retry | 246 |
| One step, consistent with at most one retry | 85 |
| Two steps or more | 3 |
| At or above the full ladder | 1 |

<Callout type="error" title="Verdict, quoted in full">
> *"RULED OUT as the cause of the tail. No ThrottlingException was recorded on any of 336 calls,
> and ModelThrottledException is the only exception this strategy retries. The two stalled calls
> carry 1.00s, 0.33s of unaccounted wall clock; even attributing ALL of it to backoff explains at
> most **1.8%** of their excess over the median call. The tail is inside the model's own reported
> latency, not in the client."*

That 1.8% is the load-bearing number. Not "we found no evidence of retries" but "even if every
unaccounted millisecond were backoff, it explains 1.8% of the problem." Connection-pool queueing
falls with it, since queueing would also appear as unaccounted client-side wall clock.
</Callout>

A 5xx has the opposite shape, and this sample contains one. The degraded run, APP-1011 rep 3, lost
both `synthetic` and `rings` to `InternalServerException` and finished in **21.2 s**, among the
quickest in the sample; the two failed calls spent 8.57 s and 8.02 s before giving up. So a 5xx
**fails fast** and costs a fraud dimension rather than time, while a stall costs time and loses
nothing. Conflating those two signatures is how a tail investigation goes wrong.

## Floor: fan-out cannot beat its slowest agent

| Layer | n | p50 | p95 | max |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Fan-out floor (slowest agent's own wall clock) | 42 | **24.03 s** | 41.04 s | 69.78 s |
| Orchestration above the floor | 42 | **0.83 s** | 1.87 s | 2.71 s |
| Synthesis + validation | 42 | 4.20 s | 5.59 s | 15.21 s |

| Share | Median |
|---|---:|
| Floor as a share of fan-out | **96.3%** |
| Fan-out as a share of end-to-end | 85.6% |
| Post-fan-out as a share of end-to-end | 14.4% |

Quoting the reading: *"Fan-out cannot return before its slowest member, so the floor distribution
IS the fan-out latency distribution up to ~1s of gathering. Any fix that does not lower the slowest
agent lowers nothing."* Orchestration costs 0.83 s at the median and never more than 2.71 s across
42 runs, so a perfect orchestration layer buys under a second.

Slowest minus second-slowest, per run, bounds any single-agent fix — the most it can remove before
another agent becomes the floor:

| n | min | p50 | p95 | max | mean |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 42 | 0.014 s | **6.34 s** | 20.56 s | 50.13 s | 8.53 s |

Making the critical-path agent instantaneous recovers 6.34 s at the median. Detail: [Critical
path](/docs/performance/critical-path).

## max_tokens sweep [#the-max-tokens-fix-was-measured-and-it-failed]

"The floor is bought one output token at a time" invites capping output tokens. That was tested
rather than assumed: **30 live Bedrock calls** in us-east-1 on 2026-07-28, caps 1024 / 1536 / 2048 /
3072 / 4096 across bustout and rings on APP-1004, APP-1012 and APP-1013. **$0.627827 measured** on
the 16 cells that returned an analysis, plus a bounded **$0.437793** on the 14 that truncated. The
control is cap 4096 — the shipped value for these tiers — **re-run in the same session**, so a cap
delta is measured against a same-session control rather than against a run from another day.

Gate stated before the sweep ran: *"A cap is only a fix if it (a) lowers wall clock, (b) truncates
nothing, and (c) leaves every specialist's risk band unchanged. Failing (b) or (c) makes a latency
win a lost fraud dimension."*

| Criterion | Measured result | Pass/fail |
|---|---|---|
| **(a)** Lowers wall clock | 10 comparable cells: 5 faster, 5 slower. Delta min −10.39 s / p50 −1.52 s / mean **−0.23 s** / max **+17.84 s** | **FAILED** |
| **(b)** Truncates nothing | **14 of 30 cells truncated.** 263.2 s of wall clock burned for no analysis (mean 18.8 s each, max 29.0 s) | **FAILED** |
| **(c)** Leaves every band unchanged | **13 of 30** agreed with the same-session control band. 17 disagreed, of which 14 lost the band entirely | **FAILED** |

Only cells that returned an analysis at **both** their cap and the control can support a latency
claim, and the 14 truncated cells are excluded from criterion (a) for that reason. Quoting:
*"A cell that truncated finished early because it stopped, not because it was faster, and counting
it would turn a lost fraud dimension into a latency win."* Including them would have produced a
headline like "lower caps are 25% faster," which would have been arithmetic on nothing.

Mechanism behind criterion (a) is visible in the cell table: at a given cap the model writes what
it wants to write, and the cap either does not bind or kills the call. **It never produces a
shorter analysis.**

**A truncated call is the worst outcome available.** Truncation here is `MaxTokensReachedException`
from Strands, which **fails the whole specialist** rather than shortening it. Quoting the artifact:
*"The output is not a briefer analysis; the dimension is gone and fan-out degrades to seven of
eight."* Full wall clock, fully billed, text discarded, fraud dimension lost — so **a cap low enough
to shorten these agents is a cap low enough to delete them.**

That $0.437793 basis is stated per cell rather than estimated: output tokens assumed equal to the
cap, which is what raised the exception, and input tokens taken from that same cell's completed
sibling at another cap. It is labelled *bounded*, not measured, because a truncated call reports no
usage.

### The failure boundary is not monotonic

| Agent | Caps tested | Truncated at | Completed at | Lowest cap where **every** cell completed |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| bustout | 1024, 1536, 2048, 3072, 4096 | **1024, 1536, 2048** | 1536, 2048, 3072, 4096 | **3072** |
| rings | 1024, 1536, 2048, 3072, 4096 | **1024, 1536, 2048** | 1536, 2048, 3072, 4096 | **3072** |

Note the overlap: both agents **both truncated and completed** at 1536 and at 2048, depending on
the application and the draw. One cell — APP-1012 rings — completed at 1536 and then **truncated at
2048**, a higher cap. No safe cap below the shipped 4096 exists for these two agents on these three
applications, and 3072 is only "lowest that completed every cell" at one draw each, which is not
enough to call it safe.

Three of the 17 band disagreements were **not** truncations and still moved a fraud band:

| Cell | Control band | At the cap |
|---|---|---|
| APP-1012 bustout @ 3072 | `HIGH RISK` | `POSSIBLE RISK` |
| APP-1012 rings @ 1536 | `POSSIBLE RISK` | `HIGH RISK` |
| APP-1013 rings @ 3072 | `HIGH RISK` | `POSSIBLE RISK` |

Whether the cap **caused** those moves is **NOT ESTABLISHED**, and the artifact refuses to claim
it: *"whether those band moves are caused by the cap or are the same run-to-run instability the
42-run benchmark already records on APP-1005 and APP-1012. One draw per cell cannot separate the
two, and this sweep does not claim to."* The source benchmark independently records band
instability on APP-1005 and APP-1012 with no cap change at all, so the honest statement is
"evidence of instability at that cap."

### Which caps would bind on observed traffic

Separately from the live sweep, a census of the 334 successful pipeline calls — which ones already
wrote more than a given cap, i.e. which would have raised `MaxTokensReachedException`:

| max_tokens | Calls already over it | % | Domains affected |
|---:|---:|---:|---|
| 1024 | 105 / 334 | **31.4%** | rings 41, bustout 24, identity 14, income 13, employment 5, synthetic 5, dealer 3 |
| 1536 | 45 / 334 | 13.5% | rings 18, income 9, bustout 8, identity 7, employment 2, synthetic 1 |
| 2048 | 21 / 334 | 6.3% | identity 7, income 5, bustout 5, rings 4 |
| 3072 | 3 / 334 | 0.9% | identity 2, bustout 1 |
| **4096** | **0 / 334** | **0.0%** | — |

Shipped caps by tier: light 3072, mid 4096, heavy 4096. Census labelling is precise: *"MEASURED:
which observed calls already wrote more than the cap... NOT MEASURED here: the latency or cost of
running with the cap, because a cap changes what the model writes."* At 1024 the binding is not
confined to the two verbose agents — **seven of eight** domains are affected, `dealer` on Haiku
among them.

## Two mechanisms, one conclusion

| | Floor | Tail |
|---|---|---|
| Cause | Output length. r²=0.9531 over n=332, so wall clock is linear in output tokens | Server-side generation speed: 2 of 336 calls (0.6%) at 16.1 and 4.7 tok/s |
| Evidence | bustout and rings write 1,331 output tokens p50 against 381 for the six capped agents; rings owned the critical path in 25 of 42 runs | Inside the model's own reported latency. 0 throttles, no retry, `stop_reason=end_turn`. 61.0% of e2e variance is within-application |
| Right instrument | The **customer's own prompt text** — the "max 4000 characters" sentence bustout and rings are missing | None available. Quoted: *"This is irreducible run-to-run Bedrock variance and no client-side change removes it"* |
| Wrong instrument | `max_tokens`, which does not shorten the analysis but deletes it — measured above, failed all three criteria | — |
| Whose call | Hers. A one-line change to two verbatim customer artifacts, [asked as a question](/docs/open-questions#5-two-of-her-prompts-carry-no-character-cap) | — |

**NOT MEASURED:** whether adding the missing character-cap sentence shortens the output. This work
did not modify the customer's verbatim prompts.

## What this analysis did not establish

Refusals, quoted from the artifact's own closing section. This list is the reason the rest of the
page can be trusted.

- **Why** a Bedrock call generates at 4.7 tok/s. *"Only that it does, that it did so on 0.6% of 336
  calls, and that it is not throttling, not retries and not output length."*
- **Whether the stall rate is stable.** *"Two events over 336 calls cannot support a rate; the 0.6%
  figure is a count divided by a denominator, not a predicted frequency."*
- Whether adding the missing character cap to those prompts shortens their output.
- Whether the band moves in the sweep were **caused** by the cap.
- **Any p99** on the end-to-end axis. *"n=42 runs and n=42 fan-outs; the p99 stays suppressed,
  which matters here precisely because the tail is the question — a 0.6%-per-call event is exactly
  what a sample this size cannot pin down."*
- AgentCore runtime overhead, signal generation, and concurrent throughput, all of which the source
  benchmark also excludes.

Most important of those is the second one. A rare-event question needs a rare-event sample, and
n=42 runs is not one, so the honest position is that **the tail is characterised, not bounded**. We
can say what caused these two events and what did not. We cannot say how often it happens, and this
site does not.

## Mitigation actually available

Not a code change: run selection plus a floor reduction.

| Step | What it is | Status |
|---|---|---|
| Demo on a measured-stable application | APP-1010 (0.64 s spread), APP-1002 (0.86 s), APP-1011 (1.57 s), each over 3 repetitions of identical input — against APP-1007 and APP-1001, whose spreads exceed 51 s | Available now. n=3 makes it a low-variance **observation**, not a guarantee: the stall rate is 0.6% **per call** and a run makes eight |
| Move `rings` off Opus 5 | rings owned the critical path in 25 of 42 runs and the floor is 96.3% of fan-out, so this is the one change that moves the median. `evals/results/tier_quality.json`, n=5 applications per cell: rings p50 **22.05 s on Sonnet 5 against 32.81 s on Opus 5**, at 2,026 against 2,448 output tokens p50, both reaching the pinned band 4 of 5 with the same one judge-confirmed false positive | **Applied** to `agents/models.py`. End-to-end effect **NOT MEASURED** — the 42-run benchmark predates it. [Tier re-derivation](/docs/model-selection/tier-rederivation) |
| Lower `max_tokens` | Measured above | **Rejected.** 14 of 30 cells truncated, non-monotonically, with no latency gain |

One caution on the rings figure: `evals/results/tail_analysis.md` also quotes a smaller separate
draw — *"n=3 applications, one draw each"* — at 16.22 s versus 32.81 s, but that draw's source is
`/tmp/bench/tiering.json`, a scratch file that is not a committed artifact. Quote the 22.05 s
figure.

## Reproducing this

```bash
# re-derive the whole analysis from the committed benchmark, no model calls
python -m evals.tail_analysis --source evals/results/pipeline_14x3_us-east-1.json \
  --json evals/results/tail_analysis.json --markdown evals/results/tail_analysis.md

# the live cap sweep (this one spends money)
AGENTCORE_BENCH_ALLOW_LIVE=1 python -m evals.tail_analysis --live \
  --caps 1024,1536,2048,3072,4096 --domains bustout,rings \
  --applications APP-1004,APP-1012,APP-1013

MOCK_MODE=1 python3 -m pytest tests/test_tail_analysis.py -q     # 50 tests
```

The sweep's provenance string in the artifact is that exact command line, and it records that the
committed summary was *"re-summarised here through the same functions without re-calling any model;
the cell measurements are unchanged."*
