*For the customer's engineer. This page is the mechanism, not a claim: what is loaded, what is hashed, what fails the build, and what was deliberately broken to prove each guard fires. No prompt text is reproduced anywhere on it.*

**Eighteen customer documents are copied byte for byte, sha256-verified on every read, and
separately diffed in CI against her originals.** Paraphrase was made impossible to land rather than
discouraged, because the person reviewing this port wrote all nine production agents and a
paraphrase silently changes what an agent detects.

## Three layers, and the hole each one closes

<Mermaid
  title="Loading and verification chain"
  chart={`
flowchart LR
  D["docs/ customer original"] -->|"shutil.copyfile<br/>never retyped"| V["prompts/verbatim/"]
  V -->|"re-hash on EVERY read"| M["manifest.json<br/>sha256 + bytes + source"]
  M -->|"mismatch"| E["PromptIntegrityError"]
  V -->|"CI byte diff<br/>vs docs/"| T["test_verbatim_copy_is<br/>_byte_identical_to_docs_source"]
  M --> L["loader: strip banner,<br/>de-indent, verify names"]
`}
/>

| Layer | What it does | What it alone cannot catch |
|---|---|---|
| **1. Byte-for-byte copy** | `shutil.copyfile` from `docs/` into `prompts/verbatim/`. No transcription step exists, so a typo has nowhere to enter | Someone editing the copy afterwards |
| **2. sha256 on every read** | `prompts/loader.py:read_verbatim` re-reads and re-hashes the file on each call and compares against `prompts/manifest.json`; a mismatch raises `PromptIntegrityError` naming the file, the expected digest and the found digest | Someone editing the copy **and** regenerating the manifest |
| **3. CI diff against her source** | `test_verbatim_copy_is_byte_identical_to_docs_source` reads both files as **bytes** and asserts equality, parametrised over all 18 names. `SOURCES` in the loader maps each packaged name to its `docs/` origin | Nothing in this chain. It is what makes the manifest non-bypassable |

The loader deliberately does not cache. Quoting its own reasoning, caching would mean *"a file
edited after import keeps serving stale bytes, which is exactly the silent drift the manifest exists
to prevent."* Files are small — the largest in the manifest is
`identity_orchestration_062026.txt` at 17,929 bytes — and are read a couple of dozen times at import
only.

## What is loaded

Eighteen customer documents, all read from disk, none present as a Python string literal anywhere in
the repository.

| Class | Count | Files |
|---|---:|---|
| Specialist agent prompts | 8 | `<domain>_agent_prompt.txt` for the eight fraud domains |
| Master synthesizer prompt | 1 | `master_agent_prompt.txt` |
| Per-domain orchestration documents | 8 | one per fraud domain |
| Master / Francis orchestration document | 1 | `master_agent_orchestration_062026` |
| **Total under the manifest** | **18** | `prompts/manifest.json` |

Counts and file list come from `prompts/manifest.json` (18 entries) and
`tests/test_prompt_fidelity.py::test_all_eighteen_customer_documents_are_packaged`.

A fresh clone cannot import prompts, and that is intended. Every one of those files is marked
confidential by the customer, so both her originals under `docs/` and the working copies under
`prompts/verbatim/` are **git-ignored**. A clean checkout therefore has `prompts/loader.py`,
`prompts/manifest.json` and the tests but no prompt text, and importing `prompts` raises
`PromptIntegrityError` naming the first missing file. The alternative — a fallback to bundled
paraphrased text — is precisely the defect this package exists to prevent. Restoration instructions
are in `prompts/README.md`.

<Callout type="error" title="What this replaced, and why it was dangerous">
The prior revision shipped a module at `agents/prompts.py` — **deleted in commit `9bb569b`, so it is
not in the tree you are reading** — which hand-rewrote all nine prompts. Its own docstring conceded
the position: *"preserved verbatim in intent."* It also invented an output format the customer never
wrote, `ROLE:` / `RISK:` / `FINDINGS:` lines.

What makes that more than a fidelity complaint is the **parser** reading the invented format: when
it could not find a `RISK:` line, it **defaulted to `LOW RISK`**. Feed that parser the customer's
real prompts — which produce titled prose and never a `RISK:` line — and every application clears. A
fraud detection system whose failure mode is "no fraud found" is worse than one that crashes. Source:
`CHANGELOG.md`, 2026-07-27 entry.
</Callout>

## Manifest contents

`prompts/manifest.json` carries one entry per file with three fields — `sha256`, `bytes` and
`source`. Its own `_comment` states the rule: regenerate **only** when the customer's document
changes.

A sample of the recorded provenance, showing that the two document classes come from different places
(orchestration documents are RTF exports converted to text under `docs/.converted/`):

| Packaged file | Recorded source | bytes |
|---|---|---:|
| `identity_agent_prompt.txt` | `docs/identity_agent_prompt.txt` | 2,412 |
| `master_agent_prompt.txt` | `docs/master_agent_prompt.txt` | 5,067 |
| `bustout_agent_prompt.txt` | `docs/bustout_agent_prompt.txt` | 4,048 |
| `rings_agent_prompt.txt` | `docs/rings_agent_prompt.txt` | 3,758 |
| `identity_orchestration_062026.txt` | `docs/.converted/identity_orchestration_062026.txt` | 17,929 |
| `master_agent_orchestration_062026.txt` | `docs/.converted/master_agent_orchestration_062026.txt` | 2,638 |

Sizes are the manifest's own `bytes` field, which `test_manifest_covers_exactly_the_packaged_files`
asserts against `stat().st_size`.

## Five mutation attempts, all caught

A guard never observed to fire is an assumption. Five deliberate mutations were run against these
layers and every one was caught. Recorded in `prompts/README.md` (*"Five mutation attempts,
including that one, were verified to fail"*) and `CLAUDE.md`.

| Attempt | Caught by | Pinned as |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Edit a packaged prompt in place | Layer 2, the sha256 check at import. Error names the file, the expected hash and the found hash | `test_loader_raises_naming_the_file_on_drift` — which rewrites the persona sentence into `ROLE: Identity Fraud Detection Agent`, mutating the file *toward* the old paraphrased format |
| 2. Edit the prompt **and** regenerate the manifest to cover it | **Layer 3.** Regenerating the manifest makes the hash consistent with the *edit*, not with the customer; the `docs/` byte diff has no dependency on the manifest | `test_verbatim_copy_is_byte_identical_to_docs_source`. The most important of the five, and why the CI diff exists as a separate layer |
| 3. Delete a packaged prompt | `read_verbatim`, which raises before hashing and names both the missing file and the `docs/` path to re-copy from | `test_loader_raises_on_missing_file`. Prevents a silent fallback to substituted text |
| 4. Rename a persona identifier — `ISAAC_IDENTITY` to something plausible | `loader._verify_agent_names()` at import, cross-checking all eight persona ids against the `# <NAME> Agent Prompt` header line inside her own file | `test_loader_rejects_a_persona_id_absent_from_the_customer_text`, monkeypatching in `IVAN_IDENTITY`. These ids are the `RESULT_TABLE.AGENT_NAME` values downstream queries filter on |
| 5. Upper-case Francis to `FRANCIS`, which looks like tidying up | An explicit import-time guard. Her only all-caps spelling appears inside `RETRO_FRANCIS`, the separate retro system Francis must deflect to, so an all-caps id collides with the one name Francis must never answer as | `test_loader_rejects_an_uppercased_francis`, plus a test asserting every `FRANCIS` token in her orchestration document belongs to `RETRO_FRANCIS` |

The header those persona ids come from is stripped before the prompt is used, so
`_verify_agent_names()` is the only place the two representations can be reconciled at all.

## What the loader is allowed to change

Exactly two things, both mechanical, both tested.

1. **Strip the extraction banner** — the confidentiality line, the `====` rule lines and the
   `# <NAME> Agent Prompt` / `# Extracted from ...` comment lines the extraction tool prepended. The
   match anchors to the top of the file and stops at the first real content line, so an interior `#`
   is never touched. `test_stripping_removes_only_the_header` asserts that **every non-header line of
   the source survives, in order, unchanged**.
2. **De-indent the YAML block scalars** in the orchestration documents — remove the longest
   whitespace prefix common to all non-blank lines, and nothing else.

<Callout type="info" title="Irregular whitespace in the customer's documents is content, not formatting">
Orchestration documents are RTF exports of Snowflake Cortex Agent YAML, so they are not valid YAML:
key indentation is inconsistent (0, 1 and 2 spaces all occur) and block bodies are sometimes less
indented than their key. A real YAML parser rejects several of them.

More to the point, `straw_orchestration_instructions_062026` contains doubled and tripled spaces
inside its rule text, and `test_straw_doc_keeps_the_customers_doubled_spaces` pins them. Tests flex
— matching loosely on whitespace — rather than the prompt being normalised. Filename inconsistency
is preserved the same way: her names are variously `..._orchestration_062026`,
`..._orchestration_instructions_062026` and `bustout_fraudbot_...`, and the loader carries that table
rather than regularising it.
</Callout>

## What is pinned beyond the hashes

A sha256 proves the bytes did not move. It says nothing about whether the load-bearing strings
survived the banner strip and reached the model, so 215 tests in `tests/test_prompt_fidelity.py`
assert the contract strings individually.

| Pinned | Count | Note |
|---|---:|---|
| Persona sentences | 8 | One per specialist, asserted to be **line 1** of the loaded prompt |
| Exact output titles | 6 | The six agents whose prompts define one — see [Output contracts](/docs/fidelity/adjudication-contract) |
| The two "return only …" rules | 2 | bustout and rings; **both spellings pinned separately** |
| Calibration bands per specialist | 8 | `LOW RISK` / `POSSIBLE RISK` / `HIGH RISK` |
| The 4,000-character cap | 6 | Asserted present on six and **absent on bustout and rings** |
| The `_context` validation rule | 8 | The rule the cross-family study later measured compliance with |
| Master prompt placeholders | 8 | In the customer's input order, under bare uppercase headings |
| Master JSON keys | 21 | Read out of the prompt's own `REQUIRED JSON FORMAT` block |
| Francis's behavioural rules | — | Identity rule, RETRO deflection, the 2-tool budget, the response shape, the routing table |

`tests/test_prompt_fidelity.py` collects **215 tests**
(`MOCK_MODE=1 python3 -m pytest tests/test_prompt_fidelity.py -q --collect-only`).

Two details worth showing her directly. **bustout and rings differ from each other by one word** —
one says *"the final analysis"*, the other *"final analysis"* — and both spellings are pinned
separately (`test_return_only_wording_differs_between_bustout_and_rings`), which is also why
`agents/output_contract.py` derives each agent's rules from *its own* prompt text rather than
matching one string across all eight. And **a customer-source quirk is pinned rather than fixed**:
the `General Rules` heading is absent from the employment document even though the rules themselves
are present, so `test_general_rules_heading_is_absent_from_the_employment_doc` records that and
nobody later "corrects" the copy.

## Anti-regression canary

Three tests exist so the invented format cannot come back, and one of them exists so the other two
are not vacuous.

- `test_invented_marker_regex_actually_matches_the_old_format` feeds the regex a synthetic sample of
  the *old* `ROLE:` / `RISK:` / `FINDINGS:` format and asserts it matches all three markers. Without
  it, an over-narrow regex would make the absence assertions below pass on any input.
- `test_no_loaded_prompt_uses_the_invented_format` asserts no loaded prompt contains those markers.
- `test_risk_colon_appears_only_as_a_calibration_band_label` is the subtle one. `RISK:` **is** legal
  in her text, but only as `LOW RISK:`, `POSSIBLE RISK:`, `HIGH RISK:`, `MEDIUM RISK:` or
  `OVERALL RISK:`. Every occurrence is checked for one of those prefixes, so a bare `RISK:` marker
  anywhere fails.

## Where fidelity ends and interpretation begins

Two honest limits, stated because the engineer will look for them.

**Two derived names are not the customer's strings.** `AGENT_NAMES` holds ten entries; eight are
literal customer strings quoted from her file headers, two are not, and the loader records the
distinction in place so nobody quotes them back to her as hers. `MASTER_FRAUD_AGENT` is this port's
identifier, derived by underscore-joining the words of her master prompt header, and
`_verify_agent_names()` pins that derivation rather than trusting it. `Francis` is her exact casing
from the master orchestration document, deliberately not upper-cased for the reason in mutation 5.

**The General Rules are extracted from one document of eight.** All 29 numbered rules are read from
the **identity** orchestration document, which carries the longest form of them. The other seven
differ: rule 6 is absent from some, and rules 11, 13, 17, 18, 20 and 24 are worded differently or
truncated per agent. `signal_layer.rules._source.variants_of()` reports the differences and
`test_rule_variants_across_documents` pins them, so a re-import surfaces any change. Source:
`docs/general_rules_coverage.md`, "Known gaps". Detail: [General Rules
coverage](/docs/fidelity/general-rules).

## How to verify this yourself

```bash
# with the customer's documents restored under docs/ and prompts/verbatim/
MOCK_MODE=1 python3 -m pytest tests/test_prompt_fidelity.py -q     # 215 tests

# the integrity check on its own, outside pytest
python3 -c "from prompts.loader import verify_integrity; verify_integrity()"
```

`verify_integrity()` walks every manifest entry and raises on the first drift. It is the same code
path the package's own import runs.
