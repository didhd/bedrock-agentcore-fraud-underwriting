*For the customer's engineer. This page reports a divergence between what two of her prompts ask for and what models actually do, and it deliberately does not repair the prose. Rewriting would hide the divergence, and the divergence is a decision for the prompt's author.*

**24 of 24 bustout and rings analyses opened with a title their prompt never defines, in 15 distinct
spellings, on all three Claude tiers.** GPT-5.6 invents none, on 36 of 36 analyses. Sources:
`evals/results/tier_quality.json:per_analysis` (48 analyses, $4.9088 spend) and
`evals/results/set_comparison.json:per_analysis` (120 analyses, $16.8869 spend).

## The 15 spellings

Every string below is the real opening line of a measured analysis, extracted from the
`invented_title` findings in `evals/results/tier_quality.json`. None is an example.

| Count | Opening line |
|---:|---|
| 4 | `# Fraud Ring Risk Analysis` |
| 3 | `# BUSTOUT FRAUD RISK ANALYSIS` |
| 2 | `# FRAUD RING DETECTION ANALYSIS` |
| 2 | `# Bustout Fraud Risk Analysis` |
| 2 | `# BUST-OUT FRAUD RISK ANALYSIS` |
| 2 | `# Fraud Ring Detection Analysis` |
| 1 | `# Bust-Out Fraud Risk Analysis` |
| 1 | `# RINGS FRAUD RISK ANALYSIS` |
| 1 | `# FRAUD RINGS RISK ANALYSIS` |
| 1 | `## Fraud Ring Risk Analysis: HIGH RISK` |
| 1 | `## Fraud Ring Risk Analysis — POSSIBLE RISK` |
| 1 | `## Bustout Fraud Risk Analysis — Nadia Kowalski` |
| 1 | `# Fraud Ring Risk Analysis — Application (Devon Cross / hash_ssn_ff02aa)` |
| 1 | `# Fraud Ring Risk Analysis — Application (Callum Reyes / hash_ssn_9e4a26)` |
| 1 | `## Fraud Ring Risk Analysis — Application (hash_ssn_5c8b17)` |

Eleven of the fifteen are generic; **four embed application-specific data** — a borrower name, a
hashed SSN, or both.

Spread matters more than the count. A consistent invented title would be cosmetic: strip one string in
the display layer and move on. Fifteen strings, at two markdown levels (`#` on 20 of 24, `##` on 4),
with inconsistent hyphenation of "Bust-Out" and three different nouns for the same domain ("Fraud
Ring", "Fraud Rings", "Rings"), is not something a fixed-string filter removes. It is also a
**contract divergence in the persisted output**: whatever consumes `rings_summary` downstream is
receiving a heading its schema never specified, in a shape that changes between runs.

Two of the fifteen carry a **risk label**, three embed a **hashed SSN**, and one a borrower's full
name — and none of that is a rule violation for these two agents, which is the awkward part.
`agents/output_contract.py` reports the risk-label rule only for the four agents whose prompts state
it, so a risk label in a rings heading violates nothing she wrote. What it is, is a divergence from
the intent of the other four prompts applied to an agent whose prompt happens to be silent. Reporting
it as a violation would fabricate a rule; not mentioning it would hide a real inconsistency. So the
module records the identifier-in-heading case explicitly as *"(observation, not a stated prompt
rule)"* and notes that it is **not** a Rule 23 breach — the SSN value is hashed, never raw — but that
it does put an identifier in a title.

<Callout type="info" title="Where the 'eleven spellings' figure in the code comes from">
`agents/output_contract.py`'s docstring says *"eleven different spellings"*. That is the count of the
**generic** strings, i.e. the fifteen distinct opening lines minus the four varying only because they
embed an application's own name or hashed SSN. Both numbers are correct readings of the same 24
analyses: **15 is the number of distinct opening lines**, 11 the number of distinct title *forms*. If a
slide needs one number, use 15 and say what it counts.
</Callout>

## GPT invents none

The cross-family study swept the same two agents across five models and re-ran the same contract
checker. Source: `evals/results/set_comparison.json:per_analysis`, 120 analyses of which 60 are
bustout or rings.

| Model | Set | bustout+rings analyses | Invented a title | Verdict |
|---|---|---:|---:|---|
| Claude Opus 5 | claude | 12 | **12** | Every analysis |
| Claude Sonnet 5 | claude | 12 | **11** | All but one |
| GPT-5.6 Luna | gpt | 12 | **0** | Clean |
| GPT-5.6 Terra | gpt | 12 | **0** | Clean |
| GPT-5.6 Sol | gpt | 12 | **0** | Clean |

Across all four swept domains and all 120 analyses, output-contract cleanliness was **Claude 17 of
48, GPT 72 of 72**. All 23 invented titles in that study belong to the two Claude models.

<Callout type="error" title="Model-sensitive, and the fix is still the prompt">
This is one of the few findings in the engagement a model swap genuinely does fix. It is still not a
reason to swap, and the reason is on the [cross-family
study](/docs/model-selection/cross-family-study) page: the specialists are held on Claude on
`_context` grounding — GPT 0.357 against Claude 0.642, lower on 4 of 4 agents, deterministic, no judge
involved.

So the recommended fix remains a sentence in the customer's two prompts, not a family change. It is
the counterpart to the missing character cap, and both are [hers to
decide](/docs/open-questions#5-two-of-her-prompts-carry-no-character-cap).
</Callout>

## Deriving the rules corrected a wrong assumption

Most useful thing this sweep produced was not the title count. It was catching a wrong assumption in
the checker itself.

An earlier revision of `agents/output_contract.py` declared the rule set as a hard-coded table and
assumed the risk-label rule applied to the same **six** agents as the title, the character cap, the
emoji ban and the alert-number ban. It does not. Reading the prompt text found that sentence in
**four** — identity, dealer, straw, employment — while `income` and `synthetic` never received it.
Confirmed independently from a second artifact:
`evals/results/tier_quality.json:contract_scope` records `no_risk_label_in_headers` with `count: 4`
and lists exactly those four, while every other shared rule carries `count: 6`.

Enforcing the six-agent contract on all eight would have reported violations **the customer never
asked for** — two agents flagged for something their prompts permit. On a fidelity review that is the
same class of error as missing a rule: it means the checker is not reading her document.

The fix was structural rather than a corrected constant. `_derive_rules(domain)` reads each rule out
of that domain's own verbatim prompt text at import:

| Rule | Derived by looking for | Agents it applies to |
|---|---|---:|
| `title` | whether `TITLES[domain]` is not `None` | 6 |
| `char_cap` | `"max 4000 characters"` | 6 |
| `no_emoji` | `"no emojis"` | 6 |
| `no_alert_numbers` | `"not alert numbers"` | 6 |
| `no_risk_labels_in_headers` | `"risk classification labels"` | **4** |

So a customer prompt update changes the contract automatically instead of leaving the table stale, and
the same mechanism is what makes the four-versus-six question answerable at all.
`tests/test_output_contract.py` collects **25 tests**, including one asserting that `RULES["bustout"]`
and `RULES["rings"]` have **every** value false, and one asserting that `output_contract.TITLES` and
`fanout.ANALYSIS_TITLES` agree.

## Model-sensitive versus not

Two defects landed on these two agents. They look similar and they are not.

| | Invented titles | The calibration failures |
|---|---|---|
| Affects | bustout, rings | APP-1014 rings, APP-1004 bustout |
| Rate | 24 of 24 Claude analyses; 23 of 23 in the cross-family study | 0 of 5 models correct, each |
| Model-sensitive? | **Yes** — GPT-5.6 invents none, on 36 of 36 analyses | **No** — wrong on all five models across both families |
| Same mistake each time? | No — 15 spellings | **Yes** — every model wrong the same way |
| Fix | a title line in the prompt, or accept and strip in display | an operational counting rule in the prompt |
| Measured to work? | The GPT result is measured; the prompt fix is **unmeasured** | **Unmeasured** |

Detail on the second column: [Open
questions](/docs/open-questions#3-the-calibration-failures-no-model-choice-fixes). Both prompts
already state the principle in the exact terms of the failure, which is why the recommendation is to
state it as a counting rule rather than as guidance.

## Two prompts with no character cap [#the-two-prompts-with-no-character-cap]

Same two agents, same root cause — an output specification consisting of one sentence. Measured over
n=334 specialist calls in `evals/results/tail_analysis.json`:

| | Domains | n calls | Output tokens p50 | Analysis chars p50 | Over 4,000 chars |
|---|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Prompt caps at 4,000 chars | identity, dealer, straw, employment, income, synthetic | 251 | **381** | 1,224 | 9 (3.6%) |
| **No cap in prompt** | **bustout, rings** | 83 | **1,331** | **3,519** | **25 (30.1%)** |

3.5x the output tokens and 2.9x the characters. Since wall clock is linear in output tokens at
r²=0.9531 over n=332 calls, these two agents are the structural owner of the fan-out floor, and
`rings` alone was the slowest specialist in **25 of 42** pipeline runs.

They are also the **only** two agents ever measured to truncate:
`evals/results/tail_analysis.json:live_cap_sweep` records `MaxTokensReachedException` on both at caps
of 1024, 1536 and 2048, non-monotonically.

Reaching for `max_tokens` instead of the prompt was measured and failed. Over 30 live calls: no
latency gain (5 of 10 comparable cells faster than control, 5 slower, mean delta −0.23 s), **14 of 30
cells truncated**, and only **13 of 30** kept the same band. A truncated call spends the full wall
clock, is fully billed, and returns no analysis, so the fraud dimension is lost. [The
measurement](/docs/performance/the-tail#the-max-tokens-fix-was-measured-and-it-failed).

## How to reproduce the check

```bash
MOCK_MODE=1 python3 -m pytest tests/test_output_contract.py -q     # 25 tests

python3 -c "
from agents.output_contract import RULES, TITLES, check
print(RULES['rings'])                       # every value False
print(TITLES['bustout'], TITLES['rings'])   # None None
print(check('rings', '# Fraud Ring Risk Analysis\n\nBody.').violations)
"
```

`check()` reports; it never repairs. `strip_invented_title()` exists for a display surface that wants
to honour the contract the prompt states, and the violation must still be recorded — because what the
customer is owed here is the finding, not a tidied output.

## Still unknown

- **Whether adding a title line to those two prompts stops the invention.** Unmeasured; her artifact
  to change.
- **Whether adding the character-cap sentence shortens their output.** Also unmeasured, and the
  larger of the two effects.
- **Whether her downstream consumer already tolerates the heading.** If `rings_summary` is rendered
  rather than parsed, this is cosmetic; if it is parsed, it is not.
