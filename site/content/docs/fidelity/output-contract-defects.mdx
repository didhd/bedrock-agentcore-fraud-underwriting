<Callout type="info" title="Audience">
This page is for the customer's engineer. It reports a divergence between what two of her
prompts ask for and what models actually do, and it deliberately does **not** repair the prose.
Rewriting would hide the divergence, and the divergence is a decision for the prompt's author.
</Callout>

## The finding

`bustout_agent_prompt.txt` and `rings_agent_prompt.txt` define **no title**. They end with
*"Return only the final analysis."* and *"Return only final analysis."* respectively — one word
apart — and that is the entire output specification. No title, no character cap, no emoji ban,
no ban on citing alert numbers, no ban on risk labels in headers. The other six prompts define
all five.

Every single analysis those two agents produced in the tier study opened with a title anyway.

| | Value | Source |
|---|---:|---|
| bustout + rings analyses in the tier study | **24** | `evals/results/tier_quality.json:per_analysis` |
| That invented a title | **24 (100%)** | same, `contract_findings` |
| Distinct title strings observed | **15** | same, derived from the finding details |
| Distinct after normalising case, markdown level and suffix | **6** | same |
| Models this held across | **3 of 3** — Haiku 4.5, Sonnet 5, Opus 5 | `run.candidate_models` |

The per-cell split: rings 15 analyses (5 per model), bustout 9 (3 per model). Zero clean.

Eleven of the fifteen are generic; **four embed application-specific data** — a borrower name,

<Callout type="info" title="Where the 'eleven spellings' figure in the code comes from">
`agents/output_contract.py`'s docstring says *"eleven different spellings"*. That is the count
of the **generic** strings, i.e. the fifteen distinct opening lines minus the four that vary
only because they embed an application's own name or hashed SSN. Both numbers are correct
readings of the same 24 analyses; **15 is the number of distinct opening lines** and 11 is the
number of distinct title *forms*. If a slide needs one number, use 15 and say what it counts.
</Callout>

<Callout type="warn" title="Why the spread matters more than the count">
A consistent invented title would be a cosmetic problem: strip one string in the display layer
and move on. Fifteen strings, at three markdown levels (`#` on 20 of 24, `##` on 4), with
inconsistent hyphenation of "Bust-Out" and three different nouns for the same domain ("Fraud
Ring", "Fraud Rings", "Rings"), is not something a fixed-string filter removes.

It is also a **contract divergence in the persisted output**: whatever consumes
`rings_summary` downstream is receiving a heading its schema never specified, in a shape that
changes between runs.
</Callout>

## Two of these titles break rules their own prompt does not state

Two carry a **risk label** — `Fraud Ring Risk Analysis: HIGH RISK` and
`Fraud Ring Risk Analysis — POSSIBLE RISK`. Three embed a **hashed SSN**, and one a borrower's
full name.

<Callout type="info" title="Neither is a rule violation for these two agents, and that is the awkward part">
`agents/output_contract.py` reports the risk-label rule only for the four agents whose prompts
state it — identity, dealer, straw, employment. rings and bustout are unconstrained, so a risk
label in a rings heading is **not** a violation of anything she wrote.

What it is, is a divergence from the intent of the other four prompts, applied to an agent
whose prompt happens to be silent. Reporting it as a violation would fabricate a rule; not
mentioning it would hide a real inconsistency in the output. So the module records the
identifier-in-heading case explicitly as *"(observation, not a stated prompt rule)"* and notes
that it is **not** a Rule 23 breach — the SSN value is hashed, never raw — but that it does put
an identifier in a title.
</Callout>

## GPT invents none

The cross-family study swept the same two agents across five models and re-ran the same
contract checker. Source: `evals/results/set_comparison.json:per_analysis`, 120 analyses of
which 60 are bustout or rings.

| Model | Set | bustout+rings analyses | Invented a title |
|---|---|---:|---:|
| Claude Opus 5 | claude | 12 | **12** |
| Claude Sonnet 5 | claude | 12 | **11** |
| GPT-5.6 Luna | gpt | 12 | **0** |
| GPT-5.6 Terra | gpt | 12 | **0** |
| GPT-5.6 Sol | gpt | 12 | **0** |

Across all four swept domains and all 120 analyses, output-contract cleanliness was
**Claude 17 of 48, GPT 72 of 72**. All 23 invented titles in that study belong to the two Claude
models.

<Callout type="error" title="Model-sensitive, and the fix is still the prompt">
This is one of the few findings in the engagement that a model swap genuinely does fix. It is
still not a reason to swap, and the reason is on the
[cross-family study page](/docs/model-selection/cross-family-study): the specialists are held
on Claude on `_context` grounding — GPT 0.357 against Claude 0.642, lower on 4 of 4 agents,
deterministic, no judge involved.

So the recommended fix remains a sentence in the customer's two prompts, not a family change.
It is the counterpart to the missing character cap, and both are
[hers to decide](/docs/open-questions#5-two-of-her-prompts-carry-no-character-cap).
</Callout>

## The correction: the rules are derived, not declared

The most useful thing this sweep produced was not the title count. It was catching a wrong
assumption in the checker itself.

<Callout type="warn" title="The 'no risk labels in headers' rule is in FOUR prompts, not six">
An earlier revision of `agents/output_contract.py` declared the rule set as a hard-coded table
and assumed the risk-label rule applied to the same **six** agents as the title, the character
cap, the emoji ban and the alert-number ban.

It does not. Reading the prompt text found the sentence in **four** — identity, dealer, straw,
employment. `income` and `synthetic` never received it.

Confirmed independently from a second artifact: `evals/results/tier_quality.json:contract_scope`
records `no_risk_label_in_headers` with `count: 4` and lists exactly those four, while every
other shared rule carries `count: 6`.

Enforcing the six-agent contract on all eight would have reported violations **the customer
never asked for** — two agents flagged for something their prompts permit. On a fidelity review
that is the same class of error as missing a rule: it means the checker is not reading her
document.
</Callout>

The fix was structural rather than a corrected constant. `_derive_rules(domain)` reads each
rule out of that domain's own verbatim prompt text at import:

| Rule | Derived by looking for |
|---|---|
| `title` | whether `TITLES[domain]` is not `None` |
| `char_cap` | `"max 4000 characters"` |
| `no_emoji` | `"no emojis"` |
| `no_alert_numbers` | `"not alert numbers"` |
| `no_risk_labels_in_headers` | `"risk classification labels"` |

So a customer prompt update changes the contract automatically instead of leaving the table
stale, and the same mechanism is what makes the four-versus-six question answerable at all.
`tests/test_output_contract.py` collects **25 tests**, including one asserting that
`RULES["bustout"]` and `RULES["rings"]` have **every** value false, and one asserting that
`output_contract.TITLES` and `fanout.ANALYSIS_TITLES` agree.

## What is model-sensitive and what is not

The engagement produced two defects on these two agents. They look similar and they are not.

| | Invented titles | The calibration failures |
|---|---|---|
| Affects | bustout, rings | APP-1014 rings, APP-1004 bustout |
| Rate | 24 of 24 Claude analyses; 23 of 23 in the cross-family study | 0 of 5 models correct, each |
| Model-sensitive? | **Yes** — GPT-5.6 invents none, on 36 of 36 analyses | **No** — wrong on all five models across both families |
| Same mistake each time? | No — 15 spellings | **Yes** — every model wrong the same way |
| Fix | a title line in the prompt, or accept and strip in display | an operational counting rule in the prompt |
| Measured to work? | The GPT result is measured; the prompt fix is **unmeasured** | **Unmeasured** |

Detail on the second column: [Open
questions](/docs/open-questions#3-the-calibration-failures-no-model-choice-fixes). Both
prompts already state the principle in the exact terms of the failure, which is why the
recommendation is to state it as a counting rule rather than as guidance.

## The two prompts with no character cap

The same two agents, the same root cause — an output specification consisting of one sentence.
Measured over n=334 specialist calls in `evals/results/tail_analysis.json`:

| | Domains | n calls | Output tokens p50 | Analysis chars p50 | Over 4,000 chars |
|---|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Prompt caps at 4,000 chars | identity, dealer, straw, employment, income, synthetic | 251 | **381** | 1,224 | 9 (3.6%) |
| **No cap in prompt** | **bustout, rings** | 83 | **1,331** | **3,519** | **25 (30.1%)** |

3.5x the output tokens and 2.9x the characters. Since wall clock is linear in output tokens at
r²=0.9531 over n=332 calls, these two agents are the structural owner of the fan-out floor, and
`rings` alone was the slowest specialist in **25 of 42** pipeline runs.

They are also the **only** two agents ever measured to truncate:
`evals/results/tail_analysis.json:live_cap_sweep` records `MaxTokensReachedException` on both at
caps of 1024, 1536 and 2048, non-monotonically.

<Callout type="error" title="Do not reach for max_tokens instead of the prompt">
Measured, 30 live calls: no latency gain (5 of 10 comparable cells faster than control, 5
slower, mean delta −0.23 s), **14 of 30 cells truncated**, and only **13 of 30** kept the same
band. A truncated call spends the full wall clock, is fully billed, and returns no analysis —
so the fraud dimension is lost. [The measurement](/docs/performance/the-tail#the-max-tokens-fix-was-measured-and-it-failed).
</Callout>

## How to reproduce the check

```bash
MOCK_MODE=1 python3 -m pytest tests/test_output_contract.py -q     # 25 tests

python3 -c "
from agents.output_contract import RULES, TITLES, check
print(RULES['rings'])                       # every value False
print(TITLES['bustout'], TITLES['rings'])   # None None
print(check('rings', '# Fraud Ring Risk Analysis\n\nBody.').violations)
"
```

`check()` reports; it never repairs. `strip_invented_title()` exists for a display surface that
wants to honour the contract the prompt states, and the violation must still be recorded —
because what the customer is owed here is the finding, not a tidied output.
