*For the customer's engineer. Every count here is read from `signal_layer/alert_categories.py` at import. The alert IDs themselves are her numbers from General Rule 3 and are shown, because the routing they drive is the thing being verified; her surrounding prose is not reproduced.*

**Ten categories, 166 list entries, 148 distinct IDs, 18 of them in more than one category.** The gap
between 166 and 148 is the whole finding: **the categories are not a partition**, so
`category_of(alert_id)` returns a list and the reverse index is many-to-many by construction.

## Why category routing decides what a specialist can see

<Mermaid
  title="Alerts reach a specialist only through category filtering"
  chart={`
flowchart LR
  A["alerts_fired<br/>on the record"] --> R3["General Rule 3<br/>10 categories<br/>166 entries"]
  R3 --> DX["DOMAIN_EXTRA_ALERTS<br/>from each agent's own doc"]
  DX --> DV["DomainView<br/>this agent's alerts only"]
  DV --> AG["specialist prompt<br/>cannot cite alert NUMBERS"]
`}
/>

A separate rule makes that filtering load-bearing: **all eight specialists are forbidden from citing
alert numbers** — *"Reference specific findings from alerts, not alert numbers."* An agent that cannot
name an alert number cannot reason its way to the right subset, so the only way a specialist ever sees
the correct alerts is for the signal layer to filter them **by category before the payload is
built**.

Quoting the module's own docstring on what a transcription error costs: *"A single wrong digit here
silently removes a detection or attributes it to the wrong specialist."* No error, no warning — just a
fraud dimension the specialist was never shown. The prior revision of this repository did not carry
these IDs at all; the whole rule set existed as six prose display strings.

ID order inside each tuple is the customer's, not sorted. Her Employment list is deliberately not

the ordering carries her grouping intent, while membership tests go through `CATEGORY_ALERTS`, which
is order-free.

Eighteen IDs in total, ten of them the Employment / Employment Deviation overlap.

<Callout type="error" title="A single-label mapping loses a category for 18 IDs">
Quoting `docs/general_rules_coverage.md` on rule 3: *"a single-label mapping would silently lose a
category for 18 ids."*

Concretely: build a `dict[int, str]` from these lists and alert **164** stops being a Bust Out Risk
alert, so the bustout specialist never sees it. Alert **97** stops being a Collateral alert. Alerts
116 and 117 stop being Default alerts. That is why `category_of(alert_id)` returns a list.
</Callout>

## The 31-39 disregard rule

Immediately after the Income list, General Rule 3 carves out the range **31–39**: she rates those nine
ids as weak fraud indicators and tells the agent it can set them aside for the most part. Her exact
sentence is held in code as `DISREGARD_RULE` — extracted from the orchestration document rather than
retyped — with `DISREGARDED_ALERTS = frozenset(range(31, 40))`.

None of 31–39 appears in any of the ten categories, and that is verified:
`DISREGARDED_ALERTS & ALL_CATEGORISED_ALERTS` is **empty**. So this is not an instruction to strip
nine IDs out of a category; it is an instruction about **uncategorised** alerts arriving in
`alerts_fired`. The implementation consequence, recorded in the module, is that they **must not be
dropped from the record** but **must not drive risk** — deleting them would be a different rule from
the one she wrote, since hers says "for the most part", which is interpretive, and a hard delete is
not.

`category_of()` returns `[]` for any ID she never categorised. In the 1–169 range that is **21 IDs**:
the nine covered by the disregard rule, plus **12 others** — 1, 2, 108, 109, 118, 121, 122, 124, 125,
126, 127, 149. Those twelve are uncategorised in her rule and are carried on the unfiltered record
rather than guessed into a category.

## Which specialist sees which categories

Only **five** of the eight fraud domains have a same-named category in General Rule 3. For the other
three the mapping is derived, and `PROVENANCE` records for every domain whether the mapping is
**DOC-QUOTED** from a customer document or **INFERRED** — so she can correct the inferred ones without
archaeology.

| Domain | Categories | Extra IDs from its own doc | Total IDs visible | Provenance | Reasoning offered |
|---|---|---:|---:|---|---|
| identity | Identity | 4 | 60 | **DOC-QUOTED** | — |
| dealer | Dealer Risk | 0 | 9 | **DOC-QUOTED** | — |
| straw | Straw Borrower | 0 | 10 | **DOC-QUOTED** | — |
| employment | Employment + Employment Deviation | 12 | 23 | **DOC-QUOTED** | — |
| income | Income | 0 | 12 | **DOC-QUOTED** | — |
| synthetic | Identity | 18 | 62 | **INFERRED** | Her synthetic document names its own supplemental list of 18 IDs, and **16 of those 18 belong to the Identity-related set** (the other two, 138 and 163, are Default). Identity is the closest customer-defined container |
| bustout | Bust Out Risk + Default | 0 | 35 | **INFERRED** for Default | She defines bust-out as coordinated rapid credit acquisition followed by non-repayment and instructs the agent to look at EPD / EPD6 / FPD defaults. `Default` is the only place those live |
| rings | Identity | 4 | 60 | **INFERRED** | Her rings document names fraud-ring alerts 64, 65, 66 and 67, and **all four belong to the Identity-related set** |

Totals are `len(alerts_for_domain(domain))`, computed from the module. Extra IDs come from
`DOMAIN_EXTRA_ALERTS` — supplemental IDs named directly in that domain's own orchestration document,
outside General Rule 3, each carried alongside the verbatim quote that named it in
`DOMAIN_EXTRA_ALERT_QUOTES` so the numbers are auditable. Each inferred row is tagged `INFERRED` in
`PROVENANCE` with an explicit "Confirm with the customer", and those three rows are the ones to put in
front of her.

Two categories are deliberately assigned to no domain. `Vehicle Risk` and `Collateral` sit in
`UNASSIGNED_CATEGORIES` because no customer document routes them to any of the eight specialists, so
they are not injected into a specialist's view on a guess. They stay reachable through
`CATEGORY_ALERTS` and are carried on the unfiltered record. The alternative — attaching them to
whichever specialist seemed closest — would put nine alert IDs in front of an agent whose prompt has
no interpretation for them, which is the same class of error as [handing a specialist a signal its
prompt never mentions](/docs/what-was-built#the-signal-registry).

## Provenance of the transcription

IDs were transcribed character by character from
`docs/.converted/identity_orchestration_062026.txt` lines 72–93, and the identical lists were
cross-checked against **the other seven** converted orchestration documents — bustout, dealer,
employment, income, rings, straw, synthetic. All eight agree exactly, **including ID order**. The
master orchestration document contains no General Rules at all.

`alerts_for_domain()` raises `KeyError` for an unknown domain rather than returning an empty set,
because a typo'd domain name must not silently produce a specialist with zero alerts.

## Verifying it

```bash
MOCK_MODE=1 python3 -m pytest tests/test_rules.py -q -k alert_categories
```

`test_rule_3_alert_categories` is the coverage entry for this module in
`docs/general_rules_coverage.md`, and separate tests assert that `MULTI_CATEGORY_ALERTS` agrees with
the docstring list and that the 31–39 set is disjoint from every category.

## Still unknown

- **The three inferred mappings.** synthetic → Identity, rings → Identity, and bustout → Default are
  reasoned, not quoted. Confirmation is a one-line answer from her.
- **Where `Vehicle Risk` and `Collateral` belong.** Nine IDs across two categories are routed to no
  specialist because no document routes them.
- **Whether the 12 uncategorised IDs outside 31–39 are intentional.** They are carried unfiltered
  rather than guessed.
