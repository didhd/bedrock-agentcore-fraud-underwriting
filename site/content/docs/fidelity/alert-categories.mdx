<Callout type="info" title="Audience">
This page is for the customer's engineer. Every count here is read from
`signal_layer/alert_categories.py` at import. The alert IDs themselves are the customer's
numbers from General Rule 3 and are shown, because the routing they drive is the thing being
verified; her surrounding prose is not reproduced.
</Callout>

## Why this is data and not a paragraph

General Rule 3 groups the alert IDs into ten named categories. It is the single largest piece
of pure data in the customer's rule set, and it is the mechanism by which each specialist gets
the *right* alerts.

That matters more than it looks, because of a separate rule: **all eight specialists are
forbidden from citing alert numbers** — *"Reference specific findings from alerts, not alert
numbers."* An agent that cannot name an alert number cannot reason its way to the right subset.
So the only way a specialist ever sees the correct alerts is for the signal layer to filter
them **by category before the payload is built**.

<Callout type="warn" title="What a single wrong digit costs">
Quoting the module's own docstring: *"A single wrong digit here silently removes a detection or
attributes it to the wrong specialist."* No error, no warning — just a fraud dimension the
specialist was never shown.

The prior revision of this repository did not carry these IDs at all. The whole rule set
existed as six prose display strings.
</Callout>

|---:|---|
| 40 | Income + Default |
| 42 | Income + Default |
| 68, 69, 70, 71, 73, 74, 75, 77, 79 | Employment + Employment Deviation |
| 89 | Income + Default |

| 117 | Employment + Default |
| 164 | Income + Bust Out Risk |

That is 18 IDs in total (ten of them the Employment / Employment Deviation overlap).

<Callout type="error" title="A single-label mapping loses a category for 18 IDs">
This is the reason `category_of(alert_id)` returns a **list**, and the reason the reverse index
is many-to-many by construction. Quoting `docs/general_rules_coverage.md` on rule 3:
*"a single-label mapping would silently lose a category for 18 ids."*

Concretely: build a `dict[int, str]` from these lists and alert **164** stops being a Bust Out
Risk alert — the bustout specialist never sees it. Alert **97** stops being a Collateral alert.
Alerts 116 and 117 stop being Default alerts.
</Callout>

## The 31-39 disregard rule

Immediately after the Income list, General Rule 3 carves out the range **31–39**: she rates
those nine ids as weak fraud indicators and tells the agent it can set them aside for the most
part. Her exact sentence is held in code as `DISREGARD_RULE` — extracted from the orchestration
document rather than retyped — with `DISREGARDED_ALERTS = frozenset(range(31, 40))`.

<Callout type="warn" title="None of 31-39 appears in any of the ten categories — verified">
`DISREGARDED_ALERTS & ALL_CATEGORISED_ALERTS` is **empty**. So this is not an instruction to
strip nine IDs out of a category; it is an instruction about **uncategorised** alerts that
arrive in `alerts_fired`.

The implementation consequence, recorded in the module: they **must not be dropped from the
record**, but they **must not drive risk**. Deleting them would be a different rule from the one
she wrote — hers says "for the most part", which is interpretive, and a hard delete is not.
</Callout>

`category_of()` returns `[]` for any ID the customer never categorised. In the 1-169 range that
is **21 IDs**: the nine covered by the disregard rule, plus **12 others** — 1, 2, 108, 109,
118, 121, 122, 124, 125, 126, 127, 149. Those twelve are uncategorised in her rule and are
carried on the unfiltered record rather than guessed into a category.

## Which specialist sees which categories

Only **five** of the eight fraud domains have a same-named category in General Rule 3. For the
other three the mapping is derived, and `PROVENANCE` records for every domain whether the
mapping is **DOC-QUOTED** from a customer document or **INFERRED** — so the customer can correct
the inferred ones without archaeology.

| Domain | Categories | Extra IDs from its own doc | Total IDs visible | Provenance |
|---|---|---:|---:|---|
| identity | Identity | 4 | 60 | **DOC-QUOTED** |
| dealer | Dealer Risk | 0 | 9 | **DOC-QUOTED** |
| straw | Straw Borrower | 0 | 10 | **DOC-QUOTED** |
| employment | Employment + Employment Deviation | 12 | 23 | **DOC-QUOTED** |
| income | Income | 0 | 12 | **DOC-QUOTED** |
| synthetic | Identity | 18 | 62 | **INFERRED** |
| bustout | Bust Out Risk + Default | 0 | 35 | **INFERRED** for Default |
| rings | Identity | 4 | 60 | **INFERRED** |

Totals are `len(alerts_for_domain(domain))`, computed from the module. Extra IDs come from
`DOMAIN_EXTRA_ALERTS` — supplemental IDs named directly in that domain's own orchestration
document, outside General Rule 3, each carried alongside the verbatim quote that named it in
`DOMAIN_EXTRA_ALERT_QUOTES` so the numbers are auditable.

<Callout type="warn" title="The three inferred mappings, and the reasoning offered for each">
These are the rows to put in front of the customer, because General Rule 3 defines no
Synthetic, Rings or Default-for-bustout mapping. The module states the reasoning and asks for
confirmation rather than presenting a guess as a contract:

- **synthetic → Identity.** Her synthetic orchestration document names its own supplemental
  list of 18 IDs, and **16 of those 18 are members of the Identity-related set** (the other
  two, 138 and 163, are Default). Identity is therefore the closest customer-defined container.
- **rings → Identity.** Her rings document names fraud-ring alerts 64, 65, 66 and 67, and **all
  four are members of the Identity-related set.**
- **bustout → Default** (in addition to Bust Out Risk, which is doc-quoted). She defines
  bust-out as coordinated rapid acquisition of credit followed by non-repayment, and instructs
  the bustout agent to look at EPD / EPD6 / FPD defaults. The `Default` category is the only
  place those live.

Each row is tagged `INFERRED` in `PROVENANCE` with an explicit "Confirm with the customer."
</Callout>

<Callout type="info" title="Two categories are deliberately assigned to no domain">
`Vehicle Risk` and `Collateral` are in `UNASSIGNED_CATEGORIES`. No customer document routes
them to any of the eight specialists, so they are **not** injected into a specialist's view on a
guess. They stay reachable through `CATEGORY_ALERTS` and are carried on the unfiltered record.

The alternative — attaching them to whichever specialist seemed closest — would put nine alert
IDs in front of an agent whose prompt has no interpretation for them, which is the same class
of error as [handing a specialist a signal its prompt never
mentions](/docs/what-was-built#the-signal-registry).
</Callout>

## Provenance of the transcription

The IDs were transcribed character-by-character from
`docs/.converted/identity_orchestration_062026.txt` lines 72-93, and the identical lists were
cross-checked against **the other seven** converted orchestration documents — bustout, dealer,
employment, income, rings, straw, synthetic. All eight agree exactly, **including ID order**.
The master orchestration document contains no General Rules at all.

`alerts_for_domain()` raises `KeyError` for an unknown domain rather than returning an empty
set, because a typo'd domain name must not silently produce a specialist with zero alerts.

## Verifying it

```bash
MOCK_MODE=1 python3 -m pytest tests/test_rules.py -q -k alert_categories
```

`test_rule_3_alert_categories` is the coverage entry for this module in
`docs/general_rules_coverage.md`, and separate tests assert that `MULTI_CATEGORY_ALERTS` agrees
with the docstring list and that the 31-39 set is disjoint from every category.
