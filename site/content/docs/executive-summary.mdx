## The bottom line

The eight-specialist forensic underwriting pipeline runs end to end on Amazon Bedrock in
**31.45 seconds at p50 and 46.55 seconds at p95**, and **40 of 42 measured runs finished
under the 60-second target**. That is a live measurement over 42 adjudications and 336
specialist model calls, not a projection.

<Callout type="warn" title="What is proven and what is not">
**Proven:** sub-minute forensic underwriting on Bedrock, at fidelity to the customer's own
prompts and output contracts, with the 21-key adjudication object validating on
[all 42 runs](/docs/measured-results).

**Not proven:** that our cost is lower than the incumbent's. That is not a measurement gap
on our side — it is one missing input from the customer. See
[the volume question](#the-one-question-that-decides-the-cost-case).
</Callout>

## What was measured

Source for every figure in this section: `evals/results/pipeline_14x3_us-east-1.json`,
run `2026-07-28T07:42:42+00:00` in `us-east-1`, git `9bb569bac801`. 14 applications x 3
repetitions = **42 live adjudications**, **336 specialist calls**, **$6.4659 of measured
Bedrock spend**.

| | n | p50 | p95 | max |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| **End to end** | 42 | **31.45 s** | **46.55 s** | 74.97 s |
| Eight specialists in parallel (fan-out) | 42 | 24.93 s | 41.05 s | 70.62 s |
| Master synthesis | 42 | 4.20 s | 5.59 s | 15.21 s |
| The same eight run sequentially (counterfactual) | 42 | 85.62 s | 157.14 s | 173.00 s |

**Cost per adjudication:** $0.1177 min, **$0.1440 p50**, $0.2536 max (n=41 fully priced
runs; the one degraded run is excluded from cost and retained in latency).

**Parallel speed-up** over the sequential counterfactual: 1.85x min, **3.64x p50**, 5.11x
max (n=42). It is reported as a distribution rather than a single number because a fan-out
is bounded by its slowest member, so the ratio moves with which specialist ran long.

<Callout type="warn" title="No p99 appears anywhere on this site">
The benchmark harness refuses a p99 below 100 samples and a p95 below 20. With n=42 it
recorded the refusal in the artifact verbatim: *"refused: 42 sample(s), need >= 100. A p99
over 42 sample(s) is not a p99."* Nothing here converts that refusal into a number.
</Callout>

## What the customer's engineer will check first, and what she will find

Fidelity is the product here, not a detail. The customer's engineer wrote all nine
production agents.

| Fidelity claim | Status |
|---|---|
| Her nine prompts | **Byte-identical**, sha256-verified at import, CI-diffed against source. Five mutation attempts, including regenerating the manifest, were all caught. |
| The adjudication object | **Exactly 21 keys in her order.** Validated on 42 of 42 runs, including the degraded one. |
| Her 29 numbered General Rules | **27 enforced as tested code**, 2 enforced by the verbatim prompt text. |
| The ten alert-ID categories | Data, not prose: 166 list entries, 148 distinct IDs, 18 multi-category. |
| Bust-out and rings output shape | Her prompts define **no title** for those two, and the port does not invent one. |

Detail: [Prompt fidelity](/docs/fidelity/prompt-fidelity) and
[the adjudication contract](/docs/fidelity/adjudication-contract).

<Callout type="error" title="What the port replaced">
The prior code in this repository **paraphrased all nine prompts** and invented a
`RISK:` / `FINDINGS:` output format. Its parser defaulted to `LOW RISK` whenever it could
not find its invented `RISK:` line — so feeding it the real prompts would have cleared
every application silently. That is the single defect a fidelity review would have found
first, and it is gone.
</Callout>

## The one question that decides the cost case

The customer reports a projected **$700K–$1M** annual Snowflake AI spend. Our measured cost
is **$0.1440 per adjudication** (p50, n=41). Annualising that requires one number nobody has
given us yet: **applications per day through the full eight-agent forensic path.**

| Applications/day through full 8-agent underwriting | Annualised at measured p50 |
|---|---|
| ~3,000 | **~$158K** |
| ~300,000 | **~$15.8M** |

Arithmetic: volume x 365 x $0.14395847. The p50 is measured; the volumes are illustrative
brackets, not the customer's figures.

Same architecture, opposite conclusion. **This is the most important open item in the
engagement** and it cannot be closed from our side. Detail and the exact question to ask:
[the Snowflake comparison](/docs/cost/snowflake-comparison).

<Callout type="warn" title="One thing to keep off a cost slide">
Per-agent model tiering — using a cheaper model for cheaper work — **saves $0.00
structurally**. Haiku-to-Sonnet is a $2/$10-per-million-token step down and
Sonnet-to-Opus is the identical step up, so two light agents fund exactly two heavy agents
at equal token counts. A test asserts that arithmetic so the claim cannot come back. The
real levers are [on this page](/docs/cost/levers); tiering is not one of them.
</Callout>

## Where the model choices came from

Two blind studies were run, both with measured spend recorded in the artifact.

- **Claude-only tier study** — 48 blind-judged analyses, judge Claude Opus 5,
  **$4.9088 measured**. `evals/results/tier_quality.json`.
- **Cross-family study** — 4 agents x 6 applications x 5 models = **120 blind-judged
  analyses, 240 verdicts, two judges** (Claude Opus 5 and GPT-5.5), **$16.8869 measured**.
  `evals/results/set_comparison.json`.

Three results worth an executive's attention:

1. **The specialists stay on Claude, and it was a decision rather than a default.** GPT-5.6
   measured faster and cheaper. It was held on one deterministic metric — how often the
   analysis validates a signal against the paired `_context` column its own prompt requires:
   Claude **0.642**, GPT **0.357**, lower on 4 of 4 agents. No judge involved.
   [Detail](/docs/model-selection/cross-family-study).
2. **The synthesizer is not Claude, also on measurement.** On the same eight narratives,
   Claude Sonnet 5 took 55.2 s and GPT-5.6 Luna took 6.1 s for the same verdict — the
   substitution that brings end-to-end under a minute. n=1 each on that comparison; the
   42-run figure for Luna in the pipeline is 4.20 s p50. [Detail](/docs/model-selection/synthesizer).
3. **Measurement overturned a reasoning-based tier assignment on two agents.** Reading the
   prompts said fraud rings and synthetic identity needed the largest model. Measurement
   contradicted it on both. [Detail](/docs/model-selection/tier-rederivation).

## Risks, stated plainly

| Risk | Severity | Status |
|---|---|---|
| The volume figure that decides the cost comparison is unknown | **Blocking the cost case** | Customer input required |
| Two fixtures are mis-banded by **every model in both families** | High — it is a prompt defect, not a model choice | Fix is a sentence in her prompts; unmeasured |
| Her `master_agent_prompt.txt` and her process-guide PDF disagree on the mid-band enum | Medium — anything keying off the band string is wrong for one of them | Surfaced, not silently resolved |
| `full_underwriting_signal_dictionary.xlsx` never received | Medium | The ~60-signal registry is reconstructed from the nine prompts |
| A ~0.6%-per-call server-side generation stall produced the two runs over 60 s | Medium | Irreducible; no client-side change removes it |

All four, with the exact questions to ask: [Open questions](/docs/open-questions).

## The Friday recommendation

<Callout type="info" title="Recommended">
**Demo on an application with a measured-stable latency profile.** Three of the fourteen
fixtures ran three repetitions within a sub-two-second spread:
**APP-1010** (28.0 / 28.4 / 28.6 s), **APP-1002** (22.3 / 22.9 / 23.2 s) and
**APP-1011** (21.2 / 22.4 / 22.8 s). Against those, APP-1007 ran 23.5 / 34.6 / **75.0** s
and APP-1001 ran 18.9 / 24.2 / **70.2** s — spreads over 51 seconds on byte-identical
input. Source: `evals/results/tail_analysis.json`.

Stated with its limit: **n=3 per application makes this a low-variance observation, not a
guarantee.** The stall rate is per *call* and a run makes eight of them.
</Callout>

Also recommended for Friday:

1. **Lead with the fidelity result, not the latency result.** The customer's engineer is the
   gate, and byte-identical prompts plus a 21-of-21-key contract on 42 of 42 runs is the
   strongest thing in the engagement.
2. **Raise the two calibration failures before she finds them.** APP-1014 rings and
   APP-1004 bustout are mis-banded by all five models across both families, every one wrong
   the same way. Presenting it as a shared prompt defect we diagnosed lands very differently
   from her discovering it in the demo. [Detail](/docs/open-questions#3-the-calibration-failures-no-model-choice-fixes).
3. **Ask the volume question in the room.** It is one number and it converts the cost slide
   from unresolvable to decided.
4. **Do not promise a p99 or a throughput figure.** Neither was measured. Applications were
   run one at a time, so nothing in this engagement says what happens when N adjudications
   overlap.

## What was explicitly not measured

Carried from the artifact's own `caveats` array, so this is the benchmark's own list rather
than ours:

- **AgentCore Runtime overhead.** The benchmarked calls went to Bedrock directly from the
  host recorded in `run.host`, so container cold start, session setup and SSE framing are
  **not** in the end-to-end figures.
- **Signal generation.** Precomputed and outside the timed window, matching the customer's
  own architecture. The end-to-end figure is agent latency, not pipeline-including-ETL
  latency.
- **Throughput.** One application in flight at a time.
- **Any p99, anywhere.**
