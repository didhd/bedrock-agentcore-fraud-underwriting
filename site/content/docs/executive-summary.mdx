*For the executive and account audience. Every figure names the artifact it came from; the engineering detail behind each claim is one link away.*

Sub-minute forensic underwriting on Amazon Bedrock is **proven**. A lower cost than the
incumbent is **not** — and that gap is one missing customer input, not a missing measurement.

The eight-specialist pipeline runs end to end in **31.45 seconds at p50 and 46.55 seconds at
p95**, with **40 of 42 measured runs under the 60-second target**. Live measurement over 42
adjudications and 336 specialist model calls, not a projection.

## Proven versus unproven

| Claim | Status | Evidence |
|---|---|---|

<Callout type="error" title="What the port replaced">
Prior code in this repository **paraphrased all nine prompts** and invented a
`RISK:` / `FINDINGS:` output format. Its parser defaulted to `LOW RISK` whenever it could not

gone.

## One question decides the cost case

AnyCompany reports a projected **$700K–$1M** annual Snowflake AI spend. Measured cost
here is **$0.1440 per adjudication** (p50, n=41). Annualising needs one number nobody has
given us: **applications per day through the full eight-agent forensic path.**

| Applications/day through full 8-agent underwriting | Annualised at measured p50 |
|---|---|
| ~3,000 | **~$158K** |
| ~300,000 | **~$15.8M** |

Arithmetic: volume x 365 x $0.14395847. The p50 is measured; the volumes are illustrative
brackets, not the customer's figures.

Same architecture, opposite conclusion. **This is the most important open item in the
engagement** and it cannot be closed from our side. Detail and the exact question to ask:
[Snowflake comparison](/docs/cost/snowflake-comparison).

One thing to keep off a cost slide: **per-agent model tiering saves $0.00 structurally.**
Haiku-to-Sonnet is a $2/$10-per-million-token step down and Sonnet-to-Opus is the identical
step up, so two light agents fund exactly two heavy agents at equal token counts. A test
asserts that arithmetic so the claim cannot come back. Real levers are on
[cost levers](/docs/cost/levers); tiering is not one of them.

## Where the model choices came from

Two blind studies, both with measured spend recorded in the artifact.

| Study | Scope | Judges | Measured spend | Artifact |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Claude-only tier study | 48 blind-judged analyses | Claude Opus 5 | **$4.9088** | `evals/results/tier_quality.json` |
| Cross-family study | 4 agents x 6 applications x 5 models = **120 analyses, 240 verdicts** | Claude Opus 5 and GPT-5.5 | **$16.8869** | `evals/results/set_comparison.json` |

Three results deserve executive attention.

1. **Specialists stay on Claude, and that was a decision rather than a default.** GPT-5.6
   measured faster and cheaper. It was held on one deterministic metric — how often an
   analysis validates a signal against the paired `_context` column its own prompt requires:
   Claude **0.642**, GPT **0.357**, lower on 4 of 4 agents. No judge involved.
   [Detail](/docs/model-selection/cross-family-study).
2. **Synthesizer selection also went to measurement, and Claude lost it.** On the same eight
   narratives, Claude Sonnet 5 took 55.2 s and GPT-5.6 Luna took 6.1 s for the same verdict —
   the substitution that brings end-to-end under a minute. n=1 each on that comparison; the
   42-run figure for Luna in the pipeline is 4.20 s p50.
   [Detail](/docs/model-selection/synthesizer).
3. **Measurement overturned a reasoning-based tier assignment on two agents.** Reading the
   prompts said fraud rings and synthetic identity needed the largest model. Measurement
   contradicted that on both. [Detail](/docs/model-selection/tier-rederivation).

## Risks, stated plainly

| Risk | Severity | Status |
|---|---|---|
| Volume figure that decides the cost comparison is unknown | **Blocking the cost case** | Customer input required |
| Two fixtures are mis-banded by **every model in both families** | High — a prompt defect, not a model choice | Fix is a sentence in her prompts; unmeasured |
| `master_agent_prompt.txt` and her process-guide PDF disagree on the mid-band enum | Medium — anything keying off the band string is wrong for one of them | Surfaced, not silently resolved |
| `full_underwriting_signal_dictionary.xlsx` never received | Medium | The 60-signal registry is reconstructed from the nine prompts |
| A ~0.6%-per-call server-side generation stall produced the two runs over 60 s | Medium | Irreducible; no client-side change removes it |

All of them, with the exact questions to ask: [Open questions](/docs/open-questions).

## Friday recommendation

**Demo on an application with a measured-stable latency profile.** Three of the fourteen
fixtures ran three repetitions within a sub-two-second spread — shown sorted ascending:
**APP-1010** (28.0 / 28.4 / 28.6 s), **APP-1002** (22.3 / 22.9 / 23.2 s) and **APP-1011**
(21.2 / 22.4 / 22.8 s). Against those, APP-1007 ran 23.5 / 34.6 / **75.0** s and APP-1001 ran
18.9 / 24.3 / **70.2** s — spreads over 51 seconds on byte-identical input. Source:
`evals/results/tail_analysis.json`. Stated with its limit: **n=3 per application makes this a
low-variance observation, not a guarantee**, because the stall rate is per *call* and a run
makes eight of them.

Four more things for the room:

1. **Lead with fidelity, not latency.** The customer's engineer is the gate, and byte-identical
   prompts plus a 21-of-21-key contract on 42 of 42 runs is the strongest thing here.
2. **Raise the two calibration failures before she finds them.** APP-1014 rings and APP-1004
   bustout are mis-banded by all five models across both families, every one wrong the same
   way. Presenting it as a shared prompt defect we diagnosed lands very differently from her
   discovering it live.
   [Detail](/docs/open-questions#3-the-calibration-failures-no-model-choice-fixes).
3. **Ask the volume question in the room.** One number converts the cost slide from
   unresolvable to decided.
4. **Promise no p99 and no throughput figure.** Neither was measured. Applications ran one at a
   time, so nothing here says what happens when N adjudications overlap.

## Explicitly not measured

Carried from the artifact's own `caveats` array, so this is the benchmark's list rather than
ours.

- **AgentCore Runtime overhead.** Benchmarked calls went to Bedrock directly from the host
  recorded in `run.host`, so container cold start, session setup and SSE framing are **not** in
  the end-to-end figures.
- **Signal generation.** Precomputed and outside the timed window, matching the customer's own
  architecture. End to end here is agent latency, not pipeline-including-ETL latency.
- **Throughput.** One application in flight at a time.
- **Any p99, anywhere.**
