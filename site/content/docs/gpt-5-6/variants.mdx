*For whoever has to pick a variant. Every cell is measured; nothing is inferred from the price list. n=6 per cell, which is this repo's floor for a band claim rather than a comfortable margin.*

**On this task, price does not predict quality.** Luna is the cheapest of the three and scored
highest on band agreement (20 of 24 against Sol's 15); Sol costs 5x Luna's output rate and scored
lowest. That is a surprising sentence, so the rest of this page is mostly the mechanism and the
caveats — because "the expensive model is worse" is the kind of claim that is usually a measurement
error.

## Why that is not as strange as it sounds

Three things explain it, and none of them is "Sol is a weaker model".

**1. Price here is a rate card, not a quality tier.** Sol's premium buys reasoning capacity, and
this task barely uses it. The specialist prompts are explicit instruction sets — apply these
numbered rules to this payload, in this format — not open problems. Output volume is nearly
identical across the three (306 median output tokens for Sol against 348 for Luna), so the 5x cost
is almost entirely the per-token rate:

| Variant | Output $/MTok | Median output tokens | Output-only cost | Measured median cost/call |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| `luna` | 6.60 | 348 | $0.00230 | **$0.0048** |
| `terra` | 16.50 | 256 | $0.00423 | **$0.0107** |
| `sol` | 33.00 | 306 | $0.01010 | **$0.0232** |

Sol writes **fewer** sentences than Luna (7.5 median against 10.0) and charges 5x per token for
them. There is no volume story here at all.

**2. More reasoning pushes toward escalation on this task, and escalation is usually wrong here.**
Nine of the fourteen `LOW RISK` fixtures in the study are anti-false-positive cases — records built
to *look* bad and resolve LOW anyway. A model that reasons harder about suspicious-looking signals
finds more to say about them. Sol escalated **7 of 24** analyses against Luna's 3, and
over-escalated **4 of 14** `LOW RISK` fixtures against Luna's 2. Its errors are directional, not
random.

**3. It is also wrong in the other direction.** On `identity`/APP-1014 (pinned `POSSIBLE RISK`) Sol
declared `LOW RISK` with grounding **0.0** — it cited none of the seven signals in its view against
their `_context` evidence. So this is not simple over-caution that a threshold could correct; the
calibration is loose in both directions.

<Callout type="warn" title="What this does and does not license">
It licenses: **do not pick a GPT-5.6 variant by price for this workload.** Test it.

It does not license "Sol is a bad model" or "reasoning effort hurts". Sol is the **judge** in
`evals/judge_sol.py` precisely because it measured the highest reasoning effort of the three, and
judging is exactly the open-ended task that rewards it. The finding is about *fit*, on 24 analyses
per variant.
</Callout>

## Band agreement across all five models

Sol being last is only meaningful next to the others, so here is every model in the study over its
own 24 analyses. Note that **Luna leads the field**, above both Claude incumbents, on band alone:

| Model | Correct band | Escalated | Over-escalated on a `LOW RISK` fixture |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| **`luna`** | **20 / 24** | 3 | 2 / 14 |
| `opus-5` | 19 / 24 | 4 | 3 / 14 |
| `terra` | 18 / 24 | 4 | 3 / 14 |
| `sonnet-5` | 17 / 24 | 4 | 2 / 14 |
| `sol` | **15 / 24** | **7** | **4 / 14** |

The spread from 15 to 20 over 24 analyses is **five cases**. That is a real ordering on this sample
and a thin one: one differently-judged application moves any model by a full point. It is reported
as a ranking on 24 analyses, not as a general capability claim — and it is *not* what the
specialist decision rests on. That rests on `_context` grounding, where every GPT variant including
Luna sits below both Claude incumbents.
[Cross-family study](/docs/model-selection/cross-family-study).

Sol's `identity` cell is the worst single result in the study and worth reading in full, because it
shows all three failure directions at once over six applications:

| Application | Pinned | Sol declared | Grounding | Reading |
|---|---|---|---:|---|
| APP-1004 | `HIGH RISK` | `HIGH RISK` | 0.571 | Correct, and its best-grounded analysis |
| APP-1009 | `LOW RISK` | `POSSIBLE RISK` | 0.143 | **Over-escalated an anti-FP fixture** |
| APP-1010 | `LOW RISK` | `POSSIBLE RISK` | 0.143 | **Over-escalated an anti-FP fixture** |
| APP-1012 | `POSSIBLE RISK` | `HIGH RISK` | 0.429 | Over-escalated |
| APP-1013 | `LOW RISK` | `LOW RISK` | 0.143 | Correct band, band read from a lead sentence |
| APP-1014 | `POSSIBLE RISK` | `LOW RISK` | **0.0** | **Under-escalated, citing no `_context` at all** |

Two of six correct, and the two anti-false-positive fixtures both escalated — the only cell in the
study to do that. APP-1014 is the reason this is not fixable by tightening a threshold: the same
model that over-escalates two benign records clears a genuinely risky one while grounding nothing.

Two readings worth taking from that grid.

**Every GPT cell has zero contract violations.** All 72 analyses, across all three variants. The
Claude incumbents recorded 34. Whatever else the comparison decided, the invented-title defect is
a Claude-side problem. [Output-contract defects](/docs/fidelity/output-contract-defects).

**Luna's weakness is specific, not general.** It is the strongest variant on three agents and
collapses on `synthetic` alone — 0.1500 grounding, less than a third of Opus's 0.517 on the same
payloads. A variant-per-agent assignment is therefore arguable on this data; it is not taken
because `synthetic`'s incumbent is measured and the alternative's n is 6.

## Region matters more than variant choice

Same prompt (31,343 characters), `effort=low`, n=3 per cell, measured 2026-07-28:

| Variant | Region | Total p50 | TTFT p50 | Output tok/s | Output tokens p50 |
|---|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| **luna** | **us-east-1** | **4.61 s** | 0.79 s | 176.0 | 812 |
| luna | us-west-2 | 5.02 s | 1.19 s | 180.0 | 903 |
| luna | us-east-2 | 6.79 s | 1.63 s | 115.8 | 878 |
| terra | us-east-2 | 6.29 s | 0.63 s | 126.4 | 825 |
| terra | us-east-1 | 6.72 s | 0.69 s | 122.3 | 785 |
| terra | us-west-2 | 7.63 s | 0.89 s | 95.1 | 790 |
| sol | us-east-1 | 11.19 s | 4.19 s | 91.1 | 1,020 |
| sol | us-east-2 | **30.66 s** | **22.07 s** | 31.6 | 900 |

Sol in `us-east-2` is **6.6x slower than Luna in `us-east-1`** on identical work. Every cell
returned the same verdict (`HIGH RISK/DECLINE`), so the spread is throughput, not different
answers.

There is **no cross-region inference on this endpoint** — no `global.*` or `us.*` prefix — so the
region is a deployment decision, not an inference-profile one. Sol is served in `us-east-1` and
`us-east-2` only. [Region selection](/docs/model-selection/regions).

<Callout type="error" title="A third-party probe ranks these regions differently, and it is not wrong">
A public latency dashboard put Luna `us-west-2` fastest at 531 ms and `us-east-2` at 2,703 ms —
a different ordering from the table above, which measured `us-east-1` fastest.

Both are correct and they measure different things. A short-prompt probe is dominated by
**time to first token**; a 31,000-character prompt producing 800+ tokens is dominated by
**throughput**. Our workload is the second kind, so our own measurement governs the deployment
and the probe is recorded as context rather than as a tiebreak.
</Callout>

## Cost accounting that differs from Converse

Two details will under-report a GPT bill if missed, both now priced and regression-tested:

| Term | Behaviour | Consequence if ignored |
|---|---|---|
| `cacheWriteInputTokens` | Billed at **1.25x** input on 5.6 (30-min cache), implicit caching **on by default** | A measured Luna synthesis reported 6,740 cache-write tokens = **$0.00927 of a $0.01499 call, 62%** |
| `input_tokens` | Responses API counts the **total** prompt including cached; Converse counts only non-cached | Double-counting cached tokens at the full rate |

GPT-5.4 and GPT-5.5 cache with **no** write charge, so their multiplier is `0.0` — recorded in
`CACHE_WRITE_MULTIPLIER` rather than assumed. `rates_for()` raises on an unpriced model id instead
of defaulting, so a new variant cannot enter a cost-reported path silently.
[What does not reduce cost](/docs/cost/what-does-not-work).

## Still unknown

- **Four agents were never run on any GPT variant** — `dealer`, `straw`, `employment`, `income` —
  and with them three of the five anti-false-positive fixtures.
- **n=6 per cell** on band, and n=3 per cell on latency. A band claim needs 5 by this repo's own
  rule, so these are at the floor rather than comfortably above it.
- **No GPT variant was measured as a specialist in production.** Every specialist figure here comes
  from the study, not from a pipeline run.
