*For anyone asking "is this a Claude system or a mixed one". It is mixed, and both halves of that were decided by measurement.*

**GPT-5.6 Luna runs the master synthesizer in production. GPT-5.6 was tested for all eight
specialists and declined.** Those two sentences are the whole summary, and neither was a
preference: the first replaced a 55.2 s Claude synthesis with a **4.20 s p50 over 42 runs**, and
the second overruled a projected **47.8% cost reduction** on one deterministic compliance
measurement.

The judge row matters more than it looks. The cross-family comparison is scored by **two judges
biased in opposite directions**, one of them OpenAI's, so a finding only counts where both agree.
GPT is therefore in this repo three times: as a shipped component, as a rejected candidate, and as
an instrument.

## What GPT-5.6 won on, undisputed

Pooled across the four swept agents, against the Claude incumbents:

| Axis | Claude | GPT-5.6 | Margin |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| Wall clock p50 | 18.88 s | **4.73 s** | **4.0x faster** |
| Cost per call p50 | $0.03125 | **$0.01066** | **2.9x cheaper** |
| Output-contract violations | 34 | **0** | GPT invents no titles at all |
| Judged substance | — | **higher on BOTH judges** | +0.398 / +0.684 |

The Claude judge scored GPT higher on substance — that is, **against its own family** — which is
much harder to dismiss than a single-judge result. And the length difference is padding rather
than lost evidence: paired on identical payloads, Claude writes 2.95x the characters while naming
1.08x the payload's signals, agreeing on band in 23 of 24 pairs.
[Length versus evidence](/docs/model-selection/length-vs-evidence).

## The one axis that decided against it

<Callout type="warn" title="A compliance measurement, not a quality opinion">
Every specialist prompt requires signals to be validated against their paired `_context` columns.
`grounded_fraction` counts how often that actually happens, deterministically, with **no judge
involved**: GPT **0.357** (n=72) against Claude **0.642** (n=48), lower on **4 of 4** agents. On
`synthetic` it falls to **0.150**.

A model can land the right band without doing the validation her prompt asks for. Band agreement
cannot see that; this can.
</Callout>

Declining it has a price, and the site states it rather than hiding it: moving the four swept
agents to Luna projects **$0.0651 per adjudication against $0.1247 — a 47.8% reduction** — and
would drop the fan-out floor from 22.05 s to 8.09 s. Both are labelled projections of a rejected
counterfactual in `agents/models.py`. [Cost levers](/docs/cost/levers).

## What was never tested on GPT

**Four of the eight specialists — `dealer`, `straw`, `employment`, `income` — never ran on GPT at
all.** Three of the five anti-false-positive fixtures (APP-1006, APP-1007, APP-1008) target
exactly those domains, so the hardest calibration cases have no GPT evidence in either direction.
`income` is also the current latency bottleneck, which makes it the first place a further sweep
would pay.

Nothing here is an argument that GPT-5.6 is worse at fraud analysis. It is an argument that on the
four agents measured, it complied less often with a specific instruction in the customer's own
prompts, and that four agents were not measured at all.
