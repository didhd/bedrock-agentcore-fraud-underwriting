## What the three evidence grades mean

`agents/models.py:evidence_grade` reads the grade out of the rationale text rather than
re-deciding it, so the rendered table and the prose cannot disagree.

<Cards>
  <Card title="MEASUREMENT" description="A measurement on this agent decided the assignment. Latency, cost, the quality verdict and n are all quoted in TIER_RATIONALE." />
  <Card title="HELD" description="A comparison WAS run and deliberately not acted on, because n was too small to move a fraud dimension. The cheaper candidate and what it would save are recorded." />
  <Card title="UNPROVEN" description="No quality comparison exists for this agent at all. The entry says so instead of borrowing a neighbour's result." />
</Cards>

<Callout type="error" title="A latency grade is not a quality grade, and the two are tracked separately">
`dealer` and `straw` are graded **MEASUREMENT** — on n=42 latency and cost — and appear in
`QUALITY_SWEPT_AGENTS` **not at all**. No blind judge has ever compared their analyses
against a larger model. Conflating "we measured it" with "we measured its quality" is how a
cost win gets presented as an accuracy result, so the code keeps two separate structures:

```python
QUALITY_SWEPT_AGENTS = {"rings": 5, "synthetic": 5, "bustout": 3, "identity": 3}
MIN_APPLICATIONS_FOR_A_BAND_CLAIM = 5
```

**Four of the eight specialists have never had their analysis quality compared against
anything.**
</Callout>

## Every rationale, with its n and its artifact

Two artifacts do all the work below:

- `evals/results/tier_quality.json` — 48 blind-judged analyses, judge Claude Opus 5, model
  identifiers stripped and order shuffled under seed `20260728`, scored against each agent's
  own verbatim CALIBRATION text and the fixture's pinned band. Measured spend **$4.9088**
  (specialists $1.3586, judge $3.5501).
- `evals/results/pipeline_14x3_us-east-1.json` — 42 live pipeline runs, 336 specialist
  calls, source of every figure quoted at n=42. Measured spend **$6.46592**.
- `evals/results/set_comparison.json` — the cross-family study, 120 analyses, two judges,
  measured spend **$16.8869**. Covers four agents only.

### `rings` — MID, moved down from HEAVY

| | Opus 5 | Sonnet 5 |
|---|---|---|
| Band agreement vs pinned expectation (n=5) | 4/5 | **4/5** |
| Judge-confirmed false positives | 1 | **1** |
| Judge calibration mean | 4.20 | 4.00 |
| Wall clock p50 | 32.81 s | **22.05 s** |
| $/call p50 | $0.0696 | **$0.0241** |
| Output tokens p50 | 2448 | 2026 |

Opus's only remaining edge is **+0.20 judge calibration**, and the judge is Opus 5 scoring
its own family. On its own 16 rows that judge scored calibration **+0.31** and substance
**+0.78** versus the other 32 while the band-agreement margin it cannot influence moved
**−0.026**. A +0.20 lead inside a +0.31 self-preference margin is refused as
indistinguishable from bias.

Why this row is worth more than any other: `rings` was the slowest agent in **25 of 42**
pipeline runs and the fan-out floor is **96.3%** of fan-out time
(`evals/results/tail_analysis.json`). **Ceiling on the fix, also measured:**
slowest-minus-second-slowest is **6.34 s p50** — even an instantaneous `rings` leaves the
next agent as the floor. [Critical path](/docs/performance/critical-path).

### `synthetic` — MID, moved down from HEAVY, accepting a latency loss

| | Opus 5 | Sonnet 5 | Haiku 4.5 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Band agreement (n=5) | 4/5 | **5/5** | **5/5** |
| Judge calibration mean | 4.20 | **4.80** | 4.40 |
| False negatives | **1** | 0 | 0 |
| Output-contract violations | 5 | 5 | **8** |
| Wall clock p50 | **8.51 s** | 13.54 s | 9.56 s |
| $/call p50 | $0.0293 | $0.0194 | **$0.0059** |

Sonnet 5 is **slower** here — slower on 4 of 5 paired applications — and it is taken anyway,
because Opus produced the sweep's only **false negative**: on APP-1014 it declared LOW RISK
against a pinned POSSIBLE RISK (`SYN-008`) and the judge scored it "dismisses".

Two things this row proves about the table as a whole:

1. **It is not cost-driven.** A cost-driven table takes Haiku here: 5/5 band, $0.0059,
   9.56 s. Haiku was rejected on the customer's own output contract — 8 violations against
   Sonnet's 5, including citing numeric alert IDs on 3 of 5 calls, which
   `synthetic_agent_prompt.txt` forbids.
2. **It is the one cell with no bias caveat available.** The margin runs *against* the
   judge's own family, so self-preference cannot explain it.

### `bustout` and `identity` — HELD at n=3

n=3 cannot support a band claim; one application moves the rate 33 points. On that thin
sample Haiku 4.5 lost on no axis:

| Agent | Incumbent Sonnet 5 | Haiku 4.5 |
|---|---|---|
| `bustout` | band 1/3, calibration 2.67, 2 judge-confirmed false positives, $0.0254, 25.31 s | band 2/3, calibration 2.67, 1 false positive, **$0.0087, 13.65 s** |
| `identity` | band 2/2 readable, calibration 5.00, 2 contract violations, $0.0171, 13.65 s | band 3/3, calibration 4.00, **7 contract violations**, **$0.0051, 8.04 s** |

Moving either would save roughly **$0.017/application** and ~11 s of critical path. It is
not being done on three applications. `identity`'s Haiku rows also carry 7 output-contract
violations against Sonnet's 2 — alert IDs its prompt forbids, plus a risk label in a section
header on all three calls — so the swap has a fidelity cost as well as a statistical one.

`bustout` is the one most worth resolving: at n=42 it is 14.46 s p50 / 35.71 s p95 at
$0.01699 and was the slowest agent in **7 of 42** runs, second only to `rings`.

## The projected cost of this assignment

`agents/models.py:projected_cost_per_adjudication` sums nine per-agent costs, each priced at
the token counts **that model actually produced**. It is a **PROJECTION** and every return
value says so.

|---|---|---|---:|---:|
| `identity` | Sonnet 5 | 3110 / 1105 / 0 | 42 | $0.01727 |
| `dealer` | Haiku 4.5 | 3518 / 379 / 0 | 42 | $0.00541 |
| `straw` | Haiku 4.5 | 2400 / 224 / 0 | 42 | $0.00352 |
| `employment` | Sonnet 5 | 2363 / 527 / 1091 | 42 | $0.01021 |
| `income` | Sonnet 5 | 3184 / 900 / 0 | 42 | $0.01537 |
| `bustout` | Sonnet 5 | 1786 / 1316 / 1297 | 42 | $0.01699 |
| **`synthetic`** | **Sonnet 5** | 3793 / 1518 / 0 | **5** | $0.02276 |
| **`rings`** | **Sonnet 5** | 1621 / 1863 / 705 | **5** | $0.02259 |
| `synthesizer` | GPT-5.6 Luna | 2 / 716 / 0 (+4300 cache write) | 42 | $0.01063 |
| **Projected total** | | | **smallest n = 5** | **$0.12475** |

Specialists $0.11412, synthesizer $0.01063.

<Callout type="info" title="Why token counts cannot be held constant across a model swap">
A different model writes a different amount. On the same five applications, `rings` on Opus
wrote **1568.8** output tokens mean; on Sonnet, **1862.6**. `synthetic` on Opus wrote
**581.5**; on Sonnet, **1517.8**. Pricing one model's rates against the other's token counts
is fiction, so `RECORDED_USAGE_PER_CALL` and
`RECORDED_USAGE_PER_CALL_PRIOR_ASSIGNMENT` each carry their own model's measured tokens.
</Callout>

Against the measured number, with both sides repriced at the same date so the Sonnet 5
promotional rate cannot be charged to one side only:

| Priced as of | This assignment (projected) | Prior assignment (projected) | Delta |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| 2026-07-28 (promo live) | $0.12475 | $0.15571 | **−$0.03096 (−19.9%)** |
| 2026-09-01 (promo lapsed) | $0.17734 | $0.18563 | **−$0.00829 (−4.5%)** |

The prior assignment's own **measured** figures, for reference: **$0.14396 p50 / $0.15616
mean**, n=41 priced runs. The projection is a sum of per-agent means, so it is comparable to
the **mean**, not the p50 — and `projected_cost_per_adjudication` returns that comparison as
a string field rather than leaving it to the reader.

<Callout type="warn" title="Do not put −20% on a slide dated after 2026-09-01">
Sonnet 5's $2/$10 is introductory and reverts to $3/$15. Repricing only the new side while
holding the old side at the fixed measured constant manufactures a fake cost *increase* of
~$0.021. `test_the_post_expiry_comparison_reprices_both_sides_not_just_one` asserts against
exactly that. From September the re-assignment is worth about **4.5%** on cost — it remains
a latency and quality decision. [Cost levers](/docs/cost/levers).
</Callout>

The projection method is validated where a measured answer exists: applying the identical
arithmetic to the prior assignment's own recorded tokens reproduces the benchmark's measured
mean to within **$0.0005**
(`test_the_same_method_reproduces_the_measured_cost_under_the_old_assignment`). The *method*
is sound; only this configuration's end-to-end result is unconfirmed.

## Output caps are a property of the agent, not of the model

Caps moved from `MAX_TOKENS_BY_TIER` to `MAX_TOKENS_BY_AGENT` on 2026-07-28, because under
the old table moving `rings` from HEAVY to MID would silently have changed which cap it
inherited — on one of only two agents ever measured to truncate.

| Agent | Max output observed | Cap | Headroom | Its prompt says |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---|
| `straw` | 989 | 3072 | 3.1x | max 4000 chars |
| `dealer` | 1464 | 3072 | 2.1x | max 4000 chars |
| `employment` | 1647 | 4096 | 2.5x | max 4000 chars |
| `synthetic` | 2737 | 4096 | 1.5x | max 4000 chars |
| `income` | 2783 | 4096 | 1.5x | max 4000 chars |
| `rings` | 2876 | 4096 | 1.4x | **nothing** |
| `bustout` | 3257 | 4096 | 1.3x | **nothing** |
| `identity` | 3582 | 4096 | 1.1x | max 4000 chars |
| synthesizer | 1005 | 4096 | 4.1x | ~1200 chars per summary |

"Max output observed" counts every live call on disk **at the model that agent is now
assigned** — the 42-run benchmark, the tier sweep's Sonnet 5 cells, and the cap sweep. Opus 5
rows are excluded on purpose: including them would size `bustout`'s cap against 3385 tokens
from a model this assignment does not use.

`identity`'s 1.1x is the thinnest margin and is deliberately **not** tightened. Its
3,582-token call carried only **1,191 characters** of analysis, so it is the agent whose
token usage is least predictable from its visible output. A cap computed as 4000/4 = 1024
would truncate it at a third of its own contract.

<Callout type="error" title="No cap was lowered, and the sweep says why">
`max_tokens` was measured as a brevity instrument and **failed all three criteria**: no
latency gain, 14 of 30 cells truncated, 13 of 30 kept the same band. A truncated call is the
worst outcome available — full wall clock, fully billed, text discarded, fraud dimension
lost. [The measurement](/docs/cost/what-does-not-work#max_tokens-failed-all-three-criteria).
</Callout>

## What no assignment fixed

Two calibration failures survive every model in both families, and every model gets them
wrong the same way.

| Fixture / agent | Pinned | Every model said | Requirement |
|---|---|---|---|
| APP-1014 / `rings` | `LOW RISK` | `POSSIBLE RISK` — Opus 5, Sonnet 5, Luna, Terra, Sol. **0 of 5.** | `RNG-020` |
| APP-1004 / `bustout` | `POSSIBLE RISK` | `HIGH RISK` — same five. **0 of 5.** | `BST-020` |

The judges named the same fault every time: alerts arising from **one underlying behaviour**
treated as independent corroboration. That is a prompt-and-guardrail problem, not a
model-selection one, and buying a bigger model does not buy the fix.
[Open questions](/docs/open-questions#3-the-calibration-failures-no-model-choice-fixes).

Also not fixed by any assignment: **the tail.** 61.0% of end-to-end variance is
*within*-application, across repetitions of byte-identical input, and the two runs over 60 s
were single calls generating at 16.1 and 4.7 output tok/s against a 66 tok/s median — inside
Bedrock's own reported latency, zero throttles. [The tail](/docs/performance/the-tail).
