## The mistake, stated first

The first version of the per-agent model table was authored by **reading the customer's
prompts**. The reasoning was:

> `rings_agent_prompt.txt` asks the model to correlate address, phone, email and employer
> reuse across distinct SSNs and decide whether a cluster is coordinated. That is
> multi-signal network reasoning, so give it the heavyweight model.
>
> `synthetic_agent_prompt.txt` asks whether a thin-file identity is fabricated, which
> requires weighing several weak indicators against each other. Heavyweight too.

Both agents went on Claude Opus 5. **On both, measurement contradicted the reading.** Neither
correction is subtle: on `rings` the cheaper model reached the identical band with the
identical false positive, and on `synthetic` the *expensive* model produced the sweep's only
false negative.

<Callout type="error" title="Why this belongs in a customer-facing document">
The reasoning that produced the original assignment is exactly the reasoning a customer will
apply to the next agent. "This dimension is harder, so it needs the bigger model" is
intuitive, it is cheap to say, and it was measurably wrong twice out of two.
</Callout>

The report's own verdict for this cell: **"DEFENSIBLE on this sample:
haiku-4-5-20251001-v1:0, sonnet-5"**.

Opus's only surviving advantage is **+0.20 judge calibration**, and +0.20 sits inside the

Sonnet 5 was taken rather than Haiku, which is the conservative half of the decision: Haiku
lost on no band but scored 0.60 lower on calibration and 1.0 lower on substance, and this is
the agent that owns the critical path.

### Why `rings` was worth more than any other row

| | Value | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Slowest agent in N of 42 runs | **25** | `pipeline_14x3_us-east-1.json` |
| Fan-out floor as a share of fan-out | **96.3%** | `tail_analysis.json` |
| Slowest-minus-second-slowest p50 | **6.34 s** | `tail_analysis.json` |

Fan-out cannot finish before its slowest member, and `rings` was that member in 25 of 42
runs. But the third row is the ceiling on the whole idea: **even an instantaneous `rings`
leaves the next agent as the floor, 6.34 s later.** This is a median improvement, not a tail
fix. [Critical path](/docs/performance/critical-path).

The report's verdict: **"DEFENSIBLE on this sample: haiku-4-5-20251001-v1:0, sonnet-5"**.

The deciding line is in the band-disagreement list:

> `synthetic` / `opus-5` on APP-1014: declared **LOW RISK**, pinned **POSSIBLE RISK**
> (`SYN-008`)

That is a false **negative** on a synthetic-identity dimension — the more dangerous error
direction on a fraud product — and it is the sweep's only one. The judge scored it
"dismisses".

<Callout type="warn" title="This row costs latency and it is taken anyway">
Sonnet 5 is **slower** here: 13.54 s vs 8.51 s p50, and slower on **4 of 5** paired
applications. The trade is stated rather than buried: **roughly 5 s of median latency bought
back a fraud dimension the faster, more expensive model missed.** On a fraud product that is
the right side to err on, and it is the single clearest evidence that this table is not
cost-driven.
</Callout>

Two further observations from this cell:

- **A cost-driven table takes Haiku.** It banded 5/5 at $0.0059 and 9.56 s — cheaper *and*
  faster than the Sonnet now assigned. It was rejected on the customer's own output
  contract: **8 violations against Sonnet's 5**, including citing numeric alert IDs on 3 of 5
  calls, which `synthetic_agent_prompt.txt` forbids.
- **No bias caveat is available here.** The margin runs *against* the judge's own family, so
  self-preference cannot explain a cheaper Claude model beating Opus on calibration.

## The padding finding: extra length is not extra evidence

This is the mechanism behind both moves, and it is the finding to keep. Paired by
(application, domain), so only analyses of the **same payload** are compared. Distinct
evidence items are distinct numbers plus distinct snake_case signal names.

Source: `tier_quality.json → verbosity_vs_substance`.

| Scope | Longer | Shorter | Paired | chars ratio | distinct-evidence ratio | Judge substance delta | Pairs where only the longer one escalates |
|---|---|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| **ALL POOLED** | opus-5 | sonnet-5 | 16 | **1.31x** | **1.60x** | +0.0 | **0** |
| `bustout` | opus-5 | sonnet-5 | 3 | 1.98x | 1.94x | +0 | 0 |
| `identity` | opus-5 | sonnet-5 | 3 | 1.31x | 1.08x | +1 | 0 |
| **`rings`** | opus-5 | sonnet-5 | 5 | **1.76x** | **1.71x** | **+0** | **0** |
| `synthetic` | opus-5 | sonnet-5 | 5 | 0.89x | 1.30x | +1 | 0 |

The `rings` row is the one quoted everywhere else in this engagement: **1.76x the characters
carrying 1.71x the distinct evidence, for a judge substance delta of +0.** The extra 76% of
text bought 71% more evidence items and zero measured substance — i.e. it is length, not
analysis.

<Callout type="info" title="The reading rule, quoted from the artifact">
*"A chars ratio above the distinct-evidence ratio means the extra length is not carrying
proportionally more evidence."*

Pooled, Opus is 1.31x longer than Sonnet for 1.60x the evidence — so **pooled, Opus is
slightly denser**, and the finding is agent-specific rather than global. Pooling four agents
can cancel out the one that motivated the question, which is why the artifact reports both.
</Callout>

### Verbosity does not drift toward over-escalation

The obvious worry about a shorter analysis is that it under-escalates, and the obvious worry
about a longer one is that it talks itself into a higher band. Neither happened: the last
column is **0 in every scope, in all three pairings** — across 48 paired comparisons, there
is **no** pair where the longer analysis escalated and the shorter one did not.

So the case against Opus on `rings` is cost and latency. It is **not** a false-positive risk
argument, and stating it as one would be unsupported.

### And Haiku is not simply "the short one"

| Scope | Longer | Shorter | Paired | chars ratio | evidence ratio | substance delta |
|---|---|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| ALL POOLED | opus-5 | haiku-4-5 | 16 | 1.0x | 1.17x | **+1.0** |
| ALL POOLED | **haiku-4-5** | sonnet-5 | 16 | **1.33x** | 1.32x | **−1.0** |

Haiku writes **more** characters than Sonnet 5 pooled (1.33x) and scores **1.0 lower** on
substance. Length and quality are close to orthogonal on this workload, which is the reason
neither can substitute for the other in a model decision.

## What the sweep could not settle

`bustout` and `identity` were swept at **n=3**, and the study's own bar for a band claim is
**5** (`MIN_APPLICATIONS_FOR_A_BAND_CLAIM`). Both cells return the same mechanical verdict:

> **n TOO SMALL TO TELL — 3 applications per cell. One case moves the band rate by 33
> points; haiku-4-5-20251001-v1:0 did not lose on any axis here**

That verdict is generated mechanically on purpose. Quoted from `evals/set_comparison.py`,
which shares the machinery: *"the honest answer to several of these questions is 'n is too
small', and that answer must not depend on the author's mood."*

Both were **HELD**. Detail and the money sitting on the unrun eval:
[the assignment](/docs/model-selection/assignment#bustout-and-identity--held-at-n3).

## What the re-derivation changed in the code

Beyond the two model ids:

1. **`MAX_TOKENS_BY_TIER` → `MAX_TOKENS_BY_AGENT`.** Under the old table, `rings` drew its
   cap from `tier_label(model)`, so this very re-assignment would have silently changed the
   truncation risk of one of only two agents ever measured to truncate. Both values happen to
   be 4096 today, so nothing changed in effect — a future move to LIGHT would have dropped it
   to 3072. `test_per_agent_caps_preserve_the_shipped_effective_values` pins that no effective
   cap moved.
2. **`TIER_RATIONALE` entries now open with a grade.** `MEASUREMENT` / `HELD` / `UNPROVEN`,
   and `evidence_grade()` reads the grade out of the text rather than re-deciding, so the
   rendered table cannot disagree with the prose. An entry stating no grade returns
   `UNRECORDED`, which is treated as a defect rather than a fourth category.
3. **`QUALITY_SWEPT_AGENTS` was added**, separately from the grade, because `dealer` and
   `straw` are graded MEASUREMENT on latency and cost and have never had their analysis
   quality compared to anything.

## What the re-derivation did not do

- **It did not re-measure the pipeline.** The 42-run benchmark ran under the *previous*
  assignment. Everything downstream of this page's two moves is a projection until
  `evals.bench --pipeline --repetitions 3` runs again.
- **It did not touch four of the eight agents.** `dealer`, `straw`, `employment` and `income`
  were never in this sweep.
- **It did not fix the two calibration failures**, and no tier does.
- **It offers no new end-to-end p50.** Fan-out is bounded by a per-run *maximum*, which
  per-agent medians cannot reconstruct. What *is* claimable is per-agent and paired: on the
  same five applications `rings` ran **22.05 s p50 on Sonnet 5 against 32.81 s on Opus 5**,
  faster on 3 of 5 paired draws, while `synthetic` ran **13.54 s against 8.51 s** — slower on
  4 of 5. Both figures are medians of five and no p95 is offered for either, because five
  samples do not support one.
