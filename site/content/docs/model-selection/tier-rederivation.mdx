*For the customer's engineer, and for any reviewer asking how much of this model table is reasoning and how much is measurement.*

Reasoning was wrong on both agents it touched. A first version of the per-agent model table was authored by **reading** the customer's prompts, which put `rings` and `synthetic` on Claude Opus 5; a blind sweep of 48 analyses then contradicted the reading on both, and the correction is the point of this page.

## Two moves, and what decided each

Neither correction is subtle. On `rings` the cheaper model reached the identical band with the identical false positive; on `synthetic` the *expensive* model produced the sweep's only false negative.

<Callout type="error" title="Why a wrong first guess belongs in a customer-facing document">
"This dimension is harder, so it needs the bigger model" is the reasoning a customer will apply
to the next agent. It is intuitive, it is cheap to say, and it was measurably wrong twice out of
two.
</Callout>

## `rings`: HEAVY → MID

n=5 applications per cell (APP-1004, APP-1009, APP-1012, APP-1013, APP-1014), paired so only same-payload analyses are compared.

| | Opus 5 | Sonnet 5 | Haiku 4.5 | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Band agreement vs pinned | 4/5 | **4/5** | **4/5** | tie |
| Judge-confirmed false positives | 1 | **1** | **1** | tie |
| Judge calibration mean / min | 4.20 / 2 | 4.00 / 2 | 3.40 / 1 | Opus +0.20, inside the bias floor |
| Judge substance | 4.0 | 4.0 | 3.0 | Haiku loses |
| Output-contract violations | 5 | 5 | 5 | tie |
| Wall clock p50 | 32.81 s | **22.05 s** | 14.65 s | Sonnet −10.76 s |
| $/call p50 | $0.0696 | **$0.0241** | $0.0081 | Sonnet −65% |
| Output tokens p50 | 2448 | 2026 | 1195 | — |

Report's own verdict for this cell: **"DEFENSIBLE on this sample: haiku-4-5-20251001-v1:0, sonnet-5"**.

Opus keeps one surviving advantage, **+0.20 judge calibration**, and +0.20 sits inside the judge's own measured **+0.31** self-preference for its family. The artifact says so in the cell itself: *"that +0.20 lead is smaller than the judge's measured +0.31 self-preference for its own family, so it is not distinguishable from bias."*

Sonnet 5 was taken rather than Haiku, which is the conservative half of the decision: Haiku lost on no band but scored 0.60 lower on calibration and 1.0 lower on substance, and this is the agent that owns the critical path.

### Why `rings` was worth more than any other row

| | Value | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Slowest agent in N of 42 runs | **25** | `pipeline_14x3_us-east-1.json` |
| Fan-out floor as a share of fan-out | **96.3%** | `tail_analysis.json` |
| Slowest-minus-second-slowest p50 | **6.34 s** | `tail_analysis.json` |

Fan-out cannot finish before its slowest member, and `rings` was that member in 25 of 42 runs. Row three is the ceiling on the whole idea: **even an instantaneous `rings` leaves the next agent as the floor, 6.34 s later.** A median improvement, not a tail fix. ## `synthetic`: HEAVY → MID on quality, accepting a latency loss

| | Opus 5 | Sonnet 5 | Haiku 4.5 | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Band agreement (n=5) | 4/5 | **5/5** | **5/5** | Opus loses |
| Judge calibration mean / min | 4.20 / 1 | **4.80 / 4** | 4.40 / 3 | Sonnet best, against the judge's own family |
| Judge substance | 4.8 | 3.8 | 3.4 | Opus best |
| Judge-confirmed false positives | 0 | 0 | 0 | tie |
| **False negatives** | **1** | 0 | 0 | **Opus disqualified** |
| Output-contract violations | 5 | 5 | **8** | Haiku loses |
| Wall clock p50 | **8.51 s** | 13.54 s | 9.56 s | Sonnet +5.03 s, accepted |
| $/call p50 | $0.0293 | $0.0194 | **$0.0059** | — |
| Output tokens p50 | 451 | 1123 | 654 | — |

Report's verdict: **"DEFENSIBLE on this sample: haiku-4-5-20251001-v1:0, sonnet-5"**.

Deciding line sits in the band-disagreement list: `synthetic` on `opus-5`, application APP-1014, declared **LOW RISK** against a pinned **POSSIBLE RISK** (`SYN-008`). That is a false **negative** on a synthetic-identity dimension — the more dangerous error direction on a fraud product — and it is the sweep's only one. The judge scored it "dismisses".

**This row costs latency and is taken anyway.** Sonnet 5 runs 13.54 s against 8.51 s p50 and is slower on 4 of 5 paired applications, so roughly 5 s of median latency bought back a fraud dimension the faster, more expensive model missed. On a fraud product that is the right side to err on, and it is the clearest single evidence that this table is not cost-driven.

Two further observations from the same cell. **A cost-driven table takes Haiku** — it banded 5/5 at $0.0059 and 9.56 s, cheaper *and* faster than the Sonnet now assigned, and was rejected on the customer's own output contract: 8 violations against Sonnet's 5, including citing numeric alert IDs on 3 of 5 calls, which her `synthetic` prompt forbids. And **no bias caveat is available here**, because the margin runs *against* the judge's own family.

## Padding finding: extra length is not extra evidence [#the-padding-finding-extra-length-is-not-extra-evidence]

Mechanism behind both moves, and the finding worth keeping. Paired by (application, domain), so only analyses of the **same payload** are compared. Distinct evidence items are distinct numbers plus distinct snake_case signal names. Source: `tier_quality.json → verbosity_vs_substance`.

| Scope | Longer | Shorter | Paired | chars ratio | distinct-evidence ratio | Judge substance delta | Pairs where only the longer escalates |
|---|---|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| **ALL POOLED** | opus-5 | sonnet-5 | 16 | **1.31x** | **1.60x** | +0.0 | **0** |
| `bustout` | opus-5 | sonnet-5 | 3 | 1.98x | 1.94x | +0 | 0 |
| `identity` | opus-5 | sonnet-5 | 3 | 1.31x | 1.08x | +1 | 0 |
| **`rings`** | opus-5 | sonnet-5 | 5 | **1.76x** | **1.71x** | **+0** | **0** |
| `synthetic` | opus-5 | sonnet-5 | 5 | 0.89x | 1.30x | +1 | 0 |

That `rings` row is the one quoted everywhere else in this engagement: **1.76x the characters carrying 1.71x the distinct evidence, for a judge substance delta of +0.** An extra 76% of text bought 71% more evidence items and zero measured substance — length, not analysis.

Reading rule, quoted from the artifact: *"A chars ratio above the distinct-evidence ratio means the extra length is not carrying proportionally more evidence."* Pooled, Opus is 1.31x longer than Sonnet for 1.60x the evidence — so **pooled, Opus is slightly denser**, and the finding is agent-specific rather than global. Pooling four agents can cancel out the one that motivated the question, which is why the artifact reports both.

### Verbosity does not drift toward over-escalation

An obvious worry about a shorter analysis is that it under-escalates, and about a longer one that it talks itself into a higher band. Neither happened: the last column is **0 in every scope, in all three pairings** — across 48 paired comparisons there is **no** pair where the longer analysis escalated and the shorter one did not.

So the case against Opus on `rings` is cost and latency. It is **not** a false-positive risk argument, and stating it as one would be unsupported.

### Haiku is not simply "the short one"

| Scope | Longer | Shorter | Paired | chars ratio | evidence ratio | substance delta |
|---|---|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| ALL POOLED | opus-5 | haiku-4-5 | 16 | 1.0x | 1.17x | **+1.0** |
| ALL POOLED | **haiku-4-5** | sonnet-5 | 16 | **1.33x** | 1.32x | **−1.0** |

Haiku writes **more** characters than Sonnet 5 pooled (1.33x) and scores **1.0 lower** on substance. Length and quality are close to orthogonal on this workload, which is why neither substitutes for the other in a model decision. [Length versus evidence](/docs/model-selection/length-vs-evidence).

## What the sweep could not settle

`bustout` and `identity` were swept at **n=3**, and the study's own bar for a band claim is **5** (`MIN_APPLICATIONS_FOR_A_BAND_CLAIM`). Both cells return the same mechanical verdict:

> **n TOO SMALL TO TELL — 3 applications per cell. One case moves the band rate by 33 points; haiku-4-5-20251001-v1:0 did not lose on any axis here**

That verdict is generated mechanically on purpose. Quoted from `evals/set_comparison.py`, which shares the machinery: *"the honest answer to several of these questions is 'n is too small', and that answer must not depend on the author's mood."* Both were **HELD**. Detail and the money sitting on the unrun eval: [model assignment](/docs/model-selection/assignment#bustout-and-identity--held-at-n3).

## What the re-derivation changed in the code

Beyond the two model ids:

1. **`MAX_TOKENS_BY_TIER` → `MAX_TOKENS_BY_AGENT`.** Under the old table `rings` drew its cap from `tier_label(model)`, so this very re-assignment would have silently changed the truncation risk of one of only two agents ever measured to truncate. Both values happen to be 4096 today, so nothing changed in effect — a future move to LIGHT would have dropped it to 3072. `test_per_agent_caps_preserve_the_shipped_effective_values` pins that no effective cap moved.
2. **`TIER_RATIONALE` entries now open with a grade.** `MEASUREMENT` / `HELD` / `UNPROVEN`, and `evidence_grade()` reads the grade out of the text rather than re-deciding, so a rendered table cannot disagree with the prose. An entry stating no grade returns `UNRECORDED`, treated as a defect rather than a fourth category.
3. **`QUALITY_SWEPT_AGENTS` was added**, separately from the grade, because `dealer` and `straw` are graded MEASUREMENT on latency and cost and have never had their analysis quality compared to anything.

## Still unknown

| Not done by this re-derivation | Consequence |
|---|---|
| Pipeline was not re-measured | The 42-run benchmark ran under the *previous* assignment; everything downstream of these two moves is a projection until `evals.bench --pipeline --repetitions 3` runs |
| Four of the eight agents untouched | `dealer`, `straw`, `employment` and `income` were never in this sweep |
| Two calibration failures unfixed | No tier fixes either, and neither does the other family |
| No new end-to-end p50 | Fan-out is bounded by a per-run *maximum*, which per-agent medians cannot reconstruct |

What *is* claimable is per-agent and paired: on the same five applications `rings` ran **22.05 s p50 on Sonnet 5 against 32.81 s on Opus 5**, faster on 3 of 5 paired draws, while `synthetic` ran **13.54 s against 8.51 s** — slower on 4 of 5. Both are medians of five and no p95 is offered for either, because five samples do not support one.
