## The conclusion up front

**The eight specialists stay on Claude.** GPT-5.6 measured **4x faster** and **3x cheaper**
pooled, wrote a fifth of the words, and produced **zero** output-contract violations against
Claude's 34. It was held anyway, on one deterministic measurement in which no judge takes
part: the fraction of the signals in view that the analysis validates against its paired
`*_context` evidence.

| | Claude | GPT | Direction |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| `_context` grounding, mean over per-analysis values | **0.642** (n=48) | **0.357** (n=72) | Lower on **4 of 4** agents |

That axis is invisible in the band, the latency and the cost — which is exactly why it
decides. Every specialist prompt requires it; `rings_agent_prompt.txt` says *"You MUST
validate signals using relevant context (columns that end in `_context`)"*.

<Callout type="warn" title="What this study did NOT find">
It did **not** find that GPT-5.6 is worse at the fraud judgement. On band agreement the two
families are within one case of each other on every swept agent except `identity`/Sol — and at
n=6, one case is 17 points. The hold rests on a measured compliance gap plus half the fan-out
being unswept, **not** on a finding that GPT reasons less well.
</Callout>

## The case for GPT, which is strong and undisputed

Pooled over the per-analysis rows (n=48 Claude, n=72 GPT):

| Axis | Claude | GPT | Ratio |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| Wall clock p50 | 18.88 s | **4.73 s** | **4.0x faster** |
| $/call p50 | $0.03125 | **$0.01066** | **2.9x cheaper** |
| Output tokens p50 | 1422.5 | 288.0 | GPT writes a fifth of the words |
| Output-contract violations, total | **34** | **0** | deterministic, `agents.output_contract.check` |
| Output-contract clean rows | 17 / 48 | **72 / 72** | |
| Judged substance margin (GPT − Claude) | — | **+0.398 / +0.684** | **better on BOTH judges** |

The single widest cell: `rings` Luna at **3.201 s / $0.0050** against the Opus 5 incumbent's
**26.2175 s / $0.0583** — **8.2x faster and 11.7x cheaper** for the same band agreement (5/6
each).

<Callout type="error" title="The Claude judge scored GPT higher on substance, against its own family">
`judge_self_preference.per_judge`:

- `global.anthropic.claude-opus-5` (claude family) scored its **own** family **+0.226** on
  calibration and **−0.398** on substance.
- `openai.gpt-5.5` (gpt family) scored its **own** family **−0.239** on calibration and
  **+0.684** on substance.

So on substance, *both* judges preferred GPT — including the one whose family that penalises.
That is much harder to dismiss than a single-judge win, and it is why "GPT writes a fifth of
the words, so it must be thinner" does not survive this study.
</Callout>

## What decided it: `_context` grounding

Mean `grounded_fraction` per (agent, model), computed over the 120 `per_analysis` rows. **No
judge is involved in any cell of this table.**

| Agent | Opus 5 | Sonnet 5 | Luna | Terra | Sol |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| `rings` | 0.875 | 0.792 | 0.625 | 0.625 | 0.583 |
| `identity` | 0.738 | 0.476 | 0.357 | 0.381 | 0.238 |
| `bustout` | 0.833 | 0.524 | 0.286 | 0.310 | 0.215 |
| `synthetic` | 0.517 | 0.383 | **0.150** | 0.350 | 0.167 |
| **Pooled mean** | **Claude 0.642** (n=48) | | **GPT 0.357** (n=72) | | |

<Callout type="info" title="Mean here, median in the verdict cells">
The table above is the **mean** of the six `per_analysis.grounding.grounded_fraction` values in
each cell. The per-cell verdicts in the accordions below quote
`measured.grounded_fraction_median` from the same artifact, and on some cells the two differ —
`identity`/Sonnet 5 is 0.476 mean against 0.429 median, `synthetic`/Opus 5 is 0.517 mean
against 0.550 median. Both are in the file; neither is recomputed into the other. The study's
own disqualification rule ("less than half the incumbent's") is evaluated on the **medians**,
which is what the verdict strings quote.
</Callout>

Three properties make this the deciding axis rather than one more column:

1. **GPT is lower on 4 of 4 agents against both Claude incumbents.** It is not one bad cell
   dragging an average.
2. **It is deterministic.** `grounded_fraction` counts how many of the signals in the
   agent's own domain view appear in the analysis *alongside* their paired `_context`
   evidence. No model scores it.
3. **The customer's prompt states it as a requirement.** A model that meets it less often is
   less compliant with her contract, whatever its band agreement says.

The `synthetic`/Luna cell at **0.150 against Opus's 0.550 median** is below half the incumbent,
which is disqualifying on the study's own mechanical rule — and that is why `synthetic` is the one
agent where Luna is graded "NOT AS GOOD".

`deterministic_cross_check`, GPT minus Claude, computed with no judge at all:

| Metric | Margin |
|---|---:|
| Band agreement | **−0.054** |
| Contract violations | **−0.756** (GPT better) |
| Grounded fraction | **−0.284** |

Quoted from the artifact: *"A judge-scored margin unaccompanied by a deterministic margin in
the same direction is more likely preference than quality."* Here the judge-scored substance
margin (+0.398 / +0.684 for GPT) has **no** deterministic margin behind it in the same
direction on grounding — so grounding is the reading.

## GPT-5.6 Sol is the worst variant on quality, at 4x Luna's price

Worth stating plainly because the naming does not suggest it and because Sol is the variant a
buyer would reach for.

| Published in-region rate, input / cache-read / output per 1M | $1.10 / $0.11 / $6.60 | $2.75 / $0.28 / $16.50 | **$5.50 / $0.55 / $33.00** |

Sol on `identity` is the study's live demonstration of what an unswept agent could hide: it
escalated **both** anti-false-positive fixtures against the incumbent's zero, dismissed a
corroborated application, and banded 2/6 against Sonnet's 3/3 — at $0.0269/call, **4x Luna's
price**. Paying more did not buy calibration.

(Sol is nevertheless the right GPT **judge** in the sibling study, precisely because it is the
only 5.6 variant that is not a candidate for a specialist slot. See

## The coverage gap is half the fan-out

**4 of 8 agents were swept.** `dealer`, `straw`, `employment` and `income` were never run on
GPT at all.

Worse for the false-positive question, quoted from the artifact's caveats: *"Only 2 of the 5
anti-false-positive fixtures (APP-1009, APP-1010) exercise the four agents swept here;
APP-1006/1007/1008 target employment, dealer and income, which were not swept. The
false-positive finding is therefore narrow and a wider anti-FP sweep is the obvious next
measurement."*

So the three fixtures designed to catch a specific false-positive failure mode target exactly
the three agents with the least evidence.

## What holding costs

A hold with an unstated price is not a decision. Both figures are **PROJECTIONS** from the
measured per-agent tokens, not benchmarks.

| | Hold on Claude | Move the four swept agents to Luna |
|---|---:|---:|
| $/adjudication, projected at 2026-07-28 rates | $0.1247 | **$0.0651** |
| Delta | — | **−$0.0596 (−47.8%)** |
| Fan-out floor lower bound | 22.05 s (`rings`) | **8.09 s (`income`)** |

Two things the second column exposes:

- We are declining a projected **47.8%** cost reduction and roughly **14 s** of fan-out floor.
- After such a move the binding agent becomes `income` — **which has no quality evidence of
  any kind.** The agent with the least evidence would set the pipeline's latency. That is an
  argument for sweeping it, and a second argument against a partial migration.

The floor figures are **lower bounds**, not predicted end-to-ends: fan-out is bounded by a
per-run *maximum*, which per-agent medians cannot reconstruct.

`agents/models.py:projected_cost_if_gpt_specialists` computes the counterfactual and labels
itself `PROJECTION OF A REJECTED COUNTERFACTUAL` — a weaker object than an ordinary
projection, because **no benchmark run would confirm it without changing the assignment
first.** It also *understates* the GPT case deliberately: the other four agents are priced at
Claude in that figure, because no measured GPT token count exists for them. A full-fan-out GPT
figure would be lower and is **not offered**.

## The two calibration failures reproduce across families

`known_failure_reproduction`:

| Fixture / agent | Pinned | Opus 5 | Sonnet 5 | Luna | Terra | Sol |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| APP-1014 / `rings` (`RNG-020`) | LOW RISK | POSSIBLE | POSSIBLE | POSSIBLE | POSSIBLE | POSSIBLE |
| APP-1004 / `bustout` (`BST-020`) | POSSIBLE RISK | HIGH | HIGH | HIGH | HIGH | HIGH |

Quoted verbatim: *"GPT reproduced it 3/3; Claude 2/2. Correct band: GPT 0/3, Claude 0/2."*

This is the study's most consequential *negative* result: it upgrades the finding from "no
*tier* fixes it" to **"no model choice fixes it."** Five models, two families, every one wrong
the same way — a second alert firing off the same underlying behaviour treated as independent
corroboration. That is the signature of a shared prompt defect.
[Open questions](/docs/open-questions#3-the-calibration-failures-no-model-choice-fixes).

## Every caveat the artifact carries

Reproduced rather than summarised, because the study's credibility is in its limits.

<Steps>

<Step>

**One judge cannot settle this.** See [judge methodology](/docs/model-selection/judge-methodology).
Nothing is "bias-corrected" — subtracting a bias estimate would produce a number that looks
adjusted and was never measured.

</Step>

<Step>

**The recommendation rests on the deterministic layers only.** Band agreement, anti-FP
escalation, `_context` grounding, `agents.output_contract.check`. The judged calibration and
substance scores are subjective, reported separately, and an axis where the judges disagree
yields no finding at all.

</Step>

<Step>

**6 applications per cell.** A band-agreement difference of one case is **17 points**, so a
one-case gap between two models is inside this sample's resolution by construction.

</Step>

<Step>

**`signals_cited` is style-sensitive** and partly measures whether a model writes identifiers
or paraphrases them. Measured: pooled over the 120 analyses Claude cites **4.56** signals per
analysis to GPT's **2.86**, and a looser stemming matcher moves GPT to **3.60** while moving
Claude only to **4.65** — so about **0.7 of the ~1.7 gap is paraphrasing rather than missing
evidence.** The looser matcher was rejected because it cannot keep `ssn_apps_last_7d` and
`ssn_apps_last_30d` distinct. Where `signals_cited` and the style-free `grounded_fraction`
disagree, grounding and the judges' substance scores are the reading.

</Step>

<Step>

**Only 2 of 5 anti-FP fixtures exercise the swept four.**

</Step>

<Step>

**No p95 anywhere.** `evals.bench.summarize_values` refuses a p95 below 20 samples and every
cell records the refusal string rather than a number.

</Step>

<Step>

**An unremovable asymmetry.** GPT specialists receive the verbatim prompt **concatenated with
the user turn** (the mantle Responses shape), while Claude specialists receive it as a
**cached system prompt**. That is a real difference in how the two families are invoked and it
cannot be removed while GPT-5.x is off Bedrock Converse.

</Step>

<Step>

**Latency and cost here are single serial calls, not the fan-out.** The end-to-end consequence
of a swap belongs to the [pipeline benchmark](/docs/measured-results).

</Step>

</Steps>
