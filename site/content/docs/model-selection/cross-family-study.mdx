---
title: Cross-family study
description: 4 agents x 6 applications x 5 models = 120 blind-judged analyses, 240 verdicts, two oppositely-biased judges, $16.89 measured. Latency and cost favoured GPT overwhelmingly and were overruled by one deterministic compliance measurement.
---

*For the customer's engineer first, and for the account team second — because the headline is a measured 47.8% projected cost reduction being declined.*

**Eight specialists stay on Claude.** GPT-5.6 measured 4x faster and 3x cheaper pooled, wrote a fifth of the words, and produced **zero** output-contract violations against Claude's 34. It was held anyway, on one deterministic measurement in which no judge takes part: `_context` grounding, **0.642 for Claude (n=48) against 0.357 for GPT (n=72)**, lower on **4 of 4** agents.

<Callout type="warn" title="What this study did NOT find">

## Design of the study

`evals/results/set_comparison.json`, generated `2026-07-28T21:32:24+00:00`, `aws_region` `us-east-1`, `mantle_region` `us-east-1`.

| | Value |
|---|---|
| Agents (`run.domains`) | `rings`, `synthetic`, `bustout`, `identity` — **4 of 8** |
| Applications (`run.applications`) | APP-1004, APP-1009, APP-1010, APP-1012, APP-1013, APP-1014 — **6** |
| Anti-false-positive fixtures in the sample | **APP-1009, APP-1010** — 2 |
| Claude set | `global.anthropic.claude-opus-5`, `global.anthropic.claude-sonnet-5` |
| GPT set | `openai.gpt-5.6-luna`, `openai.gpt-5.6-terra`, `openai.gpt-5.6-sol` |
| Cells / analyses / verdicts | 20 / **120** / **240** |
| `specialist_max_output_tokens` | 4096 |
| Shuffle seed | `20260728` |
| Judges | `global.anthropic.claude-opus-5` (claude family, **also a candidate**) and `openai.gpt-5.5` (gpt family, not a candidate) |
| Measured spend | specialists **$2.7526**, judges **$14.1343**, total **$16.8869** |
| `live_calls` in this report | 0 specialist calls, 0 judge calls — all reused from the committed sidecars |

Blinding, quoted from `run.blinding`: *"model/provider identifiers stripped from the analysis text before judging (`evals.tier_quality.anonymise`); judge order shuffled under the fixed seed; both judges see the same anonymised text and the same rubric."*

Judging cost **5.1x** the analyses it judged. That is what two judges at high reasoning effort over 120 analyses costs, and it is why this study exists once rather than per sprint. Sidecars `set_comparison_analyses.json` (120 analyses) and `set_comparison_verdicts.json` (240 verdicts) make the aggregate re-derivable at zero spend:

```bash
PYTHONPATH=. python3 -m evals.set_comparison --rescore
```

Two judges biased in opposite directions is the whole instrument, and the combination rule is on its own page: [judge methodology](/docs/model-selection/judge-methodology).

## Deciding metric: `_context` grounding [#what-decided-it-_context-grounding]

Mean `grounded_fraction` per (agent, model), computed over the 120 `per_analysis` rows. **No judge is involved in any cell.**

| Agent | Opus 5 | Sonnet 5 | Luna | Terra | Sol |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| `rings` | 0.875 | 0.792 | 0.625 | 0.625 | 0.583 |
| `identity` | 0.738 | 0.476 | 0.357 | 0.381 | 0.238 |
| `bustout` | 0.833 | 0.524 | 0.286 | 0.310 | 0.214 |
| `synthetic` | 0.517 | 0.383 | **0.150** | 0.350 | 0.167 |
| **Pooled mean** | **Claude 0.642** (n=48) | | **GPT 0.357** (n=72) | | |

Three properties make this the deciding axis rather than one more column. GPT is lower on **4 of 4** agents against both Claude incumbents, so it is not one bad cell dragging an average. Scoring is **deterministic** — `grounded_fraction` counts how many of the signals in an agent's own domain view appear in the analysis alongside their paired `_context` evidence, and no model scores it. And every specialist prompt states the requirement: the `rings` prompt tells the model it must validate signals using the columns whose names end in `_context`. A model that meets that less often is less compliant with her contract, whatever its band agreement says.

<Callout type="info" title="Mean here, median in the verdict cells">
Cells above are the **mean** of the six `per_analysis.grounding.grounded_fraction` values in
each cell. Verdict rows quote `measured.grounded_fraction_median` from the same artifact, and on
some cells the two differ — `identity`/Sonnet 5 is 0.476 mean against 0.429 median,
`synthetic`/Opus 5 is 0.517 mean against 0.550 median. Both are in the file; neither is
recomputed into the other. The study's disqualification rule ("less than half the incumbent's")
is evaluated on the **medians**, which is what the verdict strings quote.
</Callout>

`deterministic_cross_check`, GPT minus Claude, computed with no judge at all:

| Metric | Margin | Reading |
|---|---:|---|
| Band agreement | **−0.054** | inside this sample's resolution |
| Contract violations | **−0.756** | GPT better |
| Grounded fraction | **−0.284** | GPT worse — the only deterministic margin against it |

Quoted from the artifact: *"A judge-scored margin unaccompanied by a deterministic margin in the same direction is more likely preference than quality."* The judge-scored substance margin (+0.398 / +0.684 for GPT) has **no** deterministic margin behind it in the same direction, so grounding is the reading.

<Callout type="error" title="The Claude judge scored GPT higher on substance, against its own family">
`judge_self_preference.per_judge`: `global.anthropic.claude-opus-5` scored its **own** family
**+0.226** on calibration and **−0.398** on substance; `openai.gpt-5.5` scored its own family
**−0.239** on calibration and **+0.684** on substance.

So on substance *both* judges preferred GPT — including the one whose family that penalises.
That is much harder to dismiss than a single-judge win, and it is why "GPT writes a fifth of the
words, so it must be thinner" does not survive this study.
</Callout>

## Verdict grid, condensed

n=6 clears the study's own bar for a band claim (`MIN_APPS_FOR_A_BAND_CLAIM` = 5), so these verdicts *are* claimable. "As good" means the candidate lost on **none** of: band agreement, escalation on an anti-false-positive fixture, dismissal of a corroborated application, or grounded fraction below half the incumbent's.

| Agent | Luna | Terra | Sol |
|---|---|---|---|
| `rings` | **as good** | **as good** | not — band 4/6 vs 5/6 |
| `bustout` | **as good** | **as good** | not — grounding |
| `identity` | **as good** | not — band 4/5, one analysis declared no band | not — 2 anti-FP false positives, 1 false negative, band 2/6 |
| `synthetic` | not — grounded 0.150 vs 0.550 | **as good** | not — grounded 0.100 vs 0.550 |

Terra is the one GPT variant graded AS GOOD on **all four** swept agents. Luna is "as good" on 3 of 4 and is still not taken — the per-agent evidence would support moving `rings`, `bustout` and `identity` to Luna today, and it is not being done because the grounding gap is real on all three and because moving three of eight leaves a split fan-out whose *remaining* agents have **less** evidence than the ones that moved.

Two incumbent rows deserve naming while the grid is on screen. `bustout`/Sonnet 5 is the **worst** band cell in the whole study at 3/6, and all three GPT variants reached 4/6. And `identity`/Sonnet 5 has a band denominator of **3, not 6** — three of its analyses had no band the regex reader and the judge agreed on, which is a fidelity defect of the incumbent's own: *"6 application(s) answered per cell, 3 with a readable band; a band claim needs 5."*

## Sol: worst variant on quality at 4x Luna's price [#gpt-56-sol-is-the-worst-variant-on-quality-at-4x-lunas-price]

Worth stating plainly because the naming does not suggest it and because Sol is the variant a buyer would reach for.

| | Luna | Terra | Sol |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Published in-region rate, input / cache-read / output per 1M | $1.10 / $0.11 / $6.60 | $2.75 / $0.28 / $16.50 | **$5.50 / $0.55 / $33.00** |
| Agents graded AS GOOD | 3 / 4 | **4 / 4** | **0 / 4** |
| Anti-FP false positives on `identity` | 0/2 | 0/2 | **2/2** |
| Dismissals of a corroborated application | 0 | 0 | **1 (APP-1014)** |

Sol on `identity` is this study's live demonstration of what an unswept agent could hide: it escalated **both** anti-false-positive fixtures against the incumbent's zero, dismissed a corroborated application, and banded 2/6 against Sonnet's 3/3 — at $0.0269/call, 4x Luna's price. Paying more did not buy calibration.

Sol is nevertheless the right GPT **judge** in the sibling study, precisely because it is the only 5.6 variant that is not a candidate for a specialist slot. See `evals/judge_sol.py` and [judge methodology](/docs/model-selection/judge-methodology).

## Coverage gap is half the fan-out

**4 of 8 agents were swept.** `dealer`, `straw`, `employment` and `income` were never run on GPT at all. Worse for the false-positive question, quoted from the artifact's caveats: *"Only 2 of the 5 anti-false-positive fixtures (APP-1009, APP-1010) exercise the four agents swept here; APP-1006/1007/1008 target employment, dealer and income, which were not swept. The false-positive finding is therefore narrow and a wider anti-FP sweep is the obvious next measurement."*

So the three fixtures designed to catch a specific false-positive failure mode target exactly the three agents with the least evidence.

## Price of holding [#what-holding-costs]

A hold with an unstated price is not a decision. Both figures are **PROJECTIONS** from the measured per-agent tokens, not benchmarks.

| | Hold on Claude | Move the four swept agents to Luna | Recommendation |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| $/adjudication, projected at 2026-07-28 rates | $0.1247 | **$0.0651** | hold |
| Delta | — | **−$0.0596 (−47.8%)** | forgone, and stated as forgone |
| Fan-out floor lower bound | 22.05 s (`rings`) | **8.09 s (`income`)** | hold |

Two things the second column exposes. A projected **47.8%** cost reduction and roughly **14 s** of fan-out floor are being declined. And after such a move the binding agent becomes `income` — **which has no quality evidence of any kind**, so the agent with the least evidence would set the pipeline's latency. That is an argument for sweeping it, and a second argument against a partial migration.

Floor figures are **lower bounds**, not predicted end-to-ends: fan-out is bounded by a per-run *maximum*, which per-agent medians cannot reconstruct.

`agents/models.py:projected_cost_if_gpt_specialists` computes the counterfactual and labels itself `PROJECTION OF A REJECTED COUNTERFACTUAL` — a weaker object than an ordinary projection, because **no benchmark run would confirm it without changing the assignment first.** It also *understates* the GPT case deliberately: the other four agents are priced at Claude in that figure, because no measured GPT token count exists for them. A full-fan-out GPT figure would be lower and is **not offered**.

## Two calibration failures reproduce across families

`known_failure_reproduction`:

| Fixture / agent | Pinned | Opus 5 | Sonnet 5 | Luna | Terra | Sol |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| APP-1014 / `rings` (`RNG-020`) | LOW RISK | POSSIBLE | POSSIBLE | POSSIBLE | POSSIBLE | POSSIBLE |
| APP-1004 / `bustout` (`BST-020`) | POSSIBLE RISK | HIGH | HIGH | HIGH | HIGH | HIGH |

Quoted verbatim: *"GPT reproduced it 3/3; Claude 2/2. Correct band: GPT 0/3, Claude 0/2."*

This is the study's most consequential *negative* result: it upgrades the finding from "no *tier* fixes it" to **"no model choice fixes it."** Five models, two families, every one wrong the same way — a second alert firing off the same underlying behaviour treated as independent corroboration. That is the signature of a shared prompt defect. [Open questions](/docs/open-questions#3-the-calibration-failures-no-model-choice-fixes).

## Still unknown

Every caveat the artifact carries, reproduced rather than summarised, because the study's credibility is in its limits.

| Limit | Detail |
|---|---|
| One judge cannot settle this | Nothing is "bias-corrected" — subtracting a bias estimate would produce a number that looks adjusted and was never measured. [Judge methodology](/docs/model-selection/judge-methodology) |
| Recommendation rests on the deterministic layers only | Band agreement, anti-FP escalation, `_context` grounding, `agents.output_contract.check`. Judged calibration and substance are subjective, reported separately, and an axis where the judges disagree yields no finding |
| 6 applications per cell | A band-agreement difference of one case is **17 points**, so a one-case gap is inside this sample's resolution by construction |
| `signals_cited` is style-sensitive | Pooled, Claude cites **4.56** signals per analysis to GPT's **2.86**; a looser stemming matcher moves GPT to **3.60** and Claude only to **4.65**, so about **0.7 of the ~1.7 gap is paraphrasing**. The looser matcher was rejected because it cannot keep `ssn_apps_last_7d` and `ssn_apps_last_30d` distinct. [Length versus evidence](/docs/model-selection/length-vs-evidence) |
| Only 2 of 5 anti-FP fixtures exercise the swept four | APP-1006/1007/1008 target the unswept agents |
| No p95 anywhere | `evals.bench.summarize_values` refuses a p95 below 20 samples and every cell records the refusal string rather than a number |
| An unremovable asymmetry | GPT specialists receive the verbatim prompt **concatenated with the user turn** (the mantle Responses shape); Claude specialists receive it as a **cached system prompt**. That cannot be removed while GPT-5.x is off Bedrock Converse |
| Latency and cost here are single serial calls | The end-to-end consequence of a swap belongs to the [pipeline benchmark](/docs/measured-results) |

## What would change the answer

| Experiment | Cost | What it settles |
|---|---|---|
| One prompt-engineering attempt on grounding — instruct the model to quote the paired `_context` row it validated against, then re-measure `grounded_fraction` | small | The only thing standing between this evidence and a migration |
| Extend the sweep to the other four agents, including APP-1006 / 1007 / 1008 | a second $17-scale study | Whether the anti-false-positive result covers the whole fan-out rather than half of it |

**If grounding closes, the latency and cost case is already made and this assignment should change.** Plumbing is not the blocker: `agents/fanout.py` routes a GPT specialist today (`transport_for('openai.gpt-5.6-luna') == 'bedrock-mantle'`), so reversing the decision is one value in `SPECIALIST_MODELS`. The hold is a measurement result, not a missing code path.
