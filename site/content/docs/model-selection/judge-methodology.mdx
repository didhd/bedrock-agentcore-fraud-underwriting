*For the customer's engineer, and for anyone deciding whether to believe the two model studies at all.*

Bias was measured, not assumed away. An LLM asked "which of these two fraud analyses is better?" has a family, a house style, and a measurable preference for text resembling its own output — so the first study made that conflict maximal on purpose, then quantified it: the judge rated its own family **+0.78** on substance while the axis it could not touch moved **−0.026**. If this page does not hold up, neither [tier re-derivation](/docs/model-selection/tier-rederivation) nor [cross-family study](/docs/model-selection/cross-family-study) does.

## Self-preference, quantified

From `tier_quality.json → judge_self_preference`:

| | Value |
|---|---:|
| Judge | `global.anthropic.claude-opus-5` |
| Its own rows | **16** |
| Other rows | **32** |
| Judge-scored calibration margin on its own family | **+0.31** |
| Judge-scored substance margin on its own family | **+0.78** |
| Deterministic band-agreement margin (the judge cannot influence this) | **−0.026** |
| Deterministic contract-violation margin | **−0.12** |

Reading, quoted from the artifact:

> On its own 16 rows the judge scored substance +0.78 and calibration +0.31 versus the other
> 32, while band agreement moved −0.026. Treat any Opus-vs-cheaper margin below the substance
> margin as unsupported.

<Callout type="error" title="What that pair of numbers actually shows">
A judge rated its own family nearly a **full point** higher on substance while the axis it could
not touch — whether the declared band matched the fixture's pinned expectation — moved
**−0.026**, i.e. essentially nothing, and slightly *against* its own family.

So the judge-scored margin was mostly **preference**, not quality. That single comparison is the
whole justification for everything else on this page.
</Callout>

It is also what killed the last remaining argument for keeping `rings` on Opus 5. Opus's only surviving edge there was **+0.20** judge calibration, which sits **inside** the judge's own measured +0.31 self-preference and is therefore not distinguishable from bias. [Detail](/docs/model-selection/tier-rederivation#rings-heavy--mid).

## Two judges, biased in opposite directions

A harder question follows in the cross-family study — is the GPT set as good? — where a single judge from either family would decide the answer by its own identity. So every one of the 120 analyses is judged **twice**.

| Judge | Family | Also a candidate? | Why that choice | Effort | Transport |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `global.anthropic.claude-opus-5` | claude | **Yes** | The strongest possible form of the conflict: if a finding survives a judge scoring its own output, it survives | Strands structured output (forced tool schema) | Bedrock Converse |
| `openai.gpt-5.5` | gpt | **No** | Carries the mirror bias without scoring a sibling it might be deployed beside — the candidates are the 5.6 variants | `medium` reasoning effort | `bedrock-mantle` Responses |

Sol is picked by the same logic in `evals/judge_sol.py`, the standalone GPT judge module. Quoted from its docstring: *"Sol is deliberately the GPT judge rather than Luna or Terra: it is the only GPT-5.6 variant NOT a candidate for any specialist assignment (Luna is the leading candidate and the incumbent synthesizer, Terra is the middle candidate), so it is the one GPT model that is not scoring a sibling it might be assigned alongside. It also measured the highest reasoning effort of the three, which is what a judge should be."*

## What is held identical between the two judges

If the rubric drifted between judges, their agreement would mean nothing. So `judge_sol.py` and `set_comparison.py` **import** the shared pieces from `evals/tier_quality.py` rather than restating them:

```python
from evals.tier_quality import (
    JUDGE_MAX_TOKENS, AnalysisRow, _JUDGE_INSTRUCTIONS,
    _judge_verdict_model, anonymise,
)
```

| Held identical | Differs |
|---|---|
| Judge instruction text | The model |
| Anonymisation | Transport (Converse vs Responses) |
| Verdict field names, enums and score anchors | How the JSON shape is enforced |
| Pinned expectation from `fixtures.loader.expected_for` | Reasoning effort |

A test asserts the two judges use the same instruction text.

<Callout type="warn" title="The one place the transport forces a difference">
Strands structured output carries the Claude judge's verdict — the pydantic model becomes
a forced tool schema, so shape is enforced by the API. The mantle Responses path has no
equivalent here, so the GPT judge is asked for a bare JSON object and the reply is parsed and
validated by `_parse_gpt_verdict` against the same ten fields.

Critically: **a malformed or out-of-range verdict is an ERROR, not a clamped value.** Quoted
from the code's comment on `_VERDICT_FIELDS`: *"silently clamping a score would invent agreement
between the two judges, which is the one thing this design must not do."*
</Callout>

## Verdict schema

Ten fields, identical for both judges:

| Field | Type | What it is |
|---|---|---|
| `band_read_from_analysis` | `LOW RISK` / `POSSIBLE RISK` / `HIGH RISK` / `UNCLEAR` | Band the judge reads out of the prose. Compared against the regex reader; disagreements are not counted as agreement |
| `band_matches_expectation` | bool | Against the fixture's pinned band |
| `calibration_score` | int 1–5 | How well the band follows the customer's own calibration text |
| `false_positive_verdict` | `Pass` / `PassWithNote` / `FalsePositive` / `FalseNegative` | Against her anti-false-positive rules |
| `escalation_drift` | `aligned` / `escalates` / `dismisses` | |
| `substance_score` | int 1–5 | **Density, explicitly not length** |
| `padding_observed` | bool | |
| `cites_context_fields` | bool | Whether it validates signals against the paired `_context` |
| `contract_notes` | string | Output-contract deviations |
| `justification` | string, capped | Citation for the scores |

Anchors are stated in the prompt rather than left to the model. `substance_score`: *"1 = mostly restatement, scaffolding or repetition of the same finding; 3 = mixed; 5 = every section adds a distinct fact or a distinct inference. Judge density, NOT length: a short analysis that carries its evidence scores high, and a long one that repeats itself scores low."*

A `calibration_score` of 5 requires the model to *"explicitly weigh the benign explanation the prompt names for this signal before concluding, and treat alerts from one behaviour as one signal"* — the anchor encodes the customer's own anti-false-positive principle.

Blinding, from `run.blinding`: model and provider identifiers stripped from the analysis text before judging (`evals.tier_quality.anonymise`), judge order shuffled under the fixed seed, both judges seeing the same anonymised text and the same rubric. Shuffle seed `20260728` in both studies, so ordering is reproducible rather than merely random.

## Combination rule

`evals/set_comparison.py:two_judge_agreement`. Margins are **GPT minus Claude**; positive favours GPT. `JUDGE_MARGIN_FLOOR = 0.31`.

| Both margins | Verdict |
|---|---|
| favour GPT by more than the floor | **GPT better on both judges** |
| favour Claude by more than the floor | **CLAUDE better on both judges** |
| inside the floor | **NO DIFFERENCE — both margins inside the measured bias floor** |
| point opposite ways, or only one clears the floor | **NOT ESTABLISHED** |
| at least one judge produced no verdict | **NOT ESTABLISHED** |

0.31 is a measured number, not a confidence interval. Quoted from the code: *"It is not a confidence interval — the sample is far too small for one — it is the prior study's MEASURED self-preference on calibration (+0.31), used as the floor below which a judge's opinion cannot be distinguished from its own identity."* Note what the third row is: **"no difference" is a real finding, and the likeliest one — it is what "as good at the fraud analysis" looks like when it is true.**

## NOT ESTABLISHED is an outcome, not an error

Two of the ten axes in the cross-family study returned it, and no recommendation rests on either.

| Axis | Scope | Claude judge | GPT judge | Verdict |
|---|---|---:|---:|---|
| `calibration_score` | ALL POOLED | −0.226 | −0.239 | NO DIFFERENCE |
| `calibration_score` | `bustout` | +0.222 | +0.111 | NO DIFFERENCE |
| `calibration_score` | `identity` | **−0.791** | **−0.850** | **CLAUDE better on both judges** |
| `calibration_score` | `rings` | −0.083 | −0.139 | NO DIFFERENCE |
| `calibration_score` | `synthetic` | −0.361 | −0.222 | **NOT ESTABLISHED** — disagree in magnitude |
| `substance_score` | ALL POOLED | +0.398 | +0.684 | **GPT better on both judges** |
| `substance_score` | `bustout` | +0.361 | +0.861 | **GPT better on both judges** |
| `substance_score` | `identity` | +0.248 | +0.431 | **NOT ESTABLISHED** — disagree in magnitude |
| `substance_score` | `rings` | +0.528 | +0.806 | **GPT better on both judges** |
| `substance_score` | `synthetic` | +0.417 | +0.583 | **GPT better on both judges** |

Read the two NOT ESTABLISHED rows carefully, because they are the mechanism working. On `synthetic` calibration the Claude judge's −0.361 clears the floor and the GPT judge's −0.222 does not, so the axis yields **nothing**. On `identity` substance it is the mirror: +0.431 clears, +0.248 does not. Neither is reported as a small effect, and neither is averaged into a single number.

Nothing is bias-corrected, and that is deliberate. Quoted from `JUDGE_CONFLICT_NOTE`: *"Neither margin is 'corrected' by subtracting a bias estimate: that would produce a number that looks adjusted and was never measured."* A "bias-corrected" score is a fabricated score with a plausible name on it. This design's answer to bias is redundancy, not arithmetic.

## Each judge's measured preference for its own family

From `set_comparison.json → judge_self_preference.per_judge`:

| Judge | Family | Own-family calibration margin | Own-family substance margin | Behaved as predicted? |
|---|---|---:|---:|---|
| `global.anthropic.claude-opus-5` | claude | **+0.226** | **−0.398** | On calibration yes; on substance **no** |
| `openai.gpt-5.5` | gpt | **−0.239** | **+0.684** | On substance yes; on calibration **no** |

GPT's analyses were rated higher by 0.398 on substance by the Claude judge. That is the most useful single number in the study, because it is a preference running the wrong way for the judge's own identity.

## Layers no judge touches

Both studies are explicit that the judges do not decide. Quoted from the caveats:

> The recommendation rests on the DETERMINISTIC layers — band agreement against the pinned
> fixture, escalation on the anti-false-positive fixtures, `_context` grounding, and
> `agents.output_contract.check`. The judged calibration and substance scores are subjective
> and are reported separately, and an axis where the two judges disagree yields no finding at
> all.

| Layer | Computed by | Judge involved? |
|---|---|---|
| Band agreement vs the pinned expectation | `fixtures.loader.expected_for` + regex, cross-checked by the judge's own band read | No |
| False positives on the anti-FP fixtures | fixture metadata | No |
| False negatives | fixture metadata | No |
| **`_context` grounding** | `evals.tier_quality.context_grounding` | **No** |
| Output-contract violations | `agents.output_contract.check` | No |
| Calibration score | judge | Yes |
| Substance score | judge | Yes |
| Padding observed | judge | Yes |

Policing the boundary between those two groups is the `deterministic_cross_check` block's job: band agreement **−0.054**, contract violations **−0.756**, grounded fraction **−0.284**, all GPT minus Claude. *"A judge-scored margin unaccompanied by a deterministic margin in the same direction is more likely preference than quality."* The cross-family recommendation therefore rests on **grounding**, which has no judge in it, and not on the substance margin, which does.

## Reading the band twice

Small, and it matters. From `tier_quality.json` caveats: *"The band is read twice — by regex and by the blind judge. Rows where the two readers disagree are listed per cell and are not counted as agreement."*

That is how `identity`/Sonnet 5 ends up with a band denominator of **3, not 6** in the cross-family study: three of its analyses had no band the two readers agreed on, and the sample note says so — *"6 application(s) answered per cell, 3 with a readable band; a band claim needs 5."* A design taking the judge's read alone would have reported 6/6 and hidden a real fidelity defect.

## What judging cost

| Study | Analyses | Verdicts | Specialist spend | Judge spend | Total | Judge multiple |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Tier quality | 48 | 48 | $1.3586 | **$3.5501** | **$4.9088** | **2.6x** |
| Cross-family | 120 | 240 | $2.7526 | **$14.1343** | **$16.8869** | **5.1x** |

That is the real price of the methodology, measured rather than estimated, and it is why verdicts are cached in sidecar files (`tier_quality_verdicts.json`, `set_comparison_verdicts.json`) keyed by `domain|model|application` so a failed judge call can be retried without re-paying the successes. Both reports regenerate from the cached verdicts at **zero** spend:

```bash
PYTHONPATH=. python3 -m evals.set_comparison --rescore
```

`live_calls` in the committed cross-family report records `judge_calls_made: 0` and `judge_verdicts_reused_from_cache: 240`, so the published aggregate is reproducible without calling a model.

## Still unknown

| Limit | Why two judges do not fix it |
|---|---|
| **Small n** | Six applications per cell in the cross-family study, three to five in the tier study. A one-case band difference is 17 points at n=6, and two judges do not add samples |
| **A shared blind spot** | If both judges are wrong the same way, agreement will not catch it — and there is a live example on this site: five models across two families all mis-band APP-1014 and APP-1004, and the judges named the same fault rather than disputing the band. Agreement is evidence about the *analyses*, not about the *rubric* |
| **Subjectivity in the anchors** | "Every section adds a distinct fact" is a human sentence. Both judges interpret it, and the interpretation is not measured |
| **Transport asymmetry** | The Claude judge's verdict shape is API-enforced; the GPT judge's is parsed. Both validate against the same ten fields, but the failure modes are not identical |
