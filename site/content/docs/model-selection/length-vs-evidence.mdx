<Callout type="info" title="Audience">
For the customer's engineer. This is the page that tests the objection an experienced fraud
reviewer raises first, and it is the one page where the answer comes out **against** the
cheaper model on a specific agent.

Artifacts: `evals/results/set_comparison.json → length_gap` (30 paired comparisons, 6 model
pairings x 5 scopes) and `evals/results/tier_quality.json → verbosity_vs_substance` (12 paired
comparisons within Claude). Code: `evals/set_comparison.py:length_gap`.
</Callout>

## The objection

Measured, pooled over the 120 cross-family analyses:

| | Claude | GPT |
|---|---:|---:|
| Output tokens p50 | **1422.5** (n=48) | **288.0** (n=72) |

GPT writes roughly a fifth of the words. On a fraud product that reads as "a fifth of the
analysis", and that intuition is why the study was designed to test it rather than assert
either way.

The customer's own master prompt says the opposite. Rule `MSTR-018` in it states — in her
words, kept in `prompts/verbatim/master_agent_prompt.txt` rather than reproduced here — that
how much a specialist *writes* is not a signal of how severe the fraud is. That rule is the
reason this comparison exists: she had already ruled out length as evidence, so a port that
picked a model for writing more would be contradicting her.

## The method

Pairing is by **(application, domain)**, so only analyses of the *same payload* are compared
and a length difference cannot be an artifact of one model drawing easier applications. Four
ratios, all Claude ÷ GPT, all medians over the paired set:

<Accordions>

<Accordion title="chars_ratio — how much longer the Claude analysis is">
Raw character count. Says nothing on its own; it is the denominator for the question.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="evidence_ratio — distinct numeric facts plus signal identifiers">
**Style-sensitive.** A backticked identifier table inflates it. Reported, not decisive.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="signals_cited_ratio — how many of the payload's OWN signals each names">
**Also style-sensitive**, and quantified: see [the paraphrase correction](#the-paraphrase-correction-07-of-a-17-signal-gap) below.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="grounded_ratio — _context fields actually validated">
**The style-free one.** `context_grounding` counts only tokens unique to the `_context` row,
which cannot be produced by restating the signals block. This is the instruction the customer
cares most about and the one brevity is most likely to sacrifice.
</Accordion>

</Accordions>

Plus `band_justified_both`, so a shorter analysis cannot pass by declaring a band with no
reason attached.

The reading rule, quoted verbatim from `length_gap.reading`:

> `chars_ratio` well above `signals_cited_ratio` and `grounded_ratio` means the longer
> (Claude) analysis is **padding**: same evidence, more words. `chars_ratio` at or below those
> ratios means the shorter (GPT) analysis really is **dropping evidence**, which outweighs any
> latency or cost win.

<Callout type="warn" title="Which ratio wins when they disagree, decided before the numbers were read">
Quoted from the docstring: *"READ `grounded_ratio` FIRST WHERE THE TWO DISAGREE.
`signals_cited_ratio` partly measures whether a model writes identifiers or prose ... When it
and `grounded_ratio` point different ways, the honest reading is that the metrics disagree
about a paraphrase, and the judges' substance scores are the tiebreak — not whichever number
happens to support the conclusion already preferred."*
</Callout>

## The headline: padding, on the pooled result and on 3 of 4 agents

`opus-5` vs `luna`, the widest pairing in the study:

| Scope | Paired | chars ratio | evidence ratio | signals-cited ratio | grounded ratio | Same band | Finding |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---|
| **ALL POOLED** | 24 | **2.95x** | 5.08x | **1.08x** | 2.25x | **23/24** | **PADDING** |
| `bustout` | 6 | 4.62x | 4.95x | **7.0x** | 3.0x | 6/6 | **EVIDENCE LOST** |
| `identity` | 6 | 3.44x | 3.12x | 1.17x | 1.88x | 5/6 | PADDING |
| **`rings`** | 6 | **2.83x** | 7.75x | **1.00x** | 1.5x | 6/6 | **PADDING** |
| `synthetic` | 6 | 2.02x | 10.0x | 0.67x | 2.75x | 6/6 | length and evidence scale together |

The `rings` row is the cleanest statement of the finding: **Opus writes 2.83x Luna's characters
while naming 1.00x the payload's signals — exactly the same signals — and reaches the same band
in 6 of 6 paired comparisons.** The extra 183% of text names no additional signal at all.

On `synthetic` the ratio inverts: Luna names **more** of the payload's signals than Opus does
(0.67x means Opus names two thirds of what Luna names), in a third of the characters.

<Callout type="info" title="This reproduces the within-Claude result">
The Claude-only tier study found the same shape at a smaller magnitude: on `rings`, Opus wrote
**1.76x** Sonnet's characters carrying **1.71x** the distinct evidence for a judge substance
delta of **+0** (n=5 paired). So verbosity-without-substance is not a cross-family artifact —
it is a property of the larger model on this workload.
[Tier re-derivation](/docs/model-selection/tier-rederivation#the-padding-finding-extra-length-is-not-extra-evidence).
</Callout>

## Where the objection is right: `bustout`

**`bustout` returns EVIDENCE LOST against every Claude model in every GPT pairing.** It is the
only agent that does, and it is unanimous across six pairings:

| Claude | GPT | Paired | chars ratio | signals-cited ratio | Finding string from the artifact |
|---|---|---:|---:|---:|---|
| opus-5 | luna | 6 | 4.62x | **7.0x** | *"the shorter GPT analysis names 0.14x the signals for 0.22x the length"* |
| opus-5 | terra | 6 | 4.57x | 5.25x | *"names 0.19x the signals for 0.22x the length"* |
| opus-5 | sol | 6 | 5.38x | **7.0x** | *"names 0.14x the signals for 0.19x the length"* |
| sonnet-5 | luna | 6 | 2.29x | 4.5x | *"names 0.22x the signals for 0.44x the length"* |
| sonnet-5 | terra | 6 | 2.26x | 2.75x | *"names 0.36x the signals for 0.44x the length"* |
| sonnet-5 | sol | 6 | 2.96x | 3.5x | *"names 0.29x the signals for 0.34x the length"* |

The arithmetic in the top row: Luna's `bustout` analysis is **22% of Opus's length** but names
**14% of the signals** — proportionally *fewer* signals than its length would predict. That is
the definition the method set in advance, and it fires.

Look at the underlying pairs and the mechanism is stark. From `length_gap.comparisons` →
`opus-5` vs `luna` → pooled `pairs`, the `signals_cited` counts on `bustout`:

| Application | opus-5 | luna |
|---|---:|---:|
| APP-1004 | 7 | **0** |
| APP-1009 | 7 | 1 |
| APP-1010 | 7 | 1 |
| APP-1012 | 5 | 1 |
| APP-1013 | 5 | **0** |
| APP-1014 | 7 | 1 |

Luna names **zero or one** of `bustout`'s signals by name on every application. Opus names five
to seven.

<Callout type="warn" title="But read the paraphrase correction before concluding">
`bustout` is precisely the agent the artifact's own caveat singles out as most affected by the
identifier-vs-prose measurement problem. `grounded_ratio` — the style-free metric — is 3.0x on
this pairing, i.e. Opus validates three times as many `_context` rows, which is a real gap but
a much smaller one than 7.0x. And the judges scored **GPT better on substance on `bustout` on
both judges** (+0.361 / +0.861).

So the honest reading for `bustout` is: **the metrics disagree, and they disagree about
paraphrasing.** `signals_cited` says evidence lost; `grounded_fraction` says a 3x gap; the two
judges say GPT is denser. The study's own tiebreak rule puts `grounded_fraction` and the judges
first — which still leaves a grounding gap, which is the reason `bustout` stays on Claude
anyway.
</Callout>

## The paraphrase correction: 0.7 of a 1.7-signal gap

Quoted verbatim from the artifact's caveats, because this is the single most self-critical
measurement in the study:

> `signals_cited` is STYLE-SENSITIVE and partly measures whether a model writes signal
> identifiers or paraphrases them. Opus writes a backticked table of every identifier; Luna
> writes 'two SSN applications in both the last 7 and 30 days', which cites the same two
> velocity signals without containing the token 'apps'. Measured: pooled over these 120
> analyses Claude cites **4.56** signals per analysis to GPT's **2.86**, and a looser stemming
> matcher moves GPT to **3.60** while moving Claude only to **4.65** — so about **0.7 of the
> ~1.7 gap is paraphrasing rather than missing evidence**. The looser matcher was **rejected**
> because it cannot keep `ssn_apps_last_7d` and `ssn_apps_last_30d` distinct.

| Matcher | Claude signals/analysis | GPT signals/analysis | Gap |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Shipped (strict) | 4.56 | 2.86 | **1.70** |
| Looser stemming (rejected) | 4.65 | 3.60 | **1.05** |

**About 41% of the measured citation gap is a style difference, not a missing-evidence
difference.** The looser matcher was nevertheless rejected, and the reason is a fraud-domain
reason: it cannot distinguish a 7-day velocity signal from a 30-day one, which is exactly the
distinction a bust-out analysis turns on. A metric that improves the score by blurring two
signals into one is worse than a metric with a known bias.

## Band agreement survives the length gap

The strongest single argument that brevity is not costing fraud judgement:

| Pairing | Paired | Same band | Both bands justified |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| opus-5 vs luna | 24 | **23/24** | 23 |
| opus-5 vs terra | 23 | **23/23** | 23 |
| opus-5 vs sol | 24 | 18/24 | 24 |
| sonnet-5 vs luna | 21 | 19/21 | 21 |
| sonnet-5 vs terra | 20 | 18/20 | 20 |
| sonnet-5 vs sol | 21 | 15/21 | 21 |

**Terra reached the same band as Opus 5 in 23 of 23 paired comparisons**, in roughly 30% of the
characters. Luna missed one of 24.

The two low rows are both Sol, and the collapse is localised: `identity`/Sol agrees with Opus 5
in **1 of 6** and with Sonnet 5 in **0 of 3**. That is the same cell that escalated both
anti-false-positive fixtures. Sol's problem is not brevity — its per-cell output medians run
126–370 tokens, in the same range as Luna's 206–421 — it is calibration, at 4x Luna's price.
[Cross-family study](/docs/model-selection/cross-family-study#gpt-56-sol-is-the-worst-variant-on-quality-at-4x-lunas-price).

## And within Claude: length is not even monotonic in the tier

From `tier_quality.json → verbosity_vs_substance`, pooled over 16 paired applications:

| Longer | Shorter | Paired | chars ratio | distinct-evidence ratio | Judge substance delta | Pairs where only the longer escalates |
|---|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| opus-5 | sonnet-5 | 16 | 1.31x | 1.60x | +0.0 | **0** |
| opus-5 | haiku-4-5 | 16 | 1.00x | 1.17x | +1.0 | **0** |
| **haiku-4-5** | sonnet-5 | 16 | **1.33x** | 1.32x | **−1.0** | **0** |

**Haiku 4.5 writes more characters than Sonnet 5 (1.33x) and scores a full point lower on
substance.** The cheapest model in the study is not the shortest, and the longest is not the
best. Length and quality are close to orthogonal on this workload, which is why neither can
substitute for the other in a model decision.

The last column is **0 in every row of every pairing** — across 48 paired comparisons in the
tier study, there is no pair where the longer analysis escalated the band and the shorter one
did not. So verbosity does not drift toward over-escalation either.

## What this page concludes

<Steps>

<Step>

**"GPT writes a fifth of the words" is not, by itself, an objection.** Pooled, paired on
identical payloads, the longer Claude analysis is 2.95x the length for 1.08x the signals named
and the same band 23 of 24 times.

</Step>

<Step>

**On `bustout` it is a real objection, on one metric, and a smaller one on the style-free
metric.** `signals_cited` says 7.0x; `grounded_ratio` says 3.0x; both judges say GPT is denser.
The metrics disagree about paraphrasing and the study says so rather than picking the
convenient one.

</Step>

<Step>

**Brevity is not why the specialists stayed on Claude.** The deciding measurement was
`_context` grounding — 0.642 vs 0.357, lower on 4 of 4 agents, no judge involved. The length
gap was tested, and it mostly exonerates GPT.
[Cross-family study](/docs/model-selection/cross-family-study#what-decided-it-_context-grounding).

</Step>

<Step>

**The verbosity that does matter is `bustout`'s and `rings`'s own.** Those two prompts carry no
character cap and write **1,331 output tokens p50** against **381** for the six capped agents,
with **30.1%** of their calls exceeding 4,000 characters against 3.6%. That is a prompt
question for the customer, not a model question.
[Open questions](/docs/open-questions#5-two-of-her-prompts-carry-no-character-cap).

</Step>

</Steps>
