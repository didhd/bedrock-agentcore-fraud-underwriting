<Callout type="info" title="Audience">
For the customer's engineer, and for anyone about to quote a vendor latency dashboard in a
deployment decision. The transferable finding on this page is the last section: **a region
ranking is a property of your reply length, and ranking on someone else's probe picks the wrong
region.**

Code: `agents/mantle.py` (`MEASURED_SYNTHESIS_LATENCY`, `THIRD_PARTY_SHORT_PROMPT_PROBE`,
`region_choice`, `measure_matrix`). Raw probe output was `/tmp/bench/matrix.json` — scratch,
uncommitted — and the numbers below are transcribed from it into the module, where
`measure_matrix` can re-derive them.
</Callout>

## Why a region choice needs a measurement at all

A `bedrock-mantle` call is **in-region only**. There is no `global.*` or `us.*` inference
profile for GPT-5.x and no cross-region inference, so whichever region the client points at is
the region that answers. And the synthesizer is the pipeline's serial bottleneck, so its
region is on the critical path of every adjudication.

<Callout type="error" title="AWS_REGION is deliberately not consulted, and it measurably mattered">
`resolve_mantle_region` does **not** read `AWS_REGION`. With `AWS_REGION=us-west-2` — this
repo's own dev default — a previous revision sent every synthesis to us-west-2 at **5.02 s p50**
instead of us-east-1 at **4.61 s p50** (n=3 each, real prompt). That is an **8.9% latency tax**
on the pipeline's bottleneck, paid for no benefit.

Co-locating the synthesizer with the Converse specialists buys nothing either, because the
mantle call is in-region only with no cross-region profile to share.
</Callout>

## The measured matrix

Streamed Responses calls on the **real 31,343-character synthesis prompt** — the customer's
verbatim master prompt with all eight specialist narratives substituted in, APP-1004 —
`effort=low`, `max_output_tokens=8192`, **n=3 per cell, 24 calls total, 2026-07-28**.

**All 24 calls returned a valid 21-key `Adjudication` and all 24 adjudicated HIGH RISK /
DECLINE.** The latency spread below is therefore not bought with a shorter or a worse answer.

| Model | Region | p50 total (n=3) | p50 TTFT | p50 tok/s | p50 out tok |
|---|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| **luna** | **us-east-1** | **4.61 s** | **0.79 s** | 176.0 | 812 |
| luna | us-west-2 | 5.02 s | 1.19 s | **180.0** | 903 |
| luna | us-east-2 | 6.79 s | 1.63 s | 115.8 | 878 |
| terra | us-east-2 | 6.29 s | 0.63 s | 126.4 | 825 |
| terra | us-east-1 | 6.72 s | 0.69 s | 122.3 | 785 |
| terra | us-west-2 | 7.63 s | 0.89 s | 95.1 | 790 |
| sol | us-east-1 | 11.19 s | 4.19 s | 91.1 | 1020 |
| sol | us-east-2 | **30.66 s** | **22.07 s** | 31.6 | 900 |

`openai.gpt-5.5` and `openai.gpt-5.4` are priced and region-mapped in `agents/mantle.py` but
carry **no latency cell at all**, because they were never measured on this prompt. Absence is
treated as "unknown", never as "slow": `measured_region_preference` returns `()` for an
unmeasured model and the caller falls back to declared availability order.

<Callout type="warn" title="Three ways to read this table honestly, from the module's own notes">
1. **n=3 is a median of three points, not a percentile.** It separates 4.61 s from 30.66 s. It
   does **not** defend 4.61 s against 5.02 s as a stable ranking, and the code says so:
   `MIN_TRIALS_FOR_A_RANKING = 3` and every rendering prints its n.
2. **The four p50 columns are independent medians**, taken column-wise. `tok/s` is the median of
   the per-trial throughputs, **not** `out tok / total`. Those coincide exactly in **3 of the 8**
   cells and differ by up to **10.5%** elsewhere. They are kept as measured rather than
   recomputed, because a recomputed number is not a measured one.
3. **Totals are streamed totals.** Streaming is the only way to observe TTFT;
   `agents/synthesize.py` calls the endpoint **non-streamed**, and the report says so rather
   than assuming the two are equal.
</Callout>

## What the ranking is ordered on, and what that is worth

`measured_region_preference` orders on **total** latency, because for this workload total is the
number the customer feels — one adjudication, no streaming UI on the critical path.

The module is unusually careful about how much that choice is worth, and the care is warranted:

| Ranking key | Luna's first place | Same as ordering on total? |
|---|---|---|
| Total p50 | us-east-1 (4.61 s) | — |
| TTFT p50 | us-east-1 (0.79 s) | **Yes** — coincidentally identical for all three variants |
| **Tokens/s p50** | **us-west-2 (180.0)** | **No** |

So "we rank on total, not TTFT" is a statement of intent that **changed nothing on this data**,
and it is asserted as such in the tests rather than overstated. The axis that actually flips the
answer is **throughput**, and `test_ranking_on_throughput_would_pick_a_different_region` pins
that.

## Sol's regional gap is an availability question, not just a latency one

| Variant | Available in |
|---|---|
| `openai.gpt-5.6-luna` | us-east-1, us-east-2, us-west-2 |
| `openai.gpt-5.6-terra` | us-east-1, us-east-2, us-west-2 |
| `openai.gpt-5.6-sol` | **us-east-1, us-east-2 only** |

A stack deployed in us-west-2 that asks for Sol gets a confusing 404. `region_choice` therefore
**replaces** an unavailable region with the measured-best *available* one and logs the reason:
availability overrides both an explicit `region=` argument **and** the `BEDROCK_MANTLE_REGION`
override, because the alternative is a call that cannot succeed.

And Sol in us-east-2 measured **30.66 s p50 with 22.07 s TTFT** — 2.7x its own us-east-1 number
and **6.7x Luna's** us-east-1 number. That one cell would blow the customer's sub-minute target
on the synthesis call by itself.

## The precedence order

`agents/mantle.py:region_choice` returns `(region, why)`, and the `why` string is the point of
the function — it lands in the log once per distinct choice rather than once per adjudication.

<Steps>

<Step>
**An explicit `region=` argument** — a caller that has already decided.
</Step>

<Step>
**`BEDROCK_MANTLE_REGION`** — the operator override: data residency, a new region, or simply
disagreeing with our measurement.
</Step>

<Step>
**The measured-fastest region** for that model from `MEASURED_SYNTHESIS_LATENCY`.
</Step>

<Step>
**The model's first declared available region**, for a variant nobody measured.
</Step>

<Step>
**`DEFAULT_MANTLE_REGION` = us-east-1** — chosen because it is the only region where every
GPT-5.6 variant is available, and because it is measured-fastest for the default synthesizer.
</Step>

</Steps>

Steps 1 and 2 are still subject to the availability check.

## THIRD-PARTY corroboration — not our measurement

Customer-supplied third-party dashboard readings on a **~16–32 output-token** prompt. **No
sample count, no prompt text, no verdict validation came with them.** Recorded in
`THIRD_PARTY_SHORT_PROMPT_PROBE` behind a mandatory provenance label
(`THIRD_PARTY_PROBE_SOURCE`), so a screenshot of it cannot be mistaken for one of ours, and it
**never selects a region**.

| Model | Region | TTFT | tok/s |
|---|---|---:|---:|
| luna | us-west-2 | **531 ms** | **405** |
| luna | us-east-1 | 666 ms | 220 |
| luna | us-east-2 | 2703 ms | 73 |
| terra | us-west-2 | 796 ms | — |
| terra | us-east-1 | 914 ms | — |
| terra | us-east-2 | 1105 ms | — |
| sol | us-east-1 | 3896 ms | — |
| sol | us-east-2 | **15636 ms** | — |

A missing tok/s means the reading did not include one. It is recorded as `None`, not as zero.

### Where it agrees with ours — which is what makes it worth keeping

- **us-east-2 is the worst region for Luna in both.** Theirs: 2703 ms TTFT vs 666 ms in
  us-east-1. Ours: 1.63 s vs 0.79 s.
- **us-east-2 is catastrophic for Sol in both.** Theirs: 15636 ms TTFT. Ours: 22.07 s TTFT and
  a 30.66 s total.
- **Sol has by far the highest TTFT of the three variants in both.** Theirs: 3896 ms in
  us-east-1. Ours: 4.19 s, against Luna's 0.79 s.

Three independent agreements on a dataset gathered a different way is real corroboration, and it
is why the table is kept at all.

## Where it diverges, and why that is the actual lesson

The short-prompt reading ranks **us-west-2 first** for Luna (531 ms / 405 tok/s against
us-east-1's 666 ms / 220 tok/s). On our 31,343-character prompt, **us-east-1 wins** on total
latency (4.61 s vs 5.02 s p50) even though us-west-2 edges it on throughput (180.0 vs 176.0
tok/s).

**Both readings are correct about their own workload.**

<Tabs items={['A ~32-token reply', 'An ~800-1000 token reply']}>

<Tab value="A ~32-token reply">

Almost all the wall clock is **time to first token**. Generating 32 tokens at any plausible
throughput takes a fraction of a second, so the region with the lowest TTFT wins essentially by
definition.

The third-party data puts Luna's lowest TTFT in **us-west-2 (531 ms)**, and therefore ranks
us-west-2 first.

</Tab>

<Tab value="An ~800-1000 token reply">

**~83% of the wall clock is generation** — computed from our own numbers as
(4.61 − 0.79) / 4.61. Throughput decides the total and TTFT is a smaller additive term.

On the long prompt Luna's lowest TTFT is in **us-east-1 (0.79 s vs us-west-2's 1.19 s)** — so
the two datasets disagree about the **TTFT ordering itself**, not merely about which metric to
rank on. That is a stronger divergence than a metric-choice disagreement and it is worth
stating precisely rather than glossing.

</Tab>

</Tabs>

<Callout type="error" title="The transferable finding">
**Ranking on a short probe and then deploying your own workload picks the wrong region.**

Which is why the table in the code is labelled as a measurement of *our* prompt, why every cell
carries its own `prompt_chars`, and why a re-measurement on a materially different prompt size
**refuses to diff itself** against the recorded table.
</Callout>

## The table is re-derivable, not merely assertable

```bash
python3 -m agents.mantle                          # print the recorded table; spends nothing

AGENTCORE_MANTLE_ALLOW_MEASURE=1 \
  python3 -m agents.mantle --measure --dry-run    # call plan + spend upper bound, then stop

AGENTCORE_MANTLE_ALLOW_MEASURE=1 \
  python3 -m agents.mantle --measure              # re-run the matrix and diff it
```

Re-measuring is **double-gated** on the flag *and* the env var, because it is 24 billed
inference calls on a 31K-character prompt. Several guards are worth naming:

| Guard | Behaviour |
|---|---|
| `--dry-run` spend estimate | An explicit **UPPER BOUND** whose basis is stated: input estimated at chars/4 (there is no `CountTokens` API on the mantle endpoint), priced at the 1.25x cache-write rate, output priced at the **full** token cap that no measured call reached (recorded p50 output was 785–1020). |
| `--trials` below 3 | **Refused.** A cell below `MIN_TRIALS_FOR_A_RANKING` is dropped by `measured_region_preference`, so a smaller run would spend money and change nothing. |
| An unpriced model id | **Refused.** `rates_for` raises rather than guessing a rate. |
| `prompt_comparability` | A re-measurement more than **±25%** off 31,343 chars is labelled **NOT comparable** and reported as a new experiment rather than a diff — because the ranking depends on the TTFT-vs-throughput balance, which is a function of prompt and reply size. |
| `DRIFT_TOLERANCE` | 25%, deliberately generous: n=3 medians are noisy and the recorded min/max spreads span 1.3x **within** a single cell. |
| `STALE_AFTER_DAYS` | 90. Past that the renderer prints a staleness warning with the table's age in days. |
| Regions enumerated from `MANTLE_REGIONS` | Not from the recorded matrix — so a newly available region shows up as a `new` row instead of being invisible. |

The diff reports each cell as `unchanged` / `faster` / `slower` / `new` / `failed` / `missing`,
and **separately** reports whether the *ranking* moved — an individual cell drifting 10% matters
much less than us-east-1 losing first place. When the ranking changes, the printed advisory says
to update `MEASURED_SYNTHESIS_LATENCY` **with the new n and date**, not to paper over it with
`BEDROCK_MANTLE_REGION`.

<Callout type="info" title="The probe prompt is rebuilt, not stubbed">
`build_probe_prompt` runs the real eight-way fan-out and assembles today's synthesis prompt,
because the narratives **are** the prompt: substituting stubs produces a few-hundred-character
prompt whose ranking says nothing about production. Under `MOCK_MODE=1` the fan-out returns
canned analyses, the prompt is much shorter, and `prompt_comparability` catches it — the diff
labels itself not comparable rather than silently comparing two different experiments.
</Callout>

## Specialist regions are a separate question, and were not measured this way

Everything above is about the `bedrock-mantle` endpoint. The eight Claude specialists go through
Bedrock Converse with a `global.*` inference profile, where region selection is a different
problem:

- `agents/models.py:resolve_region` reads `AWS_REGION` (then `AWS_DEFAULT_REGION`, then
  us-east-1) and governs the Converse client only.
- The `global.*` prefix means those calls are **not** pinned to one region's capacity in the way
  a mantle call is — and it costs exactly **1/1.10** of the `us.*` prefix for identical
  behaviour. [Cost levers](/docs/cost/levers#lever-1-global-instead-of-us--exactly-909).
- **No model x region matrix was measured for the Claude specialists.** The 42-run benchmark ran
  entirely in us-east-1, and nothing on this site says what us-west-2 would have measured.

One older, narrower measurement exists and is worth keeping only for the shape of its result: on
a real fraud prompt with `maxTokens=120` in us-east-1, **Haiku 4.5 1848 ms, Sonnet 4.6 4481 ms,
Opus 4.8 2990 ms, Sonnet 5 @effort=low 2393 ms, Opus 5 @effort=low 2924 ms**. Sonnet 4.6 is
*slower* than Opus 4.8 — **latency does not track price.** That is a 120-token probe, so by this
page's own argument it is a TTFT-dominated measurement and must not be used to predict a
1,500-token specialist analysis.
