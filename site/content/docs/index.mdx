parallel plus a master synthesizer** — from Snowflake Intelligence (Cortex Agents) onto
**Amazon Bedrock AgentCore**.

## The one rule this site follows

Every number on this site traces to an artifact committed in the repository, and the page
that quotes it names the file. Where a figure was not measured, the page says
**NOT MEASURED** rather than estimating. Where two artifacts disagree, both are shown and
the authoritative one is named.

<Callout type="warn" title="Why that rule is stated up front">
An earlier draft of a summary for this engagement claimed *"130 analyses over 8 domains,
judged by GPT-5.6 Sol, band 23/26 per model, 12.3 s end-to-end at $0.0508."* None of those
figures appear in any artifact on disk. The real cross-family study is
[120 analyses over 4 domains with two judges](/docs/model-selection/cross-family-study);
the real measured end-to-end is
[31.45 s p50 at $0.1440 p50 over 42 runs](/docs/measured-results). Detecting that class of
error is why percentiles here always carry their `n`.
</Callout>

## The one question that blocks the cost case

The customer reports a projected **$700K–$1M** annual Snowflake AI spend. Our measured cost
is **$0.1440 per adjudication** (p50, n=41). Those two numbers cannot be compared without a
figure the customer has not yet supplied: **how many applications per day go through the
full eight-agent forensic underwriting path.**

At ~3,000/day the measured p50 annualises to roughly **$158K**. At 300,000/day it annualises
to roughly **$15.8M**. Same architecture, opposite conclusion. This is the single most
important open item and it has its own page:
[The Snowflake comparison and its blocker](/docs/cost/snowflake-comparison).
