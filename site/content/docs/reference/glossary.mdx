## Eight fraud dimensions

Each specialist carries a persona name from the customer's own documents
(`prompts.loader.AGENT_NAMES`), lifted from each prompt file's own header line.

| Domain | Persona | Detects | Its anti-false-positive rule |
|---|---|---|---|
| `identity` | `ISAAC_IDENTITY` | SSN sharing, PII mismatch, identity volatility | Multiple alerts from one underlying data point count as **one** signal |
| `dealer` | `DIANA_DEALER` | Dealer-**orchestrated** fraud | Default / EPD / consortium scores are portfolio risk, not fraud |
| `straw` | `SAMMY_STRAW` | Proxy or nominee co-borrowers, role-switching | No co-borrower means the primary mechanism is absent |
| `employment` | `EMILY_EMPLOYMENT` | Fabricated or shell employers | Missing employer fields are a data gap, not fraud |
| `income` | `IRIS_INCOME` | Overstatement, inconsistent reporting | Under 15% variance is normal; self-employment explains variance |
| `synthetic` | `SYLVIA_SYNTHETIC` | Fabricated identity plus manufactured credit | Thin files are legitimate for young adults and new immigrants |
| `bustout` | `BRUCE_BUSTOUT` | Rapid velocity, unsustainable exposure | Geography alone must not move the classification |
| `rings` | `RENEE_RINGS` | Shared identifiers, exact-unit reuse | Multi-unit housing and large employers explain overlap |

**`MASTER_FRAUD_AGENT`** — the batch synthesizer. This id is **this port's**, derived from the prompt's
header by joining its words with underscores. It is *not* a literal customer string, and the loader
records that distinction so nobody quotes it back to her as hers.

**Francis** — the *interactive* persona, in the customer's exact casing from
`master_agent_orchestration_062026`. Deliberately **not** upper-cased: the only all-caps `FRANCIS` the
customer ever writes is inside `RETRO_FRANCIS`, a separate retro system Francis must deflect to, so
`FRANCIS` here would collide with the one name she must never answer as.

**`RETRO_FRANCIS`** — the customer's separate retro-data system. Francis deflects retro questions to it
rather than answering them.

## Bands and verdicts

**AMBIGUOUS: the mid band has three names, and each is correct in its own place.**

| Layer | Vocabulary for the mid band | Correct? |
|---|---|---|
| **Specialist** output | `POSSIBLE RISK`, between `LOW RISK` and `HIGH RISK` | Yes |
| **Master adjudication** — `master_agent_prompt.txt`, the file that runs | `MEDIUM RISK`, between `LOW RISK` and `HIGH RISK` | Yes |
| **The process-guide PDF** | `POSSIBLE RISK`, with **four** recommendation values | **Conflict** |

First two rows are *both* right: a specialist bands with `POSSIBLE RISK`, the master adjudicates with
`MEDIUM RISK`. Third is the [unresolved enum conflict](/docs/open-questions#the-enum-conflict). The
prompt file wins here because it is the artifact that executes, and the conflict is surfaced to the
customer rather than reconciled quietly.

**band** — the risk classification a specialist declares, or the master's `overall_risk_decision`. On
this site, "band agreement" always means agreement with a [pinned expectation](#pinned-expectation),
never agreement between two models.

**`recommendation_decision`** — one of `APPROVE` / `REVIEW AND APPLY STIPULATIONS` / `DECLINE` in the
prompt file's three-value enum. The PDF's fourth value, **`MANUAL REVIEW`**, has no target in that enum
and is the sharpest edge of the conflict.

**flag** — a per-domain `int 0|1` in the adjudication, for example `identity_flag`. **Not** a
restatement of the band. `MSTR-039` sets a flag to `1` only on clear, material, corroborated evidence,
so **a domain can legitimately be `POSSIBLE RISK` with its flag at `0`** — and one fixture (APP-1014) is
MEDIUM overall with all eight flags at `0`. A flag-counting implementation returns LOW on it.

**adjudication** — the master synthesizer's output: **exactly 21 keys in the customer's order**,
starting at `master_summary`. There is **no `application_id` key** — it travels in the transport
envelope. See [Adjudication contract](/docs/fidelity/adjudication-contract).

**fraud score** — a AnyCompany model output on the application, banded by the customer as
`0–299` low, `300–699` medium, `700–998` high, `999` very high, where 999 means at least one fraud
accelerator fired. **A score is not evidence**: four fixtures deliberately place it in a band that
argues against the pinned outcome. It must also not be averaged, being standard-scaled, and must not be
used to pre-filter investigation.

**IncomePass** — the `income_misrep_score` field, indicating likely material income
misrepresentation. **Distinct from the fraud score** (General Rule 20).

## Measurement vocabulary

**pinned expectation** [#pinned-expectation] — the `expected` block on a fixture: the band, flag,
requirement id and quoted customer rule recording what *should* come back. The requirement id, for
example `EMP-017`, is the addressable unit of disagreement.

**AMBIGUOUS: a pin disagreement is not automatically a model error.** When a model disagrees with a
pin, either could be wrong. APP-1003 was perfectly *stable* across three runs and stably disagreed with
its pin, and the benchmark's own caveat says whether the pin or the model is wrong is the customer's
call. This site reports those disagreements and does not adjudicate them.

**grounded_fraction** — the deciding metric of the cross-family study, and the one no judge touches.
Every specialist prompt requires signals to be validated against the paired `<signal>_context` columns,
and `grounded_fraction` is the share of the signals in an agent's view whose paired `_context` row is
demonstrably used in the prose: **distinctive tokens are lifted out of the context rows and looked for
in the text**, so "cites the context field" is evidence rather than the word *context* appearing
somewhere.

Measured per-analysis means (`evals/results/set_comparison.json`, 120 analyses): **Claude 0.642 vs GPT
0.357**, over 48 and 72 analyses respectively, with GPT lower on **4 of 4** agents on both a mean and a
median basis. Deterministic, invisible in the band, invisible in latency, invisible in cost. See
[Cross-family study](/docs/model-selection/cross-family-study).

**calibration** — how well a band follows the customer's own calibration text, scored 1–5 by a blind
judge. **Subjective by construction**, and reported as such. Distinct from *band agreement*, which is
deterministic. The judge's measured self-preference (+0.31 calibration) is the noise floor below which
a calibration margin means nothing.

**substance** — a judge score for *distinct grounded evidence*, explicitly **not length**. The
customer's own master prompt supplies the reason (`MSTR-018`): the amount of analysis written does not
necessarily indicate fraud severity.

**padding** — output length not carrying additional distinct evidence. Measured by pairing on identical
payloads, pooled across the four swept agents: Opus writes **2.95×** Luna's characters while naming only
**1.08×** the payload's signals, and the two agree on band in **23 of 24** pairs. So the length gap is
padding rather than lost evidence.

**anti-false-positive fixture** — a record built to *look* bad and resolve LOW anyway, isolating one
rule the customer states as an explicit exception. **Five are designated** (`ANTI_FP_APPS`: APP-1006 …
APP-1010); **four more are embedded** in records that are not themselves LOW overall, where a single
domain must clear independently. See [Fixtures](/docs/reference/fixtures).

**false positive / false negative** — in the judges' vocabulary: escalating something the customer's
rules call benign, or clearing something the rules say should have been escalated. The customer's
stated quality goal is reducing false positives, which is why the study weights anti-FP escalation
above every other axis.

**critical path** — the specialist that was the **slowest in a given run**. Because `asyncio.gather`
cannot return before its slowest member, the distribution of slowest-specialist wall clock *is* the
fan-out floor. `rings` owned it in **25 of 42** runs.

**AMBIGUOUS: the agent that owns the median is not the agent that produces the tail.** `rings` sets the
p50 at 20.96 s, the highest of the eight. The two runs that crossed 60 s were owned by `bustout`
(69.78 s) and `income` (66.01 s), so **shaving `rings` alone would not have fixed either**. See
[Critical path](/docs/performance/critical-path).

**stall** — in `evals/tail_analysis.py`, a **single specialist** call crossing 60 s. Not an outlier
rule: it is the customer's sub-minute target applied to one agent, because one specialist consuming the
whole budget is a different physical event from a verbose one. The two stalls in the sample generated at
**16.1 and 4.7 output tok/s** against a **65.50 tok/s p50** over the 332 calls under the threshold
(`evals/results/tail_analysis.json`).

**sequential counterfactual** (`sum_of_eight_ms`) — the sum of the eight specialists' own measured wall
clocks in a run. **The terms are measured; the arrangement is hypothetical.** It is what eight calls
would cost one after another *only if* each took the same time alone.

**parallel speed-up** — fan-out wall clock divided by that counterfactual, **one ratio per run** (p50
3.64×, n=42). Reported as a distribution because the fan-out is bounded by its slowest dimension, so
the ratio moves with which specialist ran long.

**refused percentile** — `null` in the JSON *with the reason beside it*, not an omission and never a
substituted number. p50 needs 5 samples, p95 needs 20, p99 needs 100. `n/a` in a table means refused,
never zero.

**degraded run** — an adjudication that lost one or more specialists and still validated its 21 keys. It
**stays in the latency distribution**, because it really took that long, and **leaves the cost
distribution**, because eight priced calls presented as a nine-call total is an under-count wearing a
total's name. That is why end-to-end is n=42 and cost is n=41.

## Platform vocabulary

**mantle** / **bedrock-mantle** — the Amazon Bedrock endpoint serving OpenAI models through the OpenAI
Responses API, at `bedrock-mantle.{region}.api.aws`. **Not** Converse: GPT-5.x is not on Converse at
all. Needs the separate IAM action `bedrock-mantle:CreateInference`, a **bearer token** rather than
SigV4 request signing, and the base URL path `/openai/v1`, since `/v1` on the same host serves only the
open-weight `gpt-oss` family. See [GPT-5.6 on bedrock-mantle](/docs/platform/bedrock-mantle).

**tier** — this port's `light` / `mid` / `heavy` grouping, which sets `max_tokens` (3072 / 4096 / 4096)
and the default model per group.

<Callout type="error" title="AMBIGUOUS: 'tier' does not mean 'saves money'">
Haiku→Sonnet is a **$2/$10-per-MTok step down** and Sonnet→Opus is the **identical step up**, so at
equal token counts one LIGHT agent funds exactly one HEAVY agent. Tiering saves **$0.00 structurally**,
and a test asserts the arithmetic so the claim cannot return. The measured 20.13% saving was
**entirely** Sonnet 5's introductory rate, which reverts on **2026-09-01**. See
[Cost levers](/docs/cost/levers).

There is also **no HEAVY agent left** — `synthetic` and `rings` moved off Opus 5 on 2026-07-28 — so the
current shape is two cheap agents and six standard, and what remains is simply not paying Opus rates
for output that measured no better.
</Callout>

**inference profile prefix** — `global.*` or `us.*` on a Claude model id. Same weights, same API; `us.*`
bills at exactly **1.10×** on every rate category. A policy allowing only `us.anthropic.*` silently
forbids the cheaper one.

**effort** — the reasoning-effort control. On Claude via Converse:
`additional_request_fields={"output_config": {"effort": "low"}}`. On mantle:
`reasoning={"effort": …}`, where `temperature` / `top_p` / `top_k` are **ignored**.

**cache-write tokens** — input tokens written to a prompt cache. Billed at **1.25×** the input rate on
GPT-5.6 and on Claude. Omitting the term under-reported a measured Luna call by **62%**, because with
implicit caching on and a large prompt most of the input lands in that class.

**minimum cacheable prefix** — the prompt size below which a cache point silently never engages. **Not
monotonic across generations**: Opus 5 = 512, Sonnet 5 / Sonnet 4.6 / Opus 4.8 = 1024, **Haiku 4.5 =
4096**. No error, no warning, permanent 0% hit rate. Against 547–956-token specialist prompts, prompt
caching engages on **0 of 8** agents. See [Cost: what does not work](/docs/cost/what-does-not-work).

**signal** vs **`_context`** — a signal is a precomputed numeric or boolean value
(`address_reuse_distinct_ssn_count = 96`). Its `_context` row is the paired explanatory field, for
example a 240-unit apartment complex. **The benign explanation lives only in the context row**, which
is why `grounded_fraction` is the metric that separates a real reading from a fluent one.

**`DomainView`** — exactly what one specialist is entitled to: its own signals, their context, its own
alerts. There is no data tool on the agent side, so this object is the **complete universe** the
specialist reasons over.

**`SignalPayload`** — the whole-application object: core fields, eight `DomainView`s,
`guardrails_applied` (General Rule **ids**, not prose), `semantic_layer_rev`, `computed_at`.

**guardrail** — **ambiguous by platform**. In this repo it means a numbered **General Rule** from the
customer's orchestration documents, enforced in `signal_layer/`. On AWS it also names a **Bedrock
Guardrail**, a platform feature this repo does **not** configure — no rule here relies on one as its
only enforcement layer.

**General Rule** — one of **29** numbered rules repeated across all eight orchestration documents. **27
are enforced by deterministic code; 2 are prompt-enforced** interpretive guidance. See
[General Rules coverage](/docs/fidelity/general-rules).

**requirement id** — the addressable label this port assigns to a single obligation in a customer
prompt: `IDEN-014`, `EMP-017`, `MSTR-039`, `STRW-008`, `XAG-003`. The `MSTR-*` prefix is the master
prompt; `XAG-*` is a rule appearing across agents. Every fixture pin and every golden assertion cites
one, so a disagreement lands on a specific sentence.

**alert category** — one of the **ten** groupings in General Rule 3. **166 entries, 148 distinct ids, 18
ids in more than one category** — the overlap is the customer's, not a data error. Alerts 31–39 are
explicitly disregardable.

**`SIGNAL_MODE`** — the proposed flag on the Lambda Gateway target (`cortex` | `aurora`) that makes
live-Cortex and precomputed-Aurora one config change. See
[Semantic layer question](/docs/platform/semantic-layer).

## Two words to use carefully in front of this customer

<Callout type="warn" title="'measured' and '1:1' both have a narrow meaning here">
**measured** means: a real call happened, the number came from that call's own reported telemetry, and
the artifact naming the run is cited. Anything else is labelled **PROJECTION**, **NOT MEASURED**, or
**the customer's own figure, by attribution**. Their reported figures — 4,500 applications taking over
48 hours, $700K–$1M projected annual Snowflake AI spend — are **theirs**, and must never appear beside
one of ours as though both were measured the same way, because this repo has never run their Snowflake
pipeline and cannot.

**1:1** means the **prompts and the output contracts are the customer's, byte for byte** — not
"equivalent in intent". The phrase *verbatim in intent* appeared in the pre-port code's own docstring
while that code had rewritten all nine prompts, so it is a phrase to avoid entirely.
</Callout>

## Still unknown

**Three terms here are ours rather than hers, and are labelled as such where they appear**:
`MASTER_FRAUD_AGENT`, every `requirement id`, and the `light`/`mid`/`heavy` tier names. If the customer
uses different labels internally, these are the ones to reconcile — none of them changes behaviour, and
all three appear in artifacts she may read.
