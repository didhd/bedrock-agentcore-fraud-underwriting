<Callout type="error" title="The single most important open item in this engagement">
The comparison between our measured cost and the customer's reported spend is **unresolvable
today**. Not because our side is unmeasured — it is measured, over 42 live adjudications — but
because one input is missing: **how many applications per day go through the full eight-agent
forensic underwriting path.**

At ~3,000/day the measured p50 annualises to roughly **$158K**. At 300,000/day, roughly
**$15.8M**. Same architecture, opposite conclusion.
</Callout>

<Callout type="info" title="Audience, and a rule for this page">
For the executive and account audience primarily. **Two kinds of number appear on this page and
they are never mixed in one table.**

- **OUR MEASUREMENTS** — from `evals/results/pipeline_14x3_us-east-1.json`, with n stated.
- **THEIR REPORTED FIGURES** — the $700K–$1M and the "4,500 applications in over 48 hours". These
  are **the customer's own statements about their own platform.** We did not measure Snowflake,
  we have no access to their account, and nothing on this site is a benchmark of their system.

Where the two appear near each other, every row is labelled. A blended table would be the exact
failure mode this site exists to avoid.
</Callout>

**OURS x AN ASSUMED VOLUME.** The per-adjudication figure is measured; the volumes are

| **3,000** | $431.88 | $12,956 | **~$158K** |
| **15,000** | $2,159.38 | $64,781 | **~$788K** |

**~$158K/year** against a reported **$700K–$1M**.

The conclusion is a **4.4x to 6.3x reduction in AI spend**, and the migration case is

<Tab value="At 15,000/day">

**~$788K/year** against a reported **$700K–$1M**.

The conclusion is **roughly break-even** — inside the reported band. Cost is no longer the
argument, and the case rests entirely on latency (31.45 s p50 against their reported 48+ hours for
4,500 applications) and on the removal of eight Cortex Analyst calls and eight warehouse
executions per application.

This is the volume where the answer is genuinely ambiguous, and it is not an implausible one.

</Tab>

<Tab value="At 300,000/day">

**~$15.8M/year** against a reported **$700K–$1M**.

The conclusion **reverses**: the port is an order of magnitude *more* expensive on model spend,
and the architecture would need to change before it could be proposed — batch inference for
everything that is not forward-flow, a cheaper tier for the agents that measured no worse, and
almost certainly a triage stage so that not every application receives all eight specialists.

There is also a hard capacity question at this volume, below.

</Tab>

### Stated the other way: the break-even volume

Since the reported figure is a range, the more useful form of the question is what volume makes
our measured cost equal to theirs.

| Their reported annual AI spend | Break-even at our measured p50 ($0.14396) | At the current assignment's projection ($0.12475) | At the post-2026-09-01 projection ($0.17734) |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| **$700K** | **~13,300 apps/day** | ~15,400 | ~10,800 |
| **$1M** | **~19,000 apps/day** | ~22,000 | ~15,400 |

**Below roughly 13,000 applications/day, we are cheaper. Above roughly 19,000, we are not.**
Everything between is inside the reported range and undecidable without a tighter figure from
them.

<Callout type="error" title="Note how much the September rate change moves the break-even">
When Sonnet 5's introductory rate lapses, the break-even against $700K falls from ~13,300 to
~10,800 applications/day — a **19% narrower** window on the same architecture, from a calendar
date. If the volume answer lands anywhere near the break-even, the promo expiry is not a footnote.
[Cost levers](/docs/cost/levers).
</Callout>

## The exact question to ask

<Callout type="info" title="Do not ask 'how many applications do you process'">
The consortium volume is much larger than the forensic-underwriting volume, and mixing them is
how this question gets answered wrongly — in the direction that makes us look expensive.

**Ask:** *of the applications you ingest, how many per day receive the full eight-agent forensic
underwriting treatment, as opposed to a score-only or single-product path?*
</Callout>

Three secondary figures worth asking for in the same breath, because each changes the arithmetic
rather than just the total:

<Steps>

<Step>

**Is the eight-agent path applied to every application, or only above a score threshold?** If
there is already a triage stage, the relevant volume may be a small fraction of ingestion — and
the whole comparison resolves in our favour immediately.

</Step>

<Step>

**What fraction is re-run?** Repeat submissions and RETRO backfills are the workload where
[batch inference](/docs/cost/levers#lever-2-batch-inference--50-with-a-real-exclusion-list) at
−50% applies, and forward-flow is the workload where it does not. Their own reported "4,500
applications in over 48 hours" sounds exactly like a batch workload.

</Step>

<Step>

**Does the $700K–$1M include warehouse compute, or only the AI/Cortex line items?** This decides
whether our model-spend figure is being compared with a like quantity.

</Step>

</Steps>

## What is on our side of the ledger that is not a dollar figure

Two things the port removes per application. **Neither is priced here**, because pricing them
would need the customer's Snowflake credit rate, their warehouse size, and their Cortex Analyst
message pricing — none of which we have. What is claimable is the **count**:

| Per application, on the incumbent path | Per application, here |
|---|---|
| **8 Cortex Analyst messages** | **0** |
| **8 warehouse executions** | **0** |
| 9 LLM calls | 9 LLM calls |

<Callout type="info" title="Their leadership often finds the count more persuasive than the latency">
They know what a warehouse-second costs and we do not. Handing them a count they can price
themselves is stronger than handing them a dollar figure we had to assume our way into.
</Callout>

There is a latency argument attached to the same change. A published production decomposition of a
Cortex Agent call attributes **~27 s of a 55 s total** to Cortex Analyst SQL generation alone.
**That figure is not our measurement** — it is a published third-party decomposition and is
labelled as such wherever it appears. For a fixed set of fraud signal groups the queries are known
at design time, so regenerating them per application buys nothing. See `docs/semantic-layer-mcp.md`
and [the semantic layer](/docs/platform/semantic-layer).

## The capacity question that only exists at high volume

If the volume answer is at the top of the range, there is a second thing to raise **before any
load test**, not after.

At 300,000 applications/day x 9 calls x ~2,250 tokens the workload approaches **6.1B tokens/day**.
Claude Sonnet 5's daily token ceiling is **8.64B** and is **not adjustable** — it is an
account-team escalation, not a self-serve quota ticket.

That is 71% of a non-adjustable ceiling on the model six of the eight specialists run on. It does
not block anything at 3,000/day. It is a design constraint at 300,000/day, and it depends entirely
on the answer to the same question.

## What must not be said

Recorded explicitly, because each of these is an easy sentence to say in a room and each is
unsupported.

| Do not say | Why |
|---|---|
| *"We are 5x cheaper than Snowflake."* | True only at the low end of an unknown volume range, and it quotes their reported figure as if we had measured it. |
| *"$0.14 per application versus their $700K."* | A per-unit figure against an annual total, with no volume. It is not a comparison. |
| *"We benchmarked Snowflake at 48 hours."* | We did not benchmark Snowflake at all. That is **their reported figure about their own platform**, and the UI deliberately contains no "AgentCore vs Snowflake" comparison for exactly this reason — the string "Snowflake" does not appear in it. |
| *"Per-agent model tiering delivers the cost saving."* | The tier shape saves **$0.00** structurally, and a test asserts the arithmetic. [Detail](/docs/cost/what-does-not-work#per-agent-tiering-saves-000-structurally). |
| *"Prompt caching cuts the input cost."* | It does not engage on this workload at all. [Detail](/docs/cost/what-does-not-work#prompt-caching-does-not-engage). |
| *"Batch inference halves the bill."* | Worth **3.58%** on this assignment, because Bedrock offers no batch price for Sonnet 5 or GPT-5.6. [Detail](/docs/cost/levers#lever-2-batch-inference--50-with-a-real-exclusion-list). |
| Any annualised figure without its assumed volume stated in the same sentence | The volume is the entire uncertainty. |

## The one-sentence version

**Our measured cost is $0.14396 per adjudication over 41 priced runs; their reported spend is
$700K–$1M a year; the two meet somewhere between 13,000 and 19,000 applications per day, and we do
not know which side of that line they are on.**

That sentence is the honest state of the cost case, and it is one customer answer away from being
a conclusion.
